## Pattern
Following an adjective (or a "feeling" verb like 喜欢/累) with 得很, [[极]]了, or [[死]]了 pushes the degree past what plain [[很]] ([[grammar:hen-degree]]) or [[grammar:chengdu-fu-level2]]'s 特别 convey: 累得很 "really tired," 冷极了 "freezing," 忙死了 "swamped."

## Usage
These three go at the end of the description, not before it like 很/非常 — 得很/极了/死了 always follow the adjective, never precede it. 死了 is the most colloquial and exaggerated of the three; 极了 is a strong, everyday superlative; 得很 leans slightly more formal/written.

## Notes
死了 here is hyperbole, not literal — 累死了 means "exhausted," not any real danger.
