## Pattern
In colloquial passives, 给 can appear immediately before the verb after 被/叫/让 and the agent: *Patient + 被 + Agent + 给 + V*. 给 reinforces the passive event; it does not mean “give” here.

## Usage
The construction often conveys that the result was unwanted, surprising, or emphatic. Omit 给 for a neutral or formal passive. If the agent is omitted, 被给V is possible in speech but much less suitable in careful prose.

## Notes
The affected patient still comes first, as in [[grammar:bei-passive]]. Do not mistake the two markers for two agents: 被 introduces the passive relation, while 给 marks the event colloquially.
