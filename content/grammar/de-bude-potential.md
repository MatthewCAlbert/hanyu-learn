## Pattern
Some verbs take bare 得 or 不得 without a following result complement: V得 means that doing V is possible or acceptable, while V不得 means that it is impossible, forbidden, or too risky.

## Usage
The negative form is especially common in warnings: 吃不得, 急不得, 开不得玩笑. It is stronger and more idiomatic than merely saying 不可以 V. Whether it means physical impossibility or social prohibition comes from context.

## Notes
Compare [[grammar:keneng-complement]], where 得/不 is followed by a result or direction such as 看得懂 and 进不去. Here 得 or 不得 itself completes the pattern.
