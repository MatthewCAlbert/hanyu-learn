## Pattern
The thing acted upon can open the sentence as its topic, with the doer (if named at all) coming after: *O + (S) + V + Complement*. 作业我做完了 puts 作业 "the homework" first even though it's what gets done, not who does it — this is not [[grammar:ba-sentence]] or [[grammar:bei-passive]], just a topic shift with plain word order otherwise intact.

## Usage
This works best when the object is already known from context — "as for the homework, I've finished it" — and is very natural with 都 for totality: 饭都吃完了 "the food's all gone/eaten."

## Notes
No marker (把, 被, 让) appears here at all — the object simply sits in subject position and the listener understands from meaning, not grammar, that it's the one being acted on.
