## Pattern
[[只有]] names the one condition without which B cannot happen, and [[才]] marks B as depending on it: *只有 A，才 B* — "only if A, then B." This is stronger than [[grammar:ruguo-jiu]]'s 如果...就, which merely offers A as a sufficient trigger, not the sole one.

## Usage
才, unlike 就, signals that the result was hard-won or conditional — it does not follow automatically. Reversing the halves without 只有/才 loses this "only" force entirely: 你多听多说，就能学好中文 reads as a plainer, weaker claim.

## Notes
才 also marks "not until" in plain time sentences (他才起床 "he's only just gotten up now"); here its job is narrower — "and not otherwise."
