## Pattern
[[了]] right after the verb marks a completed action: 我吃了早饭. 了 at the end of a clause marks a new state — 天冷了 means "it's [turned] cold now," not "it has always been cold." The two positions can combine in one sentence.

## Usage
Negate a completed action with 没(有) and drop 了 entirely: 我没吃早饭, never *我没吃了早饭. 了 is not a general past-tense marker — habitual and future statements take no 了 at all.

## Notes
This is one character with two attachment points, not two different words; grammars that name them separately ("perfective 了" vs "sentence-final 了") are describing the same 了.
