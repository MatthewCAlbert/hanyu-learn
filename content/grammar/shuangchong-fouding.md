## Pattern
Two negatives inside one sentence cancel out into a strong positive: 没有人不喜欢她 "there is no one [such that they] don't like her" = "everyone likes her," 不可能不参加 "it's not possible [that I] don't join" = "I will definitely join." The second [[不]] usually attaches to the verb itself.

## Usage
This reads as more emphatic than the plain positive it's equivalent to — 大家都喜欢她 is a flat statement, while 没有人不喜欢她 frames it as beyond doubt. Common openers are 没有人/没有什么 + 不... and 不可能/不会 + 不....

## Notes
Both negatives must be genuinely doing separate jobs (one negating existence or possibility, one negating the verb) — this is not two negatives sitting side by side for emphasis the way some languages stack them.
