## Pattern
[[于是]] links two clauses in the order events actually happened, with B following naturally from A: "A happened, and so then B." It reads more like a story's "and then" than [[grammar:yinwei-suoyi]]'s 所以, which explains a reason more than narrates a sequence.

## Usage
于是 needs A to already be a real, completed situation — not a hypothesis or a general reason — so it fits narratives and past events much better than abstract cause-and-effect statements.

## Notes
所以 answers "why did B happen?"; 于是 answers "what happened next, given A?" — the two overlap in many sentences but are not always interchangeable.
