## Why this combination

无 ("without, there is no," as in 无论") plus 所谓 (a fixed literary
phrase meaning "what is called, so-called," from 所 marking a
nominalized clause and 谓 "to call, to say") produces a set phrase whose
literal sense — "there is no 'so-called [X]' here" — has bleached into a
general marker of indifference: "it doesn't matter (to me)."

Rated "opaque": the modern "whatever, I don't mind" sense is not
recoverable from the parts without knowing the fixed 所谓 idiom first.

## Notes

无所谓 doubles as an adjective-like predicate ("他对这事无所谓," "he's
indifferent about this") and a free-standing reply ("无所谓," "whatever,
doesn't matter to me").
