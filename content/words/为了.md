## Why this combination
为 alone already carries "for, for the sake of, in order to." 了 adds no
new lexical content — it functions here as a fixed grammatical suffix
that helps mark 为 as a complete preposition introducing a purpose clause,
rather than contributing its own "to finish" or aspectual meaning.

## Notes
为了 always precedes the goal, never the beneficiary alone — 为了健康
"for [the sake of] health," 为了你 "for you" — distinguishing it from
plain 为, which can do double duty for both "for the purpose of" and "on
behalf of."
