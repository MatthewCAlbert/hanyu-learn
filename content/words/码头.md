## Why this combination
码 here names a stone or wooden mooring-marker driven into the riverbank
or seabed for tying up boats — a specialized sense distinct from 码's
everyday "number, numeral" use — and 头 "end, point" marks it as the
terminal point of a route. Together: the fixed point where boats moor.

## Notes
码头 anchors 港口码头 "harbour dock," and figuratively 占码头 "to claim
territory/turf [gang slang, historically tied to controlling a dock's
trade]" — a vivid extension of physical control over a mooring point to
control over a business or territory.
