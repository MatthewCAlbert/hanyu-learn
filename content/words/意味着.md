## Why this combination
意味 "flavour of meaning, implication" (itself 意 "idea" + 味 "flavour" —
meaning treated as something with a taste to it) takes the continuous
suffix 着, giving "to be carrying the implication that..." The core
metaphor — meaning as a flavour one thing carries — is what makes this
semi rather than fully transparent.

## Notes
意味着 always introduces a consequence or implication clause: 这意味着
失败 "this means failure." 着 here is grammatically fixed — 意味着 is
rarely used without it.
