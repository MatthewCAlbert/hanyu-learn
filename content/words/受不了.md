## Why this combination
受 ("to receive, to bear," covered in this app's entries for 承受 and
授) takes the potential-complement pattern 得了/不了 ("able to/unable
to [complete an action]," the negative here) to mark that bearing
something has become impossible: 受不了 is literally "cannot bear it."

Marked "idiom" because the 得了/不了 potential-complement grammar is a
fixed productive pattern that has to be learned as a unit, not derived
from 受's meaning alone.

## Notes
受不了 always follows the thing that has become unbearable (我受不了这个
噪音, "I can't stand this noise") — its positive counterpart 受得了
("able to bear it") is far less common in everyday speech, since people
usually comment on what they *can't* stand.
