## Why this combination
递 "to hand, to pass" plus the resultative/directional complement 给 "to
give (to)" gives exactly "to hand something over to someone" —
splittable, as in 递了给他 in some constructions, though 递给 usually
stays together before the recipient.

## Notes
递给 always takes a recipient: 把书递给我 "hand the book to me" — a
concrete, physical handing-over action, distinct from abstract 给予 "to
give/grant."
