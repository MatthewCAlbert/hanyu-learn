## Why this combination
海, here evoking the sea as the historical route of foreign trade
(as in 上海, and coastal treaty-port customs houses) combined with 关
"a pass, a checkpoint, a barrier" (as in 海关's own 关, seen also in 关系
in an unrelated sense): the checkpoint where sea-borne trade is
inspected — customs, extended today to any border crossing.

## Notes
海关 names the customs authority and process at any border crossing,
not only ports (过海关 "to go through customs," 海关申报 "customs
declaration") — the "sea" in its name is a historical relic of when
foreign trade arrived almost entirely by ship.
