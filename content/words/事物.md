## Why this combination
A near-synonym compound: 事 ("a matter, an affair") and 物 ("a thing, an
object") both independently name something existing to be dealt with.
Pairing them gives the broad, abstract word for "things" in general —
concepts, phenomena, and objects alike.

## Notes
事物 is more abstract/philosophical than plain 东西 ("thing[s]," the
everyday physical-object word) — 新事物 ("a new phenomenon") describes a
concept or development, not a physical item you could pick up.
