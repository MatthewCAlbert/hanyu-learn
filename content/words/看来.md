## Why this combination
看 ("to look") plus the directional/resultative complement 来 ("toward the
speaker") is the same verb + result pattern seen in 说来 ("speaking of it"),
出来 ("to come out"), and 起来 ("to arise"): 来 marks that the action's
result comes back around to the speaker's own perspective. "看来" is
literally "looking at it, [what comes out is]..." — used as a whole-sentence
hedge for an inference the speaker is drawing, not for physically watching
something arrive.

Marked "idiom" rather than "semantic-compound" because a learner who knows
看 and 来 separately still cannot predict the "apparently, it seems" sense
without meeting the verb + directional-complement pattern first — it is a
grammaticalized fixed expression, even though each piece is common.

## Notes
看来 always sits at the front of a clause and is followed by the speaker's
inference (看来他不来了 "it seems he's not coming") — it never takes a direct
object the way 看 alone does.
