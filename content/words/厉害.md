## Why this combination
厉 "severe, strict" and 害 "harm" combine literally into "severely
harmful" — still visible in 厉害的病 "a severe illness." The everyday
colloquial leap to a positive "amazing, impressive" (真厉害! "wow,
amazing!") is not derivable from the literal parts; it has to be learned
as a separate, intensity-flavoured use.

## Notes
厉害 can praise or criticize depending entirely on context and tone —
你真厉害 is a compliment on skill, while 病得很厉害 describes a severe,
unwelcome illness. The word marks strong intensity; whether that
intensity is good or bad is left to context.
