## Why this combination
看 "to look" combined with the resultative-directional complement 上去
"upward and away" (see also 上去): to look [at something] and form an
impression from it — it appears, it seems.

## Notes
看上去 is followed directly by an adjective or clause describing the
apparent impression (看上去很累 "[he] looks very tired") — it names a
visual, at-a-glance impression, closer to English "it looks like" than
to 好像, which can hedge based on any kind of evidence, not only sight.
