## Why this combination
房 "house" (as in 房子, 房租) combined with 东, here "host, owner"
(from 东道主 "host," literally "the master of the eastern road" — an
ancient reference to the position a host traditionally occupied when
receiving guests): the house's host/owner — a landlord.

## Notes
房东 names the owner who rents out housing to a tenant (我的房东 "my
landlord") — 东 alone survives in this "host, owner" sense mainly
within fixed compounds like 房东 and 股东 "shareholder," rather than
independently.
