## Why this combination
全 and 都 are near-synonyms, both meaning "all, entirely" — doubled for
emphasis into a stronger, more emphatic "all, every single one."

## Notes
全都 typically precedes the verb, reinforcing totality: 全都同意 "everyone
agreed, no exceptions" — a stronger emphasis than 都 alone.
