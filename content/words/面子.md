## Why this combination
面 "face, surface" plus the noun suffix 子 gives, literally, "the surface
of a thing." The everyday sense — social standing, "face" in the English
idiom — is a metaphorical extension: how one appears to others, not a
physical surface.

## Notes
面子 anchors 给面子 "to do someone the favor of respecting their
standing," 丢面子 "to lose face," 爱面子 "to be concerned with one's
reputation" — always about social image, distinct from 脸, which centers
more on personal dignity/shame than public standing.
