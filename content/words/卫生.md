## Why this combination
卫 "to guard, to protect, to defend" (as in 保卫 "to defend") combined
with 生 "life" (see also 生命, 生存): to guard life — a classical phrase
(衛生, "to protect/nurture one's life") repurposed in the modern era,
likely via Japanese 衛生 (eisei), to translate the Western concept of
public hygiene and health.

## Notes
卫生 names both public health/hygiene as a field (卫生部 "Ministry of
Health") and, as an adjective, "clean, sanitary" (这里不卫生 "it's not
hygienic here") — 健康, its neighbour, is used instead for a person's own
bodily health/wellbeing.
