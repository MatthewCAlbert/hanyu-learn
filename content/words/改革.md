## Why this combination
改 "to change" and 革 (here in its figurative "to reform, to remove the
old" sense — see 革's own etymology, from tanning raw hide into finished
leather) combine directly into the standard political/economic word for
reform.

## Notes
改革 anchors 改革开放 "Reform and Opening-up" (China's post-1978 economic
policy), 教育改革 "education reform" — always structural, institutional
change rather than a small adjustment.
