## Why this combination
打 here functions as a "light verb" carrying little independent meaning
of its own (the same role it plays in 打扫, 打算) rather than its literal
"to hit," paired with 扮 "to dress up as, to play a part" (see 扮's own
etymology) to form the everyday verb for dressing and grooming oneself.

## Notes
打扮 covers both the act ("to get dressed up") and the resulting look
("her attire/appearance," 打扮得很漂亮 "dressed up beautifully") —
distinct from 扮演, which is specifically about playing a role rather
than personal grooming.
