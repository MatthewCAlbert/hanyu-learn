## Why this combination
充 ("to fill, full," as in 充满) plus 分 (here in its "a measure, a
degree" sense, as in 十分, "extremely," lit. "to a full ten parts") names
something filled all the way to its complete measure: fully sufficient.

Rated "semi" for 分's abstract "degree/measure" sense, distinct from its
more common "to divide" meaning.

## Notes
充分 sits before a verb as an intensifying adverb (充分利用, "to make
full use of") at least as often as it stands as a plain adjective
(理由充分, "the reasons are ample/well-founded").
