## Why this combination
功 "achievement, skill, effect" (see also 成功, 功能) combined with 夫,
here a light suffix originally meaning "a man, labour spent" (as in
丈夫 "husband" in an unrelated sense): skill built up through invested
effort and time — the source of the English loanword "kung fu."

## Notes
功夫 names martial arts skill (功夫电影 "kung fu movie") and, just as
commonly, "time and effort spent" on something (花了不少功夫 "spent quite
a lot of effort/time on it") — the martial-arts sense is the one most
familiar internationally, but the "effort/time" sense is at least as
common in everyday Chinese.
