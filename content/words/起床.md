## Why this combination
A verb-object compound, not two verbs: 起 (to rise) takes 床 (bed) as its
object — literally "to rise from bed." Chinese builds a great many everyday
verbs this way, and they behave like phrases: they can be split, so "I got up
at seven" can put the time inside the compound.

Recognising verb-object structure matters practically. 起床 cannot take another
object, because it already has one — you cannot say 起床房间.

## Notes
Same pattern in 睡觉 (sleep + sleep), 吃饭 (eat + rice), 上班 (ascend + shift).
All are inseparable-looking words that are grammatically verb + object.
