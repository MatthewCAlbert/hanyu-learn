## Why this combination
干 "dry" + 净 "clean, pure." Dryness and cleanliness go together — a wiped, dry
surface is a clean one — and the pair became the everyday adjective "clean." By
extension "leaving nothing": 吃干净 "eat it all up," 忘得一干二净 "forget
completely."

## Notes
干 is *gān* here (dry), not *gàn* "to do." The "nothing left" sense is why
干净 attaches to verbs as a resultative.
