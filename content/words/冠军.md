## Why this combination
冠 "a cap, a crown" (also "to top, to be first") plus 军 "army, troops"
comes from a military idiom for the leading unit — "the crowning force
[at the front]" — that shifted from military rank to sporting rank once
the word generalised.

## Notes
冠军, 亚军, 季军 form the everyday medal-podium set (1st, 2nd, 3rd place);
夺冠 "to win the championship" (literally "to seize the crown") keeps the
older "crown" imagery alive in modern sports reporting.
