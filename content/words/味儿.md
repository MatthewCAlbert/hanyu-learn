## Why this combination

味 ("taste, flavor," as in 味道) plus the retroflex diminutive suffix 儿
(common in northern spoken Mandarin) names the everyday casual word for
a taste or smell.

## Notes

有味儿 ("has a smell/flavor," often implying an unpleasant one in
context) and 什么味儿 ("what does it smell/taste like?") both build on
this word — the everyday, casual spoken counterpart to the more neutral
written 味道.
