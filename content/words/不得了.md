## Why this combination
得了 here is the potential-complement pattern "can be managed/resolved"
(得 "to manage to" + 了 "finished, settled"); negated by 不, 不得了
literally says "cannot be managed" — a situation so extreme it exceeds
control. From there it grammaticalized into a general intensifier, a leap
not recoverable by parsing the three characters at face value.

## Notes
不得了 works both as an alarmed exclamation ("不好了，不得了了!" "oh no,
this is terrible!") and, after 得, as an intensifier meaning "extremely":
高兴得不得了 "extremely happy" — the two uses share the same idiom but
opposite emotional valence, decided entirely by context.
