## Why this combination

A near-synonym compound: 树 ("a tree") and 林 ("a forest, a grove," as in
森林, "forest [lit. tree-tree + tree-tree-tree, doubled and tripled for
denser growth]") both independently name wooded land. Pairing them gives
the everyday word for a stand of trees.

## Notes

树林 names a smaller stand of trees than 森林 ("forest"), which implies a
larger, denser wooded area — the same 林 root scales up through
repetition (木 "tree" → 林 "grove," two trees → 森 "dense forest," three
trees).
