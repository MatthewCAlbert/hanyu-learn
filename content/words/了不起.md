## Why this combination

了 (a grammatical complement particle) plus 不起 ("cannot rise to,
cannot manage," 起 as in 起来, "to get up/arise") fuses into a fixed
idiom whose literal sense — "beyond what one could rise to or handle" —
bleached into praise for something beyond the ordinary: amazing.

Rated "opaque": the modern complimentary sense isn't recoverable from
the grammatical parts without already knowing the set phrase.

## Notes

了不起 works as a standalone exclamation ("了不起!", "Amazing!") and as a
predicate (他真了不起, "he's really impressive") — 没什么了不起
("nothing much, no big deal") negates it into a dismissive set phrase.
