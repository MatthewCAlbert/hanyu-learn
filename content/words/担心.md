## Why this combination
担 here is the verb "to carry (on a shoulder pole)," and 心 ("heart," standing
for one's state of mind) is its object: worry is imagined as a weight the
heart is made to carry. As a verb-object compound it behaves like a phrase —
it can split, so "worried about you" becomes 担你的心 in older or more
literary phrasing, though modern speech usually keeps 担心 together and adds
a 为/替/对 phrase instead (为你担心).

The "semi" transparency comes from 担's less common literal sense: learners
who only know 担 from 负担 (burden) or 扁担 (carrying pole) still have to make
the "carrying a weight" leap to reach "worry."

## Notes
Common pattern: 担心 + clause (我担心他不来 "I'm worried he won't come") — no
particle needed between 担心 and the thing worried about, unlike English
"worried that."
