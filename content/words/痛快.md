## Why this combination

痛 (here an intensifying "thoroughly, to the fullest," an extended
sense beyond its literal "pain," as in 痛苦) plus 快 ("happy, pleased,"
as in 快乐) names being thoroughly and fully pleased: delighted.

Rated "semi" for 痛's intensifying role here, distinct from its literal
"pain" meaning — the same "extreme = intense" pattern as English
"painfully honest," but here applied to pleasure instead.

## Notes

玩得很痛快 ("had a thoroughly great time") and 说话很痛快 ("speaks very
frankly/straightforwardly") both build on this word — covering both a
feeling of full satisfaction and, by extension, a manner that holds
nothing back.
