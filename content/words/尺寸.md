## Why this combination

尺 (a *chi*, a traditional unit of length, roughly a third of a meter,
also "a ruler") plus 寸 (a *cun*, a smaller traditional unit, one-tenth
of a *chi*) names two units of measure together, standing in for "size"
or "measurements" in general.

Rated "semi": the specific traditional units are no longer part of
everyday measurement, so the compound now reads as a fixed word for
"size" rather than a literal length.

## Notes

量尺寸 ("to take measurements") and 尺寸不对 ("the size/measurements are
wrong") use 尺寸 for clothing, furniture, and other fitted items —
不合尺寸 specifically means "doesn't fit."
