## Why this combination
Fully opaque — you cannot derive "thing" from "east" and "west." The compound
is a contraction of a directional pair standing for "everywhere," and by
extension "whatever there is."

The popular story attributes it to the east and west markets of Tang-era
Chang'an, where one shopped for goods. It is a good story and it may be true,
but it is not securely attested; treat it as folk etymology rather than
established fact. Hence confidence: medium.

Note the tone: *dōngxi* (neutral second syllable) means "thing," while
*dōngxī* with a full first tone means the actual compass directions.

## Notes
A useful early example that Chinese compounds are not always decomposable —
sometimes the honest answer is "this one you memorise."
