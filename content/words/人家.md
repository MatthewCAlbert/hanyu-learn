## Why this combination
人 ("person") plus 家 ("family, household") literally names a household —
and that concrete sense survives (一户人家, "a household/family"). But the
word's everyday spoken uses have drifted far from that literal meaning:
"other people in general" (人家都这么说, "that's what everyone says") and,
most surprisingly, a colloquial way to refer to oneself or a specific
third person with a note of complaint or coyness (人家不想去嘛, "I [said
with a pout] don't want to go").

Marked "idiom"/opaque because those colloquial pronoun-like uses cannot
be derived from "person + household" at all — they must be learned as
fixed, separate conversational functions of the same written word.

## Notes
Context and tone of voice do most of the work distinguishing 人家's three
very different jobs — written Chinese gives few other clues, so listening
practice matters more than analysis here.
