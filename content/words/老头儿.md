## Why this combination
老 "old" (as in 老人, 老年) combined with 头 here used colloquially for
"a person, a fellow" (not its literal "head" sense) plus the 儿-suffix
common in Beijing-flavored colloquial speech: an old fellow.

## Notes
老头儿 is a casual, somewhat familiar way to refer to an old man — 老人,
its more neutral and respectful relative, is appropriate in any register,
while 老头儿 can read as endearing among familiars or dismissive
depending on tone and context.
