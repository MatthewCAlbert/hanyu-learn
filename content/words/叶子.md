## Why this combination

叶 ("a leaf" — covered elsewhere in this app as a character that
borrowed an unrelated older glyph originally meaning "harmonious" when
simplified) plus the nominal suffix 子 names the everyday plant part: a
leaf.

## Notes

树叶 ("tree leaves," lit. "tree + leaf," no suffix) and 叶子 both name
leaves — 叶子 is the more standalone, spoken-friendly form, while 叶
alone mostly appears bound in compounds.
