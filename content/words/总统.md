## Why this combination
总 carries its "overall, chief, in command of everything" sense (as in
总经理, 总共), and 统 carries its "to unify, to govern as a single whole"
sense (also seen in 系统, 传统). "The one who chiefly governs the whole [of
the country]" names the head of state in a republic — a term coined for a
modern institution that has no exact precolonial Chinese equivalent.

## Notes
总统 is specifically the head of state in a presidential system (美国总统,
"the US president"); China's head of state carries the different title
主席 (Chairman/President) — the two titles are not interchangeable even
though both translate to English "president."
