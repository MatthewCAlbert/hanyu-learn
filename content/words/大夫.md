## Why this combination
大, read dài rather than dà in this word, combined with 夫 "a man, a
gentleman [of rank]": originally an ancient Chinese official title
(大夫 dàfū, "grand master," a court rank), which drifted in colloquial
Northern Chinese speech to mean "physician" — the two readings and
senses have since split apart.

## Notes
大夫 (dàifu) is the everyday spoken word for "doctor" in much of
northern China (看大夫 "to see a doctor") — 医生, the standard, national
word for "doctor," is used everywhere and in writing; the historical
official-title reading dàfū survives only in that older, unrelated
sense.
