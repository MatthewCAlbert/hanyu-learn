## Why this combination
签 "to sign, to inscribe" (see 签's own etymology) acting on the object 字
"character, written name" gives exactly "to sign one's name" —
splittable, as in 签了字 "has signed."

## Notes
签字 leans toward formal/legal contexts (合同签字 "to sign a contract") —
close to 签名, though 签字 often implies the specific act of putting pen
to an official document.
