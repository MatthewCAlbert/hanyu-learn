## Why this combination
全 "whole, entire" (see also 全面, 全部) combined with 场 "a site, a
venue, a duration [of an event]" (see also 场所, 场合): the entire
venue/duration — everyone present, or the whole span of an event.

## Notes
全场 covers "everyone present" (全场欢呼 "the whole audience cheered")
and "the entire duration of a match" (全场比赛 "the whole match") —
both senses share 场's dual "venue" and "the event held there" logic.
