## Why this combination

吃 (here "to undergo, to suffer [an experience]," an extended sense
beyond its literal "to eat," as in 吃苦, "to endure hardship") takes 惊
("to be startled, alarmed," as in 惊讶) as its object: to undergo a
fright.

Rated "semi" for 吃's extended "to undergo" sense here, distinct from
its literal "to eat" meaning.

## Notes

令人吃惊 ("astonishing, lit. makes people jump") is the common
attributive frame; 吃惊 itself is more a state/reaction than an active
verb someone deliberately performs.
