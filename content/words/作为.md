## Why this combination
作 is "to do, to act," and 为 here takes its less-common reading wéi, "to be,
to serve as" (the same 为 as in 成为, 认为) rather than the far more frequent
preposition reading wèi ("for"). "To act as/to be" collapses into the
preposition-like "作为" (as a X, 作为老师 "as a teacher") and, separately, into
the noun "conduct, what one does/accomplishes" (他很有作为 "he has real
accomplishments").

The tone shift (为 wéi, not wèi) is exactly the kind of thing that makes this
"semi" rather than fully transparent: get the reading wrong and the meaning
does not track.

## Notes
As a preposition, 作为 always precedes a noun phrase describing a role or
capacity (作为一个学生 "as a student"); as a noun, it describes a person's
achievements or the good they have done (有所作为 "to accomplish something").
