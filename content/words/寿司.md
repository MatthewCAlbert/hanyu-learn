## Why this combination
寿司 transliterates Japanese "sushi" (寿司, shòu-sī approximates the
Japanese pronunciation, itself borrowed into Mandarin using the same
Chinese characters Japanese uses to write the word); 寿 "longevity" and
司 "to manage" were selected for sound, carrying no combined meaning of
their own here.

## Notes
寿司 anchors 寿司卷 "sushi roll," 寿司店 "sushi restaurant" — a rare case
of a loanword borrowed via shared kanji/hanzi rather than pure sound
transcription, since Japanese itself wrote "sushi" with these same
characters.
