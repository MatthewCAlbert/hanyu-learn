## Why this combination
所, here a nominalising particle "that which..." (as in 所以, turning a
following predicate into a noun phrase) combined with 长, here read
cháng "strong, excelling" (as in 长处, its own close relative) rather
than "long": that which [one] excels at — one's forte. (A separate,
unrelated word 所长 suǒzhǎng, read with a different tone on 长, means
"head of an institute.")

## Notes
所长, read suǒcháng, names a person's particular strength or talent
(发挥所长 "to make the most of one's strengths") — 长处, its
near-synonym built the same way, is used interchangeably in this sense.
