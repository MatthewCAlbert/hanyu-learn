## Why this combination
妻 is the classical, still-current word for "wife" (also seen in 夫妻,
husband and wife); 子, read with a neutral tone here, is the same generic
nominal suffix that turns single morphemes into two-syllable nouns
elsewhere (桌子, 椅子, 儿子's own 子 originally meant "child" but is
grammaticalized here into pure suffix). No "child" meaning is present in
this word at all — a common trap for learners who know 子 mainly as
"child."

## Notes
Do not read 妻子's 子 as "child" — the classical, literary use of 妻子 to
mean "wife and children" together exists in older/formal registers, but
the everyday modern qīzi is simply "wife."
