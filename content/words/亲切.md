## Why this combination
亲, here "close, intimate" (see also 亲人, 亲自) combined with 切 "cutting
close, pressing, deeply felt" (as in 关切 "concerned"): close and
deeply felt — warm, cordial.

## Notes
亲切 describes a warmly welcoming manner or a feeling of closeness
(亲切的问候 "a cordial greeting," 感到亲切 "to feel a sense of warmth/
closeness") — 热情, its neighbour, describes enthusiasm and warmth more
generally, while 亲切 stresses the intimate, personal quality of the
warmth.
