## Why this combination
客气 "to stand on ceremony, to be politely reserved" (客 guest + 气 manner —
"guest-like reserve"). 不客气 = "no need for ceremony," the standard reply to
谢谢. The same phrase said of a person means the opposite of polite: 他说话很
不客气 "he was quite blunt."

## Notes
别客气 "make yourself at home." The two readings — gracious vs. brusque — are
told apart entirely by context.
