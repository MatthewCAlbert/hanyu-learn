## Distinctions
Both are colloquial verbs for "doing work" that contrast with neutral 工作
(see [[work-register]]). 干活儿 is the more general "do manual or hands-on
labour / get on with the task" — it can describe a single stretch of physical
work (干了一天活儿) without implying an employer. 打工 specifically means
working for someone else, usually casual, hourly, or migrant/student labour,
and carries a flavour (via its Cantonese-into-Mainland origin) that 干活儿
lacks. Do not use 打工 for one's regular, salaried profession — that stays
工作 or 上班.

## Corpus evidence
Lexicographic (L): 现汉/汉语大词典 both mark 打工 as informal, hired/temporary
labour; 干活儿 is glossed as doing (typically manual) work generally.
Reviewed (R): 打工's own entry distinguishes it from 工作 (neutral) and 上班
(regular job) but not explicitly from 干活儿 — medium confidence because the
干活儿/打工 boundary is a usage pattern, not a direct lexicon cross-reference.
