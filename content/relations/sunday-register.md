## Distinctions
Both name the same day. Monday–Saturday are numbered (星期一…星期六); Sunday
breaks the pattern and takes 星期 + a "day" word instead — colloquial 天 in
speech, literary 日 in writing. Calendars, official notices, and timetables
print 星期日; people say 星期天 out loud. Neither is wrong in the other's
context, but swapping them reads slightly off — a spoken 星期日 sounds stiff,
a printed 星期天 slightly casual.

## Corpus evidence
Lexicographic (L): 现汉 marks 日 as the more literary register of "day"
against colloquial 天 in this construction.
Reviewed (R): both word files state the register split explicitly — 星期日's
entry: "日 is the more formal register — hence 星期日 on calendars... 星期天 in
speech."
