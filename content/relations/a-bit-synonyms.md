## Distinctions
一点儿 is the bare quantity phrase: "a little (of something)" as the object of
a verb (喝一点儿 "drink a bit") or, after an adjective, "a bit more" (好一点儿
"a bit better"). 有点儿 adds 有 and moves in front of an adjective or verb as an
adverb meaning "somewhat," and by convention flags something mildly negative
or unwelcome (有点儿累, 有点儿贵) — it does not cover the "a bit more" use
(好一点儿, not 好有点儿). 有一点儿 is the fuller, more emphatic form of 有点儿,
sharing the same adverbial, negative-leaning sense; the two are interchangeable
in that use. Do not swap 一点儿 for 有点儿/有一点儿 when it is the direct object
of a verb.

## Corpus evidence
Lexicographic (L): 现汉 cross-references 有点儿 to the "somewhat" adverbial use
and keeps 一点儿 as the general quantity phrase; 有一点儿 is glossed as the fuller
form of 有点儿.
Reviewed (R): three-way split by syntactic slot (object vs pre-adjectival
adverb) and register (textbook vs fuller spoken form).
