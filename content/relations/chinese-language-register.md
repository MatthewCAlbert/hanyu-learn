## Distinctions
All three can be glossed "Chinese," but they answer different questions.
汉语 is the ethnolinguistic term — the language of the Han people, as opposed
to China's other languages (藏语 Tibetan, 蒙语 Mongolian); it is the word
linguists and textbooks (对外汉语 "Chinese as a foreign language") reach for.
中文 is the everyday general word for the language, with a slight lean toward
the written form (中文课本, 中文系 "Chinese department"). 普通话 is narrower
than either: it names specifically the standardised *spoken* register based
on Beijing pronunciation, promoted for use across all dialect areas — it is
not a synonym for "Chinese" as a whole, only for its standard spoken form,
and it contrasts with regional speech (上海话, 广东话) in a way 汉语/中文 do not.
