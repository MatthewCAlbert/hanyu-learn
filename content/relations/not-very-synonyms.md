## Distinctions
Both soften a following adjective or verb: 不太好 and 不大好 both read "not very
good." 不太 is the more universal, textbook-safe hedge and sits comfortably
before almost any adjective or verb. 不大 carries an extra "not often" sense
before an action verb (不大来 "doesn't come often") that 不太 does not share,
and reads a little more colloquial/spoken. They are not always freely
swappable — some set phrases favour one by convention, and 不大 sits less
naturally before abstract or formal adjectives.

## Corpus evidence
Lexicographic (L): 现汉 lists both as pre-adjectival/pre-verbal negators of
degree; only 不大 carries the additional frequency ("not often") sense.
Reviewed (R): near-synonymous hedge, register/collocation caution — medium
confidence because the finer distinction is a usage pattern rather than a
dictionary cross-reference.
