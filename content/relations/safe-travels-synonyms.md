## Distinctions
Both are fixed parting formulas said to someone setting off on a journey, and
are close to interchangeable as a general "have a good trip." The nuance:
一路平安 (built on 平安 "safe and sound") emphasises safety — coming through
unharmed. 一路顺风 (built on 顺风 "a following wind," from the age of sailing
junks) emphasises ease and smoothness of the journey itself, not just safety.
Either works as a generic send-off; 一路平安 is the safer default when the
trip itself carries real risk (a long drive, a flight in bad weather).

## Corpus evidence
Lexicographic (L): 现汉 defines both as parting wishes for a traveller, with
一路平安 built on 平安 "safety" and 一路顺风 on the nautical "following wind"
metaphor for an easy journey.
Reviewed (R): both word files independently gloss themselves as "a parting
wish" for a traveller's journey, differing only in what they emphasise.
