## Distinctions
Both are adverbs meaning "always." 总是 is neutral and works for a fact or
habit without any attitude attached (他总是很忙 "he's always busy" — neutral
observation). 老是 is more colloquial and, in most contexts, carries a
complaining or exasperated tone about a repeated, unwanted behaviour (他老是
迟到 "he's ALWAYS late" — a gripe). Do not use 老是 where you mean a plain,
neutral fact with no attitude implied.

## Corpus evidence
Lexicographic (L): 现汉 marks 老是 as colloquial (口语) with a nuance of
repeated/undesired action; 总是 is unmarked/neutral.
Reviewed (R): near-synonymous adverbs of frequency, split by
register/attitude — medium confidence because the "annoyance" reading is a
usage tendency rather than an absolute rule.
