## Distinctions
Both describe panic, sharing the root 慌 "flustered." 慌张 typically
describes an individual's own visibly flustered manner — shaky hands, a
stammering voice (神色慌张 "a flustered expression"). 慌乱 more often
describes the resulting chaos, especially of a crowd or scene thrown into
disorder by panic (人群慌乱 "the crowd panicked into disorder") — disorder as
much as the feeling itself.

## Corpus evidence
Lexicographic (L): 现汉 keeps 慌张 tied to an individual's flustered manner
and 慌乱 tied to resulting chaos/disorder, both built on 慌 "panic."
