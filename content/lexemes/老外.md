老 "old, familiar" (the same casual prefix as [[哥们]], [[老铁]]) + 外 "outside,
foreign" → the everyday spoken word for a foreigner, especially one of
non-Asian appearance. Casual and generally good-natured rather than
derogatory in ordinary use, but — unlike the neutral, written-safe 外国人 —
it is a familiar, slightly reductive nickname rather than a formal
descriptor; avoid it in writing or in addressing someone directly in a formal
setting.
