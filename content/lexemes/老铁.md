老 "old, familiar" (the same affectionate prefix as [[哥们]]) + 铁 "iron," used
figuratively for an unbreakably close friendship — the same 铁 as in 铁哥们儿
"iron buddy." Originally Northeastern regional slang for a close friend, it
spread nationwide through livestreaming and short-video culture (hosts
greeting an audience as 老铁们). Casual, warm, and address-term-like; not used
in writing. Confidence medium: the 老 + 铁 "iron friendship" pattern is
well-attested via 铁哥们儿, but 老铁 itself as a standalone term is documented
mainly through corpus/media use rather than a direct current dictionary
headword.
