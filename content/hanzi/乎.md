## Etymology
A pictograph of breath or voice, per Shuowen: built on 兮 (an exclamatory
breath-particle shape) with strokes representing the sound rising up and
dispersing into the air — the visual and auditory image of a voice
trailing upward at the end of an utterance, exactly the intonation of a
question or exclamation.

## Mnemonic
A voice rising up and scattering into the air at the end of a sentence:
"乎," the classical particle marking a question or exclamation.

## Notes
乎 survives in modern Mandarin mostly fossilized inside compounds like
似乎 and 几乎 (both covered elsewhere in this app), where it no longer
carries independent meaning — its old question-marking role is now
almost entirely classical/literary.
