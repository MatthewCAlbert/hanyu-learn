## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 木 (wood) supplies the meaning, and 直 *zhí* supplies the
sound, an exact match. Shuowen's original, narrow definition names a
specific wooden fixture: a door's supporting post, standing straight up
(直, "straight, upright") — the same "standing straight" logic behind
the everyday modern sense "to plant" (standing a sapling upright in the
ground).

## Mnemonic
Wood 木 planted straight 直 into the ground, upright as a doorpost:
"植," to plant.

## Notes
植物 ("plant," lit. "planted thing") and 种植 ("to cultivate/grow,"
pairing 植 with 种) both build on this character's core "planted upright"
image.
