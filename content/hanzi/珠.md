## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 王 (here 玉, jade — used broadly for precious stones) supplies
the meaning, 朱 *zhū* supplies the sound and reads unchanged. Shuowen's
gloss 蚌之陰精 is a poetic classical description: "the yin-essence of a
mussel" — an early folk-scientific account of how pearls form inside
shellfish.

## Mnemonic
A precious stone 王, coloured deep as cinnabar red 朱 — a pearl or gem.

## Notes
珠 anchors 珍珠 "pearl," 珠宝 "jewellery," and the classifier-like use in
一珠 (a bead/drop) — also appears in 眼珠 "eyeball," extending "small round
precious object" to the eye itself.
