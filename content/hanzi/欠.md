## Etymology
Pictographic. Shuowen: 張口气悟也, "opening the mouth as breath escapes" —
象气从人上出之形, "picturing breath coming up and out of a person." The graph
is a kneeling person with an open mouth, breath curling out: a yawn. "To
lack, to owe" is a later borrowed sense with no pictorial connection to the
yawn — the character was simply reused for its sound/shape.

## Mnemonic
A person 人-like figure with mouth open ⺈, breath escaping: a yawn.

## Notes
Kangxi radical 76, and still the mouth/breath component in 吹 (to blow),
次 (next, unrelated borrowing), and 欢 (happy, simplified from 歡). The
"lack, owe" sense (欠钱 "to owe money," 欠缺 "to be short of") is now by far
the more common use in modern Mandarin.
