## Etymology
The oracle-bone graph is widely read as a pestle — the original of 杵 *chǔ*
"pestle." It was borrowed very early to write the seventh of the twelve
earthly branches, and with it the branch's slot in the day: the double-hour
around noon. Shuowen's gloss (啎 "to go against") reflects a later
sound-association, not the picture.

## Mnemonic
A pestle standing upright at the height of the day: noon.

## Notes
The branch 午 gives 上午 / 中午 / 下午. The pestle sense moved to 杵. Not to be
confused with 牛 "ox" or 干 "trunk," which it resembles.
