## Etymology
Ideographic per Shuowen: 禾 (grain) over 子 (child) forms "the young/lesser
designation" — 季 originally meant "the youngest" (季父, "youngest uncle";
季弟, "youngest brother"), not a period of time at all.

"Season, quarter (of a year)" is a later, separate extension: each of the
four farming seasons was itself numbered oldest-to-youngest within the
year, echoing the same "ranked by youngness" idea that 季 already carried
for people. This is a real but somewhat indirect bridge, hence "medium"
rather than "high" confidence on the full meaning chain.

## Mnemonic
A grain stalk 禾 topped by a small child 子: the youngest — and, by
extension, the season a young crop is harvested in.

## Notes
季节 (season) and 季军 (third place in a competition — literally "the
youngest-ranked army/contender") both descend from the same "youngest, last
in rank" core sense, in two very different directions.
