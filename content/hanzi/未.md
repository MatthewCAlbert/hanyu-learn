## Etymology
A pictograph of a tree with overlapping, densely doubled branches and
leaves (象木重枝葉) — foliage grown thick but the fruit not yet ripened. That
"not yet ready" image is why 未 was borrowed to write the negative adverb
"not yet." It is one of the twelve Earthly Branches (地支) as well, a
separate, unrelated calendrical use riding on the same graph.

It is easy to conflate 未 with its near-twin 末 (tip, end): 末 marks the very
*top* stroke of the tree (the tip), while 未 shows *overlapping* branches
(fullness, not-yet-ripeness). The two graphs are deliberately close because
the concepts they mark — "the very end" vs. "not yet at the end" — are
opposites built from the same tree pictograph.

## Mnemonic
A tree 木 with an extra branch still crowding the top, not yet grown into
its final shape: "未," not yet.

## Notes
Compare 末 (tip/end) — same tree pictograph, opposite idea, and a common
confusion pair for learners because the two characters differ by only where
the extra stroke sits.
