## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 歹 (bone fragments, the death/decay radical seen in 死,
残) supplies the meaning — Shuowen's original definition is stark, "to
die, to be executed" — and 朱 *zhū* supplies the sound.

From that grim original sense, 殊 generalized to "a decisive, extreme
separation" and, from there, to the far milder modern sense "markedly
different, exceptional" (特殊, covered elsewhere in this app) — a
striking severance softened over time into simply "notably distinct."

## Mnemonic
Something cut off 歹 so decisively, marked in red 朱, that it stands
apart from everything else: "殊," exceptional.

## Notes
特殊 ("special, particular") is 殊's overwhelmingly dominant modern use —
the character's dark original "death" sense survives only as etymological
history, not living usage.
