## Etymology
This is one of the strangest etymologies in the HSK set. Shuowen's 強 has
nothing to do with strength: it names 蚚, a small grain-eating beetle, built
from 虫 (insect, meaning) and 弘 *hóng* (sound). "Strong, powerful" is a
phonetic loan (假借) — 強 was borrowed purely for its sound to write an
unrelated word, and the borrowed sense is the only one that survived into
modern Chinese.

The modern shape has drifted further still: today's components, 弓 and 虽,
match neither the original 虫 nor 弘 cleanly, though 虽 itself is built from
口 over 虫 — so a trace of the original "insect" is still buried two layers
down, just not at a level the app's component check can point to directly.
Confidence is low because the meaning you learn (strong) is definitionally
disconnected from the etymology (a beetle), and the graphic history
connecting old and new shapes is not fully reconstructable.

## Mnemonic
A bow 弓 too strong for anyone else 虽 to draw.

## Notes
A useful character for the general lesson that pronunciation loans are
common and can fully erase a character's original meaning — nothing about
"strong" is recoverable from "beetle" without the history.
