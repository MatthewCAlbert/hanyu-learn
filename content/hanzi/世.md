## Etymology
Ideographic-and-phonetic. 卄 (a graphic doubling of 十 "ten," so "thirty"
when tripled) with its final stroke drawn out long — Shuowen's gloss,
三十年為一世, "thirty years make one generation," reads the extended
stroke as both a visual cue (stretching thirty out over time) and,
per Xu Shen, a borrowed sound value.

## Mnemonic
Thirty 卄 years, stretched out long: one lifetime, one generation.

## Notes
"A thirty-year generation" generalised into "an era, an age" (古代 vs
现代 both use different words, but 一生一世 "a whole lifetime" keeps the
original span) and, more broadly, into "the world" (世界, 全世界) — one's
generation and one's whole world converging into a single word.
