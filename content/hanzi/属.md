## Etymology
The traditional 屬 was pictophonetic: 尾 (tail) supplied the meaning —
Xu Xuan's commentary explains directly, 尾之連於體, "a tail is what
stays continuously attached to the body," the image behind "connected,
belonging to." 蜀 *shǔ* supplied the sound.

Simplification kept only the top portion of 尾 (now converged with the
unrelated 尸) and replaced the phonetic 蜀 with the unrelated 禹 (the
legendary sage-king). Confidence is medium: the "tail attached to the
body" logic is well attested for the traditional form, but the modern
components do not visibly carry it.

## Mnemonic
Like a tail 尸 that never separates from its owner 禹: belonging,
attached.

## Notes
属 covers both the concrete "family members, dependents" (家属, 亲属)
and the abstract "to belong to, to be classified as" (属于, 金属) — both
descend from the same "permanently attached" image.
