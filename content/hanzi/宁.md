## Etymology
The traditional form 寧 packed 宀 (a roof), 心 (heart), and 皿 (a food
vessel) together — a home with a full heart and a full table, the very
picture of peace and contentment — with a phonetic element rounding out
the sound.

**Simplification stripped away 心 and 皿 entirely**, leaving only 宀 with
丁 *dīng* standing in for sound. The rich "heart and provisions at home"
image is gone from the modern shape; only the bare roof remains as a
loose echo of "a peaceful home."

## Mnemonic
A roof 宀 sheltering a single, solid nail 丁 driven in for good: settled,
"宁," at peace.

## Notes
Two readings split by grammar: *níng* is the everyday adjective "peaceful,
calm" (安宁); *nìng* is the adverb "would rather" (宁可, 宁愿) — a distinct
grammaticalized use with no obvious link to "peace."
