## Etymology
Pictophonetic — with a twist. Shuowen's entry is actually under 它, not 蛇:
它 was the original pictograph for a snake, "long like a worm, its curled,
drooping tail pictured directly." Once 它 was borrowed for the grammatical
sense "it, other" (它 today's third-person neuter pronoun), 虫 (a generic
creature/worm radical) was added to make a new, unambiguous character for
the animal: 蛇, with 它 now demoted to a pure sound component.

## Mnemonic
A worm-like creature 虫 with the same curled, drooping shape 它 that once
meant "snake" all by itself.

## Notes
This is a clean example of semantic drift forcing a new character: the
pronoun took over 它's shape entirely, so 蛇 had to be built to keep "snake"
sayable. 虫 marks a large family of small creatures (蚂, 蚁, 蜘, 蛛) regardless
of their biological classification.
