## Etymology
Pictophonetic per Shuowen: 八 (to divide, to spread outward) supplies a
sense of extension, and 向 *xiàng* ("facing, direction") supplies the
sound — together suggesting something reached for or aspired to, still
extending outward. Shuowen's gloss, 庶幾 ("hopefully, it is to be
hoped"), points to an aspirational sense that has since faded from
everyday use, kept at medium confidence given how abstract that original
gloss is.

Two modern branches survive: "to esteem, to hold in high regard" (崇尚,
时尚 "fashion," lit. "what is currently esteemed") and, grammaticalized,
"still, yet" (尚且, 尚未 "not yet") — an aspiration not yet reached is, in
a sense, still pending.

## Mnemonic
Something still reaching 八 outward, facing 向 toward what it hopes to
become: "尚," esteemed — or, still not there yet.

## Notes
尚 survives in modern speech mostly through 时尚 (fashion/trendy) and the
formal adverb 尚且/尚未 (still, not yet) — a much narrower footprint than
its classical range.
