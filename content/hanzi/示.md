## Etymology
Pictographic. Shuowen reads the three hanging strokes as the sun, moon
and stars — celestial signs hung in the sky for people to read: 天垂象，
見吉凶，所以示人也, "Heaven hangs down signs, showing fortune and
misfortune, thereby revealing them to people." 示 names both the act of
divine revelation and, by extension, any altar or ritual through which
that revelation was sought.

## Mnemonic
Signs hanging in the sky 二小, there for anyone to read: to show, to
reveal.

## Notes
示 as a radical (compressed to 礻) marks the entire vocabulary of ritual,
worship and fate — 祝, 福, 社, 神 all group under it — while 示 itself,
uncompressed, generalised into the everyday "to show, to demonstrate"
(显示, 表示, 暗示).
