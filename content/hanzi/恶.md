## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 心 (heart, mind) supplies the meaning — Shuowen defines it
as a "fault, a wrongdoing" of the heart — and 亚 *yà* supplies the sound.

The reading splits by sense: *è* is the adjective "evil, vicious" (恶
人, 罪恶); *wù* is the verb "to hate, to detest" (厌恶, literally "to find
a fault and reject it"); *ě* survives only in 恶心 ("to feel nauseous," the
stomach's own "revulsion").

## Mnemonic
A heart 心 twisted by a fault 亚 (second-rate, flawed): "恶," evil — or
that same twisting feeling in the stomach, "恶心," nauseous.

## Notes
Three readings map to three distinct grammatical roles: *è* (adjective,
evil), *wù* (verb, to hate/detest), *ě* (bound in 恶心 only, nauseous) —
worth memorizing as three separate words sharing one graph.
