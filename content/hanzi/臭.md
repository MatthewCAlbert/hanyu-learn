## Etymology
Ideographic. Shuowen explains it through a dog's tracking ability: 犬 (dog)
below, 自 (originally a picture of a nose, the same component seen atop 鼻)
above — a dog identifies a fleeing animal's trail by its nose. The character
originally named the act of smelling/tracking a scent generally; "stench,
bad smell" is the sense that later dominated.

## Mnemonic
A nose 自 wrinkling at a dog's 犬 unmistakable odor.

## Notes
Two readings: *chòu* "stinking, bad" (臭味, 臭豆腐 — a genuinely fragrant food
despite the name) is by far the more common; *xiù* "smell (neutral)"
survives in the literary compound 无声无臭 "silent and odorless." 自, not 白,
is the nose component here — an easy stroke to miscopy.
