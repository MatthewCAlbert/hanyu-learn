## Etymology
Accounts of 免's origin diverge: some read the oldest forms as a person
removing a cap or helmet (免冠, "to remove one's hat," still a fixed
expression today), giving "to take off" as the root sense behind
"exempt, excused from"; others connect it to childbirth, the same root
later written 娩 with 女 added, "to be delivered, to escape (the womb)."
Neither account is settled enough for this entry to assert with
confidence, so it stays low.

Whichever the origin, the modern senses cluster tightly: "to remove/be
excused from" an obligation (免除, 避免) and "free of charge" (免费,
covered elsewhere in this app) both share the core idea of being let off
from something.

## Mnemonic
A hat lifted off and set aside — released from the obligation of wearing
it: "免," exempt.

## Notes
避免 ("to avoid") and 免费 ("free of charge") both extend from the same
"let off, excused from" core, applied to a danger and a cost
respectively.
