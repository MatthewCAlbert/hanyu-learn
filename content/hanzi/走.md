## Etymology
The old graph is 夭 "a person running with arms swinging" above 止 "a foot" —
the classical meaning is "to run." Shuowen: 趨也 "to hasten." The modern top
is a flattened 夭. In modern Mandarin the sense shifted to "to walk," with 跑
taking over "run."

## Mnemonic
Arms pumping 夭, feet flying 止: on the move.

## Notes
走 is Kangxi radical 156 and the semantic in 起 (rise), 越 (exceed), 超
(surpass), 趣. Classical "run" survives in 走狗 "running dog," 奔走.
