## Etymology
Shuowen's original definition is a surprise: 童 named a male slave or
servant — specifically one enslaved as punishment for a crime (辛, the
punishment-related component, marks this origin) — with a reduced form
of 重 *zhòng* supplying the sound. The word's dark original sense had
faded by the time it broadened into today's neutral, innocent "child" —
this entry keeps confidence at medium since the modern shape (立 over
里) does not cleanly preserve Shuowen's own components.

## Mnemonic
A small figure standing 立 within the safety of home 里, far removed from
the character's darker original sense: "童," a child.

## Notes
儿童 (children, covered elsewhere in this app) and 童年 ("childhood," lit.
"child-years") both use 童 in its fully modern, neutral sense — the old
"servant" meaning survives nowhere in living usage.
