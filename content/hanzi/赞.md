## Etymology
Ideographic in the traditional form 贊: 兟 (two 先, figures stepping
forward one after another, as in a formal procession) with 貝 (money,
gifts) — Shuowen's definition, 見, points to the act of formally
"appearing before" someone of rank, gifts in hand, in support of a cause
or petition. Given the layered, ceremonial nature of that original scene,
this entry keeps confidence at medium rather than asserting a single
clean semantic-phonetic split.

That formal act of presenting support broadened to "to assist" (赞助,
"to sponsor," lit. "to assist and help") and "to praise" (赞美, 称赞) —
supporting someone with gifts and supporting them with words both
counted as lending one's backing.

## Mnemonic
Figures stepping forward one after another, gifts 贝 in hand, to lend
their support: "赞," to praise or assist.

## Notes
赞成 ("to approve of, to support," as in a vote) and 赞美 ("to praise")
both extend this character's core "formal show of support" sense, one
toward a decision, one toward a person's qualities.
