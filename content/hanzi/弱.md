## Etymology
Ideographic per Shuowen: the character pictures something bending,
yielding under pressure (橈), with the fine, feathery strokes (彡彡,
echoed today by the two 冫-like marks) representing fine hair or down —
soft, easily bent things. The repeated 弓-like shapes suggest something
naturally curved and yielding, not (per Shuowen's own account) a story
about broken bows, which is a later folk reading of the same picture.

## Mnemonic
Two soft, bending shapes side by side, easily curved by the slightest
pressure: "弱," weak.

## Notes
弱 and 强/强 (strong) are among the first antonym pairs learners meet;
弱点 ("weak point," a person's or plan's specific vulnerability) is a
common extension into the abstract.
