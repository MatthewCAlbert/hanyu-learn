## Etymology
Ideographic per Shuowen: 甘 ("sweet, pleasant" — the taste-radical) with
匹 (a mate, a companion) — "extreme comfort and pleasure," Shuowen's own
gloss, the deep contentment of good food shared with good company. That
sense of an extreme, intensified state generalized to the everyday
adverb "very, extremely" (甚至, covered elsewhere in this app).

## Mnemonic
Something sweet 甘 enjoyed with a companion 匹: contentment taken to its
utmost — "甚," extremely.

## Notes
甚至 ("even, to the extent that," covered elsewhere in this app) is 甚's
overwhelmingly dominant modern use; the plain intensifier sense ("very")
survives mostly in more literary or set expressions.
