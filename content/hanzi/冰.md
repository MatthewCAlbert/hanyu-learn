## Etymology
Ideographic per Shuowen: 水 (water) plus 仌 (the original pictograph of ice
crystals/cracks, which later became the 冫 radical seen in 冷, 凉, 冻) —
water that has become hard. 仌 alone was the earliest way to write "ice";
水 was added once 仌/冫 had been generalized into a component reused across
many cold-related characters, to keep this specific character
unambiguously about water's frozen state.

## Mnemonic
Water 水 struck through with ice-crack lines 冫: "冰," ice.

## Notes
冰 is the one character in this ice-radical family where 冫 keeps its
literal "ice" meaning — contrast 冲, where the same two strokes stand in
for water instead.
