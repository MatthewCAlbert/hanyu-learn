## Etymology
The original character was pictophonetic: 手 (hand) supplied the
meaning; 乙 *yǐ* supplied the sound. Shuowen's gloss, 縱也, "to release,
to let slip," pictures something slipping loose from a grip — the direct
ancestor of "to lose."

Over time the shape drifted into the current form, which the app's
decomposition data reads as 丿 over 夫 — a graphic regularisation with no
recoverable "hand" or "乙" visible today. Confidence is medium because
the drift from 手+乙 to the modern shape is not documented step by step.

## Mnemonic
Something slipping 丿 right out of a grown man's 夫 hands: lost.

## Notes
失 covers both the concrete "to lose [an object]" (失去, 丢失-adjacent)
and the abstract "to fail, to miss" (失败, 失望, 失误) — every sense
keeps the core image of something that was held, then wasn't.
