## Etymology
Pictophonetic in an older form written 沈: 氵 (water) supplied the
meaning, and 冘 *yín* supplied the sound. Shuowen's own definition names a
specific kind of standing floodwater on high ground; separately, ancient
ritual texts describe sacrificing an ox by submerging it in a river (沈
牛于河), which plausibly reinforced the general sense "to submerge, to
sink" that the character carries today.

The modern shape 沉 shows what looks like 冗 in place of 冘 — a graphic
descendant rather than a fresh component with its own meaning, which is
why this entry keeps confidence at medium and does not declare it as a
separate phonetic.

## Mnemonic
Water 氵 closing calmly over something as it goes under: "沉," to sink.

## Notes
沉 extends from the literal "to sink" to a settled, weighty temperament
(沉默, "silent, taciturn"; 沉重, "heavy, grave") — something submerged
deep below the surface, whether an object in water or a person's outward
expression.
