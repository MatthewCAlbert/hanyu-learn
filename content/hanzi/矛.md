## Etymology
Pictographic. Shuowen identifies the specific weapon — a long spear
mounted on a war chariot, two *zhang* in length — and marks the graph
象形, "a picture," of that spear's shaft and blade.

## Mnemonic
A spear's shaft and point, angled up and ready to thrust.

## Notes
矛 is Kangxi radical 110 and survives mainly in the idiom 自相矛盾
"self-contradictory" (literally "spear and shield clashing with each
other" — from a classical parable about a merchant who claimed his spear
could pierce anything and his shield could block anything, a contradiction
impossible to reconcile).
