## Etymology
Ideographic. Shuowen glosses 尸 here not as "corpse" (its more familiar
sense) but as "that which presides, the roof/covering shape," with 至
"to arrive" beneath it — a person arrives and comes to rest under a roof.
Confidence is medium because Shuowen itself offers two competing readings
of 尸 in this entry (an owner presiding, or a pictorial roof), without
settling which is original.

## Mnemonic
Someone arriving 至 and settling under a roof 尸: home.

## Notes
屋 is a bound form in modern Chinese — it appears in compounds (房屋,
屋子, 屋顶) but is rarely used standalone the way 房 or 家 are.
