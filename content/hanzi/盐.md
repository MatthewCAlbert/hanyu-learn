## Etymology
Pictophonetic in the traditional form 鹽: 鹵 (brine, salt-bearing earth)
supplied the meaning, and 監 *jiān* supplied the sound — 監 itself
originally pictured a person peering into a basin of water, and that
basin connects naturally to the evaporation vessels used to produce salt.

**Simplification replaced 鹵 and reduced 監 to a fragment.** The modern
top component of 盐 no longer reads as 鹵, and only 皿 (vessel, from the
bottom of 監) survives as a recognizable, meaningful piece — fittingly,
since salt was traditionally produced by evaporating brine in shallow
vessels.

## Mnemonic
Brine left to evaporate in a shallow vessel 皿 until only white crystals
remain: "盐," salt.

## Notes
盐 shows the same simplification pattern as 权 and 奖: a real component
(here 皿) survives with its meaning intact, while the phonetic is lost or
reduced to an unreadable fragment.
