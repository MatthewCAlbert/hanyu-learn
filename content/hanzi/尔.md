## Etymology
Not securely attested in any recoverable concrete picture. Shuowen's own
gloss is already abstract — describing 爾 through a poetic compound 麗爾
"radiant, splendid" rather than analysing a clear image — and the
character's dominant classical use as the second-person pronoun "you" and
the adverbial suffix "thus, so" (犹 未尔, 果尔) long predates any settled
account of its origin.

## Mnemonic
A simple mark standing in for "you" — no picture needed, just address.

## Notes
尔 survives today mainly in fixed literary compounds: 尔虞我诈 "each trying
to outwit the other," and as the suffix -尔 in transliterated place names
(耐克, unrelated) — largely retired from everyday grammar in favour of 你.
