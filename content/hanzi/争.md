## Etymology
Ideographic. Two hands (爪 above, 又 below — together 𠬪, "to give and
take") pulling on a single object (丨, here regularised to 亅) from
opposite ends — Shuowen's gloss, 引也, "to pull, to draw toward oneself,"
and his own elaboration make it explicit: 凡言爭者，皆謂引之使歸於己,
"in every use of 爭, it means pulling something to make it one's own."

## Mnemonic
Two hands ⺈彐, pulling one object 亅 in opposite directions: a tug of
war.

## Notes
争 keeps its "pulling toward oneself" logic intact across very different
registers — 争取 "to strive to obtain," 竞争 "to compete," 战争 "war" —
all are, at root, two sides pulling the same thing toward themselves.
