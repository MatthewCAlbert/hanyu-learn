## Etymology
Pictographic. The oracle-bone form shows a raised mound or clod of earth
sitting on a ground line — a lump of soil, drawn directly. Shuowen reads
the strokes almost the same way, though framed as an etymological pun: 土
"earth" sounds like 吐 "to give birth to, to bring forth" — 地之吐生物者
也, "that which the earth brings forth living things from" — with the
two horizontal strokes as the ground above and below, and the vertical
stroke as a sprout pushing up through it.

## Mnemonic
A mound of earth 十, one stroke deep, on the ground 一: soil.

## Notes
土 as a component marks anything earth- or ground-related across a wide
family — 地, 城, 场, 域 — one of the most productive semantic radicals in
the script.
