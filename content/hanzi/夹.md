## Etymology
Ideographic, traditional 夾: Shuowen glosses it 持也, "to hold, to grasp,"
and describes the graph as a large figure 大 flanked by two smaller people,
one under each arm — literally "sandwiched between." The simplified 夹
keeps that same picture with 丷 standing in for the two flanking figures.

## Mnemonic
A big person 大 with someone tucked under each arm 丷 — squeezed in tight.

## Notes
夹 is both the verb "to sandwich, to clamp" (夹菜 "to pick up food with
chopsticks," literally "clamp food") and the noun for clamping objects
(夹子 "clip, folder"). Three readings by use: *jiā* is standard; *jiá*
(lined, unpadded, as in clothing) and *jià* (a narrow gorge, in place
names) are specialised.
