## Etymology
匈 was itself the original word for "chest" (its own inner 凶 component
contributing sound/visual association rather than an independent
"ferocious" meaning here); once 匈 was repurposed as the sound-alike name
for the Xiongnu (匈奴), a semantic determiner ⺼ (肉, flesh/body — the same
component as in 脱, 肥) was added to keep the "chest" word visually
distinct from the ethnonym. So 胸 is pictophonetic, with 匈 supplying both
the sound and the character's original meaning before ⺼ was layered on.

## Mnemonic
The body 肉 at its widest point, marked by 匈: "胸," the chest.

## Notes
胸 anchors a small family of chest/heart-adjacent compounds: 胸怀 (breadth
of mind, lit. "chest-bosom"), 胸口 (the pit of the chest) — the body part
standing in for feeling and character, much as English "heart" does.
