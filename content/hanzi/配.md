## Etymology
酉 (a wine vessel) is the one component every source agrees on. Shuowen
reads the rest as phonetic, 己 *jǐ*, and glosses the original meaning as
酒色, the particular colour produced by blending two different wines.

But oracle-bone and bronze forms instead show a kneeling person (卩) beside
the wine vessel — someone attending to or matching the wine — with 卩
gradually eroding into the unrelated shape 己 by the Warring States period.
Confidence is medium because the "person, not sound" reading better
explains the modern sense ("to pair, to match, to be matched with a
spouse") than Shuowen's phonetic-and-blended-wine story does.

## Mnemonic
A person 己 kneeling beside the wine jar 酉, matching the right cup to the
right guest.

## Notes
From "matching wines" or "a person paired with wine," 配 broadened cleanly
into "to match, to pair, to be worthy of" — 配合 "to coordinate," 分配 "to
allocate," 不配 "unworthy of."
