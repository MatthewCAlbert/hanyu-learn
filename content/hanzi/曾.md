## Etymology
Shuowen treats 曾 as 从八从曰, an "unpacking" particle of speech — already a
grammaticalised reading, not a picture of anything.

The oracle-bone form tells a different, more concrete story: it pictures a
steaming vessel (甑), the bottom part showing the perforated grate that a
steamer sits on, the top showing steam rising off it. That original "steam
vessel" sense was later written with 瓦 added (甑), while the bare 曾 was
freed up to be borrowed (假借) purely for its sound, to write the temporal
particle "once, already" used today. The app's decomposition data cannot
resolve the middle component, which fits with 曾's original picture no
longer being legible in the modern shape.

## Mnemonic
Once 曾, long ago, rice steamed 曾 in this very pot.

## Notes
曾 keeps two readings that track the two histories: *céng* "once, already"
(the borrowed grammatical sense used in HSK 3) and *zēng* in kinship terms
like 曾祖父 "great-grandfather," where "once removed" echoes the same "one
step back" idea.
