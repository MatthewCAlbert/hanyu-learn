## Etymology
Shuowen offers a gentle gloss: 眾萌也, "the sprouting multitude" — the
common people as new, tender growth. Modern paleographic scholarship
reads the oracle-bone and bronze forms very differently and far more
grimly: an eye pierced by a sharp point, a mark historically used on
captives or slaves to signify subjugation — "the people" originally
meaning, quite literally, the conquered and blinded.

Confidence is low because these two accounts are in real tension, and
neither this dataset nor mainstream scholarship treats the question as
settled — Shuowen's own reading may itself be a later, more palatable
reinterpretation of an already-obscured shape.

## Mnemonic
New growth 巳, still tender, still finding its footing 戈: the common
people.

## Notes
Whichever origin is correct, 民 has meant "the common people, the
citizenry" since the earliest connected texts — 人民, 公民 "citizen,"
农民 "farmer" — with no trace of either sprouting plants or captivity
surviving in ordinary modern use.
