## Etymology
止 (foot; to stop) is the one component every source agrees is really
there. Shuowen pairs it with 匕, glossed there as "to compare, to be next
to," giving "to stop, in relation to" — the seed of a demonstrative
"this [place/one], as opposed to that."

Modern scholarship generally doubts Shuowen's own gloss of 匕 here: the
oracle-bone form looks more like a foot planted beside a person, i.e. 止 +
人, with the "person" later flattening into the unrelated shape 匕 (as also
happened in 死). Confidence is medium because the component split is solid
but which story explains the second half is not settled.

## Mnemonic
A footprint 止 stopped right here 此, next to you 匕.

## Notes
此 opposes 彼 ("that") the way "this" opposes "that" in English — 此外
"besides this," 从此 "from this point on," 因此 "for this reason."
