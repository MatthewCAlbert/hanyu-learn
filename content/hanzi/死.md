## Etymology
Ideographic. 歹 (originally 歺, weathered bone/skeletal remains) paired with
a person — Shuowen reads it as 从歺从人: a living person set beside the
remains of the dead, "the separation of a person [from life]." The oracle-bone
form is more vivid still: a kneeling, mourning figure beside the bones.

In clerical script 歺 became 歹 and the person-component flattened into 匕,
which is why the modern right-hand side looks like the unrelated character
匕 "spoon" — it is not.

## Mnemonic
Bones 歹 beside a bowed figure 匕: what's left when life ends.

## Notes
歹 as a semantic component reliably marks "bad, death, misfortune" across a
small family: 殆 (dangerous), 殊 (different — originally "to die/differ"),
残 (残 injure/remnant).
