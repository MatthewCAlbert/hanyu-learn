## Etymology
Ideographic. 木 (a tree) enclosed within 囗 (a boundary) — Shuowen glosses
this, somewhat unexpectedly, as 故廬也 "an old dwelling": Duan Yucai
explains that a family plot of that era necessarily had trees planted
within its walls, so "tree inside an enclosure" pictured a homestead.
"Hemmed in, trapped" is the sense that survived and generalised.

## Mnemonic
A tree 木 boxed in on every side 囗: nowhere left to grow.

## Notes
困 keeps "trapped, hard-pressed" as its living sense — 困难 "difficulty,"
困境 "a predicament" — the "old dwelling" reading is now purely
etymological trivia.
