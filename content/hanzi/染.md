## Etymology
The classical composition is debated in its details, but the overall
picture is agreed: 水/氵 (water, top-left) for the dye-bath, 木 (wood,
bottom) for the plant source of natural dyes, and a middle stroke read by
different sources as either a numeral-like phonetic remnant or a graphic
simplification with no independent value of its own. Rather than assign
that middle stroke a definite role, it is safest to say: dyeing cloth in a
plant-based liquid is the core, agreed image, and "to catch [a disease],"
"to acquire [a habit]," and "to add colour washes [to a painting]" are all
later extensions of colour/substance transferring from one thing to
another.

## Mnemonic
Cloth dipped in water 水 steeped with dye from a tree 木 — the colour takes.

## Notes
染 spans literal dyeing (染发 "to dye one's hair") and contagion/influence
(感染 "to become infected," 传染病 "infectious disease," 沾染 "to be tainted
by") — colour, illness, and bad habits are all things that "染" onto you.
