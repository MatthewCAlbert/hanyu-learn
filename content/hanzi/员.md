## Etymology
Ideographic. 贝 (cowrie shell, used as ancient currency and a general
marker of counted value) with an enclosure above it — Shuowen's gloss,
物數也, is "the count of things," since valuables were what got counted
most carefully. The oracle-bone form actually shows a round-mouthed
tripod vessel, a sense later taken over entirely by 圆/圓; the "counting"
sense is what 员 kept.

The modern top component is written 口 rather than the original 囗 — the
two enclosure shapes converged visually over the character's history, not
a change in what is meant.

## Mnemonic
Valuables 贝 kept inside a boundary 囗, tallied one by one: a counted
member.

## Notes
"The count of things" narrowed specifically to "the count of people [in a
role]" — 演员, 服务员, 会员 — 员 today almost always names a person
belonging to some counted group, not an object.
