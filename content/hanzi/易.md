## Etymology
Pictographic, with two competing readings recorded side by side in
Shuowen itself. The primary entry says 易 pictures a lizard or chameleon
(蜥易/蝘蜓/守宮), an animal whose colour-changing skin made it a natural
symbol for "change." An alternative reading Shuowen also records takes
日 (sun) over 月 (moon) as picturing yin and yang in alternation —
another route to the same "change, exchange" sense. Confidence is medium
because the character's own dictionary tradition leaves this genuinely
open.

## Mnemonic
The sun 日, giving way to what's forbidden 勿 by night — day changing to
night, easy as that.

## Notes
易 covers both "to change, to exchange" (交易, 贸易 "trade") and,
separately, "easy, simple" (容易, 轻易) — something that changes readily
is, almost by definition, not difficult to shift.
