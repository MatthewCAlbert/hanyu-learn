## Etymology
The oracle-bone graph shows a person lying on a bed with an exaggerated,
half-shut eye — the picture *is* someone dreaming. Shuowen's Han-era analysis
already reads the traditional form 夢 as 夕 (night, semantic) plus an
abbreviated 瞢 (*méng*, phonetic, itself built from 眉+目+夕ish elements
evoking blurred sight).

**Simplification did not keep that phonetic.** The top of 梦 is 林 (two
trees), which is a graphic simplification of the old eye/eyebrow-based
component, not the tree radical — 林 supplies no meaning and no sound here.
Only 夕 (night), on the bottom, is a real, meaningful surviving component.

## Mnemonic
Two trees 林 standing silent under the night sky 夕: the stillness of sleep,
where 梦 ("dreams") happen.

## Notes
Do not read 林 as "forest" here — it is worth flagging explicitly, since it
is the single most visually plausible false lead in this character.
