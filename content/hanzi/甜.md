## Etymology
Ideographic. 甘 ("sweet, pleasant-tasting" — itself a pictograph of
something held in the mouth) beside 舌 (tongue) — Shuowen glosses 甜 as
美也, "delicious," nearly identical to its gloss of 甘 itself. Originally
甘 and 甜 both covered "good-tasting" broadly; 甜 later narrowed
specifically to "sweet," likely because sweetness is the most universally
preferred of the basic tastes.

## Mnemonic
Something pleasant 甘 lingering right on the tongue 舌: sweet.

## Notes
甜 and 甘 still overlap in some compounds (甘甜 "sweet," used together for
emphasis) — a reminder that the two characters were once near-synonyms
before 甜 specialised.
