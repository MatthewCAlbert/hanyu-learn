## Etymology
Ideographic. 纟 (silk thread) with a second component depicting broken
thread ends — the picture is threads, snapped, being tied back together.
Shuowen even records an older variant form, 賡 (庚+貝), which scholars
read as things passed on in succession, reinforcing the same "unbroken
continuation" sense from a different angle. Confidence is medium because
the app's decomposition data cannot resolve the right-hand piece as a
separate leaf.

## Mnemonic
Silk thread 纟, knotted back together after a break: to carry on.

## Notes
继 covers both the neutral "to continue" (继续, 相继) and the more
specific "to inherit, to succeed to" (继承, 继父/继母 "stepfather/
stepmother," i.e. one who continues a parental role) — unbroken
continuation whether of an action or of a family role.
