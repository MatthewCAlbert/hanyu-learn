## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 女 (woman) supplies the meaning, and 夷 *yí* supplies the
sound, an exact match. Shuowen's original definition is a precise
kinship term: specifically "one's wife's younger sister."

That narrow technical sense broadened to "mother's sister" (阿姨, the
everyday address term) and, further, to a general polite address for any
woman of one's mother's generation — the kinship term loosening into a
social courtesy title.

## Mnemonic
A woman 女 as familiar and welcome 夷 (夷 here carries only sound) as
one's own mother's sister: "姨," aunt.

## Notes
阿姨 is both the specific "maternal aunt" and, very commonly today, a
polite way to address any older woman, especially one performing a
caretaking role (a nanny, a cleaner) — parallel to how 叔叔 (uncle)
functions for men.
