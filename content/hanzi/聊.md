## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 耳 (ear) supplies the meaning, and 卯 *mǎo* supplies the
sound. Shuowen's original definition is a surprise: 聊 meant "a ringing in
the ears" — tinnitus, nothing to do with conversation at all.

The modern senses — "to chat" (聊天) and "merely, just" / "having nothing
better to do" (无聊, 聊以自慰) — both plausibly grew out of that same idle,
idle-noise association: an ear filled with idle ringing, or a mind with
nothing better to occupy it than chatter.

## Mnemonic
An ear 耳 catching the sixth sign 卯 (of the zodiac, here carrying only
sound) — ringing with idle chatter: "聊," to chat.

## Notes
无聊 ("boring, bored," lit. "without even idle chatter to fall back on")
and 聊天 ("to chat," lit. "chat about the sky/anything") both trace back to
this same root sense of idle, unfocused activity.
