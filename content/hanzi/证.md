## Etymology
Two originally distinct characters stand behind today's 证. 證 (semantic
言, phonetic 登) meant "to report, to bear witness" — the ancestor of the
modern "proof, certificate." 証 (semantic 言, phonetic 正), a separate,
older character, meant "to remonstrate, to correct a superior's conduct
through speech."

Modern simplification reused the smaller, already-existing 証 to write
every sense of 證. Confidence is medium: the 讠/正 split declared here is
genuinely Shuowen-attested, but for the "remonstrate" character, not the
"proof, evidence" meaning speakers actually use 证 for today.

## Mnemonic
Words 讠 spoken straight and true 正: proof.

## Notes
证 sits alongside 极 and 胜 in this batch as a third case of simplification
solving a stroke-count problem by resurrecting an old, rare character that
happened to sound close enough and already existed.
