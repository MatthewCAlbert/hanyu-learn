## Etymology
The oracle-bone form shows a kneeling figure (卩) drawn right up against a
food vessel (the left-hand element, worn down in the modern form to an
unresolved fragment) — someone approaching food to eat. "To approach,
arrive at" is the base meaning (即位, "to approach the throne" = to accede
to it).

From "arriving right at" a place or moment, 即 grammaticalized into "namely,
that is to say" (即, right at *this* point) and "even if" (即使, arriving
even at a hypothetical extreme). Shuowen marks 卩 as also carrying the
sound (从卩聲), so this may in fact be pictophonetic rather than purely
ideographic — kept at medium confidence since the left-hand vessel
component is not cleanly recoverable in the modern shape.

## Mnemonic
A kneeling figure 卩 pulled right up to the table: "即," right there,
immediately.

## Notes
Contrast with 既 (already, having finished) — the two are a matched pair:
即 is a figure approaching food, 既 is a figure turning away, having already
eaten.
