## Etymology
Pictophonetic: ⺼ (flesh) supplies the meaning, 古 *gǔ* supplies the
sound, shifted to *hú*. Shuowen's gloss 牛顄垂也 is "the dewlap hanging
under a cow's chin" — the loose flap of skin and flesh there. That
"hanging under the chin" image is very likely behind 胡子 "beard," and
the character was separately borrowed to refer to non-Han peoples to the
north and west (胡人), from which "reckless, wild, indiscriminate" (胡说,
胡闹) — the behaviour outsiders were stereotyped as having — later
developed.

## Mnemonic
Flesh ⺼ hanging loose under the chin, old 古 as the first beard grown
there.

## Notes
胡 covers three distinct lives: the beard (胡子), the ethnonym-derived
"foreign, northern" (胡琴 "a fiddle-type instrument of northern origin"),
and the intensifying "recklessly" (胡说八道 "to talk nonsense"). The last
sense dominates casual modern speech.
