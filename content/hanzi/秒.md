## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 禾 (grain) supplies the meaning, 少 *shǎo* supplies the sound
(shifted to *miǎo*). Shuowen's original sense is 禾芒也, "the awn/tip of a
grain ear" — the finest, thinnest part of the plant. That "smallest
measurable bit" sense is exactly why 秒 was later reused for the smallest unit
of time (a second) and of angle (an arc-second).

少 itself only lends sound here; do not read "few/little" into the etymology
even though it happens to echo the later "tiny unit" meaning — that is
coincidence of sense, not how the character was built.

## Mnemonic
A single grain-tip 禾 sliced so thin it takes only a second 少 to see it.

## Notes
The metaphorical leap — plant-tip to time-unit — mirrors how many measure
words in Chinese started as concrete objects (点 "dot," 刻 "carved mark" for a
quarter-hour).
