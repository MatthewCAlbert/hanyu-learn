## Etymology
Two of the same element stacked. Shuowen reads it 从重夕 "a doubled 夕," but
the 夕 here is generally taken as 肉 "a piece of meat," not "evening" — two
cuts of meat piled up, standing for abundance. Doubling a graph to mean "many"
is a regular device (林 "woods," 众 "crowd").

## Mnemonic
One portion of meat, and then another: more.

## Notes
The everyday opposite is 少. 多 also asks a degree: 多大, 多远, 多少. 多么 "how
(…!)" as an exclamation.
