## Etymology
The traditional 衛 pictured 行 (a crossroads/road, split to frame the
character on both sides) surrounding 韋 (feet marching around a
perimeter) — soldiers patrolling a boundary, the direct source of "to
guard, to defend."

Simplification reduced the entire picture to a bare two-stroke remnant
with no recoverable link to roads, feet, or patrolling. Confidence is low
and no component is declared, since the modern shape is essentially a
cursive shorthand rather than a semantic-plus-phonetic construction.

## Mnemonic
A single watchful stroke, standing guard at the gate.

## Notes
卫 survives almost entirely in compounds — 卫生 "hygiene" (literally
"guarding life"), 保卫 "to defend," 卫星 "satellite" (something that
"guards" or orbits a planet) — the original patrol has generalised into
protection of every kind.
