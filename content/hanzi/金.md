## Etymology
Bronze-script forms show two to four small dots beside/within a shape
generally read as 土 (earth) — metal ore-nuggets embedded in the ground,
pictured directly. Shuowen already analyses it as pictophonetic, 从土
(earth, meaning) with an added 今-like element for sound, describing gold
as the ore "within the earth."

Confidence is medium because the modern shape (人 over 王 over 丷) does not
transparently preserve that picture: 人 and 王 here are graphic
regularisations of the old earth-and-lid shape, not the semantically
unrelated characters "person" and "king," and the small dots (丷) are what
is left of the ore.

## Mnemonic
Someone 人 guarding a king's 王 treasure, marked with glittering flecks 丷:
gold.

## Notes
金 as a component marks metal broadly, not just gold — 铁 (iron), 银
(silver), 钱 (money) and dozens more all carry 钅 (its compressed
left-side form) as their semantic radical.
