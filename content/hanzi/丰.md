## Etymology
Pictographic. Shuowen reads it as grass or plants growing thick and lush
— 艸盛丰丰也 — built on 生 ("to grow, to be born") with the sense of
reaching both up and down at once (above ground and rooted below). Xu
Xuan's commentary adds a nice observation: luxuriant growth above
implies deep roots below, a natural correspondence.

Confidence is medium: 丰 is unrelated to, and now visually merged with,
an entirely different character 豐 (a ritual vessel piled high with
offerings, itself the historical ancestor of "abundant" in most modern
compounds) — simplification collapsed both into this one shape.

## Mnemonic
A stalk 丨, thick with growth on every side 三: lush, abundant.

## Notes
Because two originally separate characters (丰 "luxuriant growth" and 豐
"a full ritual vessel") were merged, every modern sense of "abundant,
plentiful" — 丰富, 丰收, 丰盛 — could descend from either ancestor; the
distinction is no longer recoverable from the shape alone.
