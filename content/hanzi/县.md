## Etymology
The traditional form 縣 pictured a head hung upside down from a rope or
tree (県, an inverted 首 "head") — the character's original meaning was
simply "to hang, to suspend," a sense later given its own dedicated
character, 懸/悬, with 心 added for clarity.

"County, administrative district" is a separate word that came to be
written with this same graph, most likely by phonetic loan — an
administrative unit was said to "hang" (be suspended, i.e. depend
directly) from the authority above it, though the exact mechanism of that
extension is not something this entry can confirm with confidence, hence
the low rating. The modern simplified 县 no longer preserves the hanging
head/rope image at all.

## Mnemonic
A district hanging administratively from the authority above it, the way
a head once hung from a rope in this character's oldest form: "县,"
county.

## Notes
悬/懸 (to hang, to suspend) is the character that split off to carry
縣's original literal meaning once 縣 itself became fully claimed by the
administrative sense.
