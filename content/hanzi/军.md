## Etymology
Ideographic, traditional 軍: Shuowen glosses 軍 as 圜圍也, "to encircle," and
gives the specific military unit size: 4,000 men to an army. The graph
combines 車 (chariot) with a reduced form of 包 (to encircle/wrap) —
chariots drawn up in an encircling formation, the picture of an
encampment. Not "soldiers under a distorted 力 in a cart," a folk gloss
with no source behind it.

## Mnemonic
Chariots 车 drawn into a ring 冖, encircling the camp.

## Notes
军 anchors 军队 "army/troops," 军人 "soldier," 海军/空军/陆军 "navy/air
force/army" — the whole modern military vocabulary descends from this one
ancient encampment picture.
