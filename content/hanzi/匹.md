## Etymology
Pictographic-leaning. Shuowen defines 匹 as a fixed unit of cloth — 四丈也,
"four zhang [of woven cloth]" — and the graph is generally read as a
picture of a folded bolt of fabric. "A pair, a match" (匹配 "to match,"
匹敌 "to be a match for") likely grew from cloth being measured and matched
in standard bolt-lengths; the classifier use for horses is a separate,
later development whose link to the cloth sense is not clearly documented.

## Mnemonic
A bolt of cloth folded neatly to a standard length — one unit, matched to
the next.

## Notes
匹 today survives mainly as the classifier for horses and mules (一匹马) and
in 匹配/匹敌 "to match, to be equal to." The literal cloth-unit sense has
fallen out of everyday use.
