## Etymology
Shuowen reads 美 at face value as 从羊从大 — "sheep" over "big" — and
explains it through cuisine: sheep were the meat animal that mattered most
for feasts, so "big sheep" came to mean "good-tasting," then "beautiful."
This is the famous "羊大为美" (big sheep is beauty) story, and it is the
etymology most often repeated.

But the oracle-bone form does not show a sheep and a big person at all — it
shows a standing person wearing a tall feather or horned headdress, i.e. a
dancer in ceremonial dress. The "sheep + big" reading is a later
reinterpretation of a shape whose original picture had already become
illegible by Xu Shen's time.

Confidence is low because the two accounts genuinely conflict and neither
this app nor Shuowen can adjudicate which one explains the character on the
card. Treat "sheep, big" as what the written form now literally shows,
and the headdress story as the likelier original picture.

## Mnemonic
A big 大 sheep 羊 — the fattest one at the feast is beautiful.

## Notes
A clean example of why "every component is a picture" fails: the
component-level story you can read off the modern glyph (羊+大) is not
the same as what the earliest attested forms depict.
