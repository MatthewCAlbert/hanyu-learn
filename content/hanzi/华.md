## Etymology
The traditional 華 was built on 艸 (plant) with 𠌶, a pictograph of a
flowering plant in full bloom — Shuowen's gloss, 榮也, "to flourish, to
bloom," is the direct picture: a plant at the peak of its flowering.

Simplification replaced the whole thing with the unrelated shapes 化 and
十, a cursive-derived graphic substitution with no recoverable link to
either "plant" or "bloom." Confidence is low, and no component is
declared, because the modern shape cannot be read back into the
traditional picture.

## Mnemonic
Change 化 that reaches its full flowering 十: splendour, in full bloom.

## Notes
"A plant in full bloom" generalised into "splendid, illustrious" (华丽,
豪华) and, by a further step tied to the ancient self-name of the
Chinese civilisation (华夏), into "Chinese" itself — 中华, 华人, 华语.
