## Etymology
Shuowen reads 每 as pictophonetic: 屮 (sprouting grass) for meaning, 母 *mǔ*
for sound, giving "grass growing thick and tall." But the oracle-bone and
bronze forms actually picture a woman with an elaborate headdress, not
grass — the "grass" reading looks like a Han-dynasty reinterpretation of an
already-worn-down shape.

The app's decomposition data cannot resolve the top component at all (it is
recorded as unknown), which mirrors this genuine disagreement between what
Shuowen describes and what the older script shows. Only 母 is declared here,
as the one piece every source agrees on.

## Mnemonic
Every mother 母 has her own habits: "each, every."

## Notes
"Grass growing" and "each, every" look unrelated because they likely are:
"each, every" is probably a phonetic loan onto a character whose original
meaning (headdress ornament, later grass) had already stopped being used by
the time of the classical texts that give us "every."
