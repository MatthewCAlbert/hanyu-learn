## Etymology
Pictophonetic: Shuowen's entry for this word uses 金 (metal) as the
semantic component — 鍊，冶金也，"to smelt metal" — while the modern
character 炼 instead pairs the same phonetic 柬 *jiǎn* with 火 (fire), a
closely related but distinct semantic choice (fire being the tool of
smelting, metal being its object). Given that the standard modern
character is built on a different, though sensible, semantic component
than the one Shuowen records, this entry keeps confidence at medium.

"To smelt (metal) with fire" generalizes to "to refine, to temper"
anything through repeated intense process — 锻炼 ("to forge/temper," now
"to exercise"), 炼字 ("to polish one's word choice," in writing).

## Mnemonic
Fire 火 selecting and refining 柬 (to select) only the purest metal from
the ore: "炼," to refine.

## Notes
锻炼 ("to exercise, to work out," lit. "to forge and refine") is by far
the most common everyday compound — the body being "smelted" into better
shape, the same metaphor as English "to forge" a strong physique.
