## Etymology
Ideographic per Shuowen: two hands (廾) gripping an axe (斤) — a picture of
armed strength, "the look of joined force." The modern top shape (丘-like)
is a later graphic flattening of that two-hands-plus-axe image, not a real
reference to a hill.

"Weapon" (Shuowen's own primary sense, 械) generalized metonymically to
"the one who wields the weapon" — a soldier.

## Mnemonic
An axe gripped in both hands, held ready: "兵," a soldier armed for war.

## Notes
Do not read the top of 兵 as "丘" (hill) — it is a worn-down version of two
hands holding an axe, one more case (like 未, 雷) where the modern shape's
resemblance to an unrelated common component is coincidental.
