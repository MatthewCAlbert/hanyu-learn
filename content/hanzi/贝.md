## Etymology
A pictograph, per Shuowen: the character draws a cowrie shell — Shuowen
calls it directly "a sea creature with a shell." Cowrie shells served as
an early form of currency in ancient China, which is why 贝 became the
semantic component running through nearly every character to do with
money and valuables.

## Mnemonic
The ridged outline of a cowrie shell, once carried as coin: "贝," shell —
and, by extension, money.

## Notes
贝 is the semantic core of a whole family of money-related characters:
财 (wealth), 购 (to purchase), 货 (goods), 贵 (expensive), and 贫 (poor) —
all traced back to shells once used as currency.
