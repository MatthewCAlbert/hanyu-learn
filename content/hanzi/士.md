## Etymology
Shuowen records a numerological gloss attributed to Confucius: numbers
run from one 一 to ten 十, and a "士" is one who can "extend the ten back
to the one" — someone capable of grasping the whole range of affairs.
Many modern paleographers instead read the oracle-bone shape as a small
ceremonial axe, similar in spirit to (though visually distinct from) the
axe behind 王 — a badge of a capable, armed man of standing. The two
accounts do not fully agree, so this entry keeps confidence medium.

Either way, 士 settled into meaning a capable man of a particular class —
originally a low-ranking noble or soldier, later a scholar or a person
skilled in a trade.

## Mnemonic
A small axe held ready, marking its bearer as a person of standing:
"士," a gentleman or scholar.

## Notes
士 survives today mostly as a professional-title suffix: 战士 (soldier),
博士 (doctor/PhD), 护士 (nurse) — each naming someone accomplished in
their particular field.
