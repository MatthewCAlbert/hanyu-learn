## Etymology
Shuowen: 番 is 采 (to distinguish; claw marks) over 田, the pad of an animal's foot: a paw print, read as an animal's foot or track. All later uses — "to take turns", "occurrence, time", "foreign" — are borrowings. The picture of "sowing rice in a field" is a modern misreading of the shape.

## Mnemonic
A clawed paw 采 pressing a pad 田 into the mud: a print.

## Notes
番 as a counter: 一番 "a round", 三番五次 "again and again". 番茄 "tomato" (the foreign eggplant), 番薯 "sweet potato", 番号 "number". 翻 *fān* and 蕃 *fān* use it as sound.
