## Etymology
Pictophonetic in the traditional form 帥: 巾 (cloth) supplies the meaning —
originally a sash or cloth worn at the waist — and 𠂤 supplied the sound.

**The sound clue is gone in the simplified form.** Simplification replaced
𠂤 with the similar-looking 刂 (knife radical), which is neither semantic
nor phonetic here — a shape substitution only. So 帅 keeps its "cloth"
component but gives no hint toward *shuài*.

"Commander-in-chief" grew from that waist-sash: a ceremonial sash marked
rank, and the highest-ranked sash-wearer led the army. "Handsome, dashing"
(帅哥) is a much later, informal extension — a sharp-looking commander lending
his word to anyone sharp-looking.

## Mnemonic
A cloth sash 巾 cut sharp as a blade 刂: the commander's mark of rank — and,
these days, just "帅," good-looking.

## Notes
The "handsome" sense is colloquial and relatively recent; formal writing
about military rank uses 帅 only in its original commander sense (元帅,
统帅).
