## Etymology
着 is a vernacular reduction of 著 — originally ⺿ "grass" over 者 *zhě* for
sound, meaning "conspicuous, to manifest, to attach." All the modern senses of
着 grow from "to come into contact / attach": *zhuó* "to wear, to apply,"
*zháo* "to touch, to catch (fire, cold, sleep)," *zhāo* "a move (in chess),"
and the toneless durative particle *zhe* (坐着 "sitting").

The 羊-over-目 shape of 着 is a graphic accident of cursive reduction, not a
picture of anything.

## Mnemonic
Something that sticks fast — and stays that way (…着).

## Notes
著 keeps the sense "to write / a work" (著作, 名著) and "marked" (显著). Learn
着's four readings by the contexts above.
