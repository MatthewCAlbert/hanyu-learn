## Etymology
The traditional form 殺 is old, and its internal composition is not settled —
oracle-bone and bronze forms show a weapon striking an animal or person,
consistent with Shuowen's gloss 戮也 "to put to death." But exactly how the
graph decomposes is debated across redactions of Shuowen itself.

The simplified 杀 is not a clean semantic-plus-phonetic split: 乂 and 木 in
today's shape are graphic residue left over from simplifying 殺, not
independently meaningful components. Best treated as a single sign rather than
decomposed for teaching purposes — do not read "wood" or "coffin" into it, as
some popular mnemonic lists do.

## Mnemonic
A cross-stroke 乂 cutting something down.

## Notes
Appears as the phonetic in 刹 (chà/shā, "brake"). The verb-suffix use — 笑杀
"die laughing," 气杀我 "furious" — is a separate, later grammaticalised sense
of "extremely," unrelated to the original weapon imagery.
