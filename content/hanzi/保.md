## Etymology
Ideographic in origin, though Shuowen already reads it as phonetic. The
oracle-bone and bronze forms are unambiguous: a person carrying a child on
their back, one hand reaching around to steady it — a picture of nurture
and protection. Shuowen's gloss, 養也 "to raise, to nurture," is exactly
that image put into words, though by Xu Shen's time the seal-script form
had already regularised enough that he analyses it as a person with a
reduced phonetic rather than describing the child directly.

## Mnemonic
A person 亻 holding something precious close, guarding it with care.

## Notes
From "to carry and nurture a child" the meaning generalised fully to "to
protect, to guarantee, to keep safe" — 保护, 保证, 保险 — with the original
child long gone from the picture.
