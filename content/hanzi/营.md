## Etymology
The traditional 營 was pictophonetic: 宮 (a dwelling — two connected
rooms) supplied the meaning; an abbreviated 熒 (a glow, related to fire)
supplied the sound. Shuowen's gloss, 市居也, "market dwellings," pictures
a cluster of buildings where people gather to trade and live — which
generalised into "an army camp" and, separately, "to manage, to run [an
operation]."

Simplification compressed both halves heavily, leaving 艹, 冖 and 吕 with
no cleanly separable meaning or sound clue. Confidence is low, and no
component is declared, because the modern shape cannot be read back into
either 宮 or 熒 directly.

## Mnemonic
Rooms 吕, sheltered under one roof 冖, glowing 艹 with activity: a camp,
a busy operation.

## Notes
营 covers the concrete "camp" (夏令营 "summer camp," 野营 "camping") and
the abstract "to manage, to operate" (经营, 营业 "to be open for
business," 营养 "nutrition" — literally "to manage/build up the body")
— both descend from the same image of a bustling settlement.
