## Etymology
Ideographic. 礻 (altar) with a person whose mouth is open — Shuowen glosses
祝 as 祭主贊詞者, "the one who speaks the praise-words at a sacrifice": a
priest or shaman addressing the spirits on the community's behalf. The
oracle-bone form is vivid: a kneeling figure, mouth open, bowing before a
deity.

## Mnemonic
At the altar 礻, a person's 兄 words go up to the heavens: a blessing.

## Notes
祝 shifted from "the person who prays on your behalf" to "the act of
wishing someone well" — 祝你生日快乐, 祝贺, 祝福 — the officiant has
disappeared from the meaning, leaving only the well-wish.
