## Etymology
Pictophonetic, traditional 嚇: 口 (mouth) supplies the meaning, 下 *xià*
supplies the sound and reads unchanged in the *xià* pronunciation. Shuowen
records an old sense tied to vocal expression through the mouth; the dominant
modern meaning "to scare, to frighten" is a later development that has come
to eclipse the classical gloss, which is why the exact semantic path from
Shuowen's entry to today's "scare" sense is not fully traceable.

## Mnemonic
A mouth 口 crying out when startled from below 下.

## Notes
Two readings, two functions: *xià* "to scare" (吓人, 吓一跳) is the verb;
*hè* is the interjection of surprise or scorn (吓!). Learners should not
confuse this simplified 吓 with 下 alone — the 口 is what turns "down" into
"a startled cry."
