## Etymology
Pictophonetic, traditional 榮: Shuowen's own gloss is architectural — "the
two upturned ends of a roof's eaves" — with 木 (wood) for meaning and a
reduced form of 熌 for sound. The now-common reading of the simplified 荣
as "grass and trees flourishing in a garden" is a plausible-sounding
modern gloss that does not match Shuowen's actual account, so it is noted
here as a popular reading rather than the documented etymology. "Glory,
to flourish" is the sense that dominates today regardless of which
picture originally produced it.

## Mnemonic
Trees 木 growing tall and lush, a whole household's pride.

## Notes
荣 anchors 光荣 "glory, honour," 荣誉 "honour, distinction," and 繁荣
"prosperous, flourishing" — nearly always figurative in modern use, the
architectural origin long forgotten.
