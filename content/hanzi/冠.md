## Etymology
Ideographic. Shuowen glosses 冠 as the general term for ceremonial
headwear that binds up the hair, and explains the graph as 元 (a person's
head) under 冖 (a covering), with 寸 (a hand, marking regulation) added
because headwear of this kind followed strict sumptuary rules about rank.

## Mnemonic
A hand 寸 fitting a cap 冖 onto a head 元, exactly by the rules.

## Notes
冠 covers the concrete (皇冠 "crown," 冠军 "champion" — literally "the crown
[winner]") and, in classical contexts, the ritual coming-of-age headwear
男子二十弱冠 "a young man capped at twenty." 冠军, 亚军, 季军 (1st, 2nd, 3rd
place) are everyday sports vocabulary today.
