## Etymology
Pictophonetic. 女 (woman) supplies the meaning; 古 *gǔ* supplies the
sound. Shuowen's original sense, 夫母也, is specifically "husband's
mother" — a mother-in-law — the traditional kinship-role sense that
later broadened to include "father's sister" as well.

## Mnemonic
An older 古 woman 女 on the husband's side of the family: an aunt, a
mother-in-law.

## Notes
姑 keeps two live kinship senses today — 姑姑 "paternal aunt" and, in
more formal or classical registers, "mother-in-law" — both descend from
the same "an older female in-law" root.
