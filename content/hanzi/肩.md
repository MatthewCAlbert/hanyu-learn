## Etymology
Pictographic/ideographic. Shuowen glosses 肩 simply as 髆也, "the
shoulder-blade," and marks the graph 象形, "a picture" — the top shape
(resembling 户) traces the shoulder-blade's outline, sitting over ⺼
(flesh/body-part radical). It is not a picture of "a man bringing home
meat," a folk gloss that circulates online but has no source behind it.

## Mnemonic
The curved outline of a shoulder-blade 户 set into the body ⺼.

## Notes
肩 covers the body part (肩膀 "shoulder") and the figurative "to shoulder [a
burden]" (身肩重任 "to bear heavy responsibility") — the same metaphor
English uses for "shouldering" a duty.
