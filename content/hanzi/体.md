## Etymology
The character on the card is not the historical "body" graph. That is 體 —
骨 "bone" for meaning, 豊 *lǐ* for sound, glossed by Shuowen as "the whole of
the body's twelve members."

体 is a much older, unrelated simple graph — 亻 "person" + 本 "root / coarse"
— that meant "crude, inferior" (read *bèn*). Simplification adopted this
ready-made shape to stand in for 體 purely because it was easy to write. So
the modern 体 gives you neither the meaning nor the sound of "body"; it is a
borrowed form.

## Mnemonic
A person 亻 at their most basic 本: the body.

## Notes
A clean example of simplification by shape-borrowing rather than reduction —
compare 过 (咼 lost), 邓 (鄧). The 豊 phonetic of 體 also writes 礼 / 禮.
