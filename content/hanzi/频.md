## Etymology
Ideographic, traditional 頻: Shuowen describes someone standing at a
river's edge, repeatedly frowning and hesitating before wading across —
built from 涉 (to wade across water) and 頁 (a head, showing the frowning
face). That image of repeated hesitation at a threshold generalised into
"repeatedly, again and again, frequency." The modern simplified 频 keeps
页 (head) alongside a reduced trace of the "wading/stepping" component.

## Mnemonic
A face 页, frowning again and again, step 步 by step, at the water's edge.

## Notes
频 anchors 频率 "frequency," 频繁 "frequent," and 频道 "channel [TV/radio]"
— the last a modern extension from "frequency" in the physics sense to
the broadcast band it's tuned to.
