## Etymology
Ideographic. 命 and 令 were the same character in oracle-bone script — a
person kneeling to receive an order, with an inverted mouth above them
issuing it. In Western Zhou bronze script, 口 (mouth) was added to 令 to
create a distinct 命, marking specifically the *spoken* act of commanding.
Shuowen's commentary makes the theological extension explicit: 命者，天之
令也, "命 is Heaven's command" — a person's fate is what Heaven has
ordered for them.

## Mnemonic
A mouth 口 issuing an order 令 from on high: a command, and — from
Heaven — one's fate.

## Notes
命 carries both "an order" (命令) and "life, fate" (生命, 命运) because
one's lifespan was traditionally understood as a decree handed down, not
a thing one controls.
