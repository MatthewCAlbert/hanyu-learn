## Etymology
Indicative per Shuowen: 又 (a hand) with 一 (a single mark) placed one
inch below the wrist — the exact spot where a pulse is taken, still
called 寸口 in traditional Chinese medicine today. The unit "an inch" is
named directly after this specific point on the body.

## Mnemonic
A hand 又 with a single mark 一 measured one small unit below the wrist,
right where the pulse beats: "寸," an inch.

## Notes
寸 also appears as a component (not a full radical of its own everywhere,
but a recurring signal for "a hand's small, measured action") in
characters like 对, 寺, 得 — worth watching for as a recurring shape
across the script.
