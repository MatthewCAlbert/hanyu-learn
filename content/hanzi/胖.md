## Etymology
Pictophonetic, with the phonetic also carrying meaning (亦聲). ⺼ (flesh)
supplies the meaning; 半 *bàn* "half" supplies the sound and, per
Shuowen, the sense too: 半體肉也, "half a sacrificial animal's carcass" —
the ritual portion given to a guest of honour. "Large, substantial" is
the natural next step from "a generous half-portion of meat."

## Mnemonic
A half 半 side of meat 月 — plenty to go around, and plenty to carry.

## Notes
Two readings split old and new senses cleanly: *pán*, in classical
phrases meaning "at ease, contented" (心广体胖), versus the everyday
*pàng* "fat, plump" — both trace back to the same "generous portion"
image.
