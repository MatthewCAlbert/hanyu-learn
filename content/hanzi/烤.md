## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 火 (fire) supplies the meaning, 考 *kǎo* supplies the sound
and reads unchanged. 烤 is a strikingly modern character — it is absent
from classical dictionaries, and is often said to have entered wide written
use only in the early 20th century as Beijing roast-meat vendors (most
famously for 烤鸭, roast duck) needed a standard character for the cooking
method their signs advertised. That popular account is well known but not
independently documented here with a primary source, so treat it as likely
rather than settled.

## Mnemonic
Fire 火 testing 考 the meat until it's done.

## Notes
烤 is now completely standard for any dry-heat cooking over or near fire:
烤鸭 "roast duck," 烤箱 "oven," 烧烤 "barbecue." Its recent, practical origin
is a useful contrast to characters whose shape goes back to the oracle
bones.
