## Etymology
Pictographic — an infant: a large head at the top, two arms out to the sides,
and the legs wrapped together below (contrast 了, the same figure with the arms
tucked). Shuowen ties it to the eleventh month, when "the yang stirs and all
things multiply."

## Mnemonic
Big head, arms out, legs swaddled: a baby.

## Notes
子 is Kangxi radical 39. Uses: son / child (儿子, 子女), the first earthly
branch (子时), a master / philosopher (孔子, 老子), and the toneless noun
suffix (桌子, 孩子).
