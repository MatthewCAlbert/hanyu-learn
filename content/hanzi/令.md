## Etymology
Ideographic. Shuowen glosses 令 as 發號也 "to issue a command." The graph shows
a kneeling figure (the top, related to 人) beneath a tally or seal 卪 — a
person receiving, or a mouth issuing, an order. The composition is agreed on
in outline; which half is "issuing" and which is "receiving" is where accounts
differ, so the interpretation is medium rather than high confidence.

## Mnemonic
A person kneeling to receive a sealed command 卪.

## Notes
Three readings split the senses: *lìng* "order, cause to" (命令, 令人); *lǐng*
in the loanword 令 (a unit, rare); *líng* in 口令 "password/drill call" and the
old poetic-form sense (词令 lyric metre). As phonetic in 领 lǐng, 冷 lěng, 铃
líng — a productive series where 令 carries sound across several finals.
