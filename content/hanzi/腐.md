## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 肉 (meat, flesh) supplies the meaning — Shuowen's
definition is simply "rotten" — and 府 *fǔ* (covered elsewhere in this
app) supplies the sound.

"Meat gone bad" generalizes to "to rot, to decay" of anything (腐烂) and,
figuratively, to moral corruption (腐败, "corrupt," lit. "rotten and
defeated") — decay applied to institutions and character, not just food.

## Mnemonic
Meat 肉 left too long in the storehouse 府, gone bad: "腐," rotten.

## Notes
豆腐 (tofu, lit. "bean-curd," using 腐 in its older, gentler sense of
"curdled/fermented" rather than "rotten") and 腐败 ("corrupt," of an
official or institution) show the same character spanning food and
morality.
