## Etymology
呆 has a split history. In Shuowen it appears as an old variant form of 保
"to protect" — a person 人 protecting a child 木-like shape — but that is not
the source of today's meaning "foolish, blank." The "foolish" sense is a
later, separate colloquial development, popularly analysed as 口 (mouth) over
木 (wood) — a mouth gone as still and unresponsive as a block of wood — but
that reading is a folk gloss rather than a sourced etymology, so confidence
stays low.

## Mnemonic
A mouth 口 struck as silent as a block of wood 木.

## Notes
呆 describes a blank, unresponsive kind of foolish (呆住了 "frozen/stunned,"
发呆 "to zone out"), distinct from 傻's endearing foolishness and 笨's
clumsy slowness. 呆 also survives in 呆板 "rigid, inflexible" for things, not
just people.
