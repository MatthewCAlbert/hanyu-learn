## Etymology
Ideographic. Shuowen's entry is under 亯 (the ancestor of both 享 and 亨),
glossed 獻也, "to offer" — 象進孰物形, "picturing cooked food being
presented," most likely the shape of a temple or altar building where such
offerings were made. That is not "children eating in a house," a folk
gloss with no source; the picture is a place or act of ceremonial
offering, and "to enjoy [the benefit of]" is the sense that survived on
this particular graph once 亨 (success/smooth progress) and 烹 (to cook)
split off to carry the others.

## Mnemonic
A hall 亠 where offerings 口/子-shapes were laid out and, eventually, enjoyed.

## Notes
享 today means "to enjoy": 享受 "to enjoy," 分享 "to share," 享有 "to enjoy
[a right/reputation]." Its close relatives 亨 (as in the name 亨利 "Henry")
and 烹 "to cook" show how one ancestral picture split into three distinct
modern characters.
