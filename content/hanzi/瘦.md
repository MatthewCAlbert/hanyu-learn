## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 疒 (sickness/affliction) supplies the meaning, 叟 *sǒu*
(old man) supplies the sound, shifted to *shòu*. Early dictionaries gloss it
as 臞也, "emaciated" — thinness was classed alongside illness, not as a
neutral or desirable body state, which is why 疒 sits over what looks like an
unrelated "old man" component.

## Mnemonic
An affliction 疒 that leaves even an old man 叟 thin as a reed.

## Notes
瘦 is the standard antonym of 胖 pàng "fat/plump" and, unlike English "thin,"
carries no automatic positive spin — 太瘦了 can be a concern, not a
compliment. Also used for lean meat (瘦肉) and tight-fitting clothes (瘦身
"slimming," of clothing or diet plans).
