## Etymology
Pictophonetic, traditional 駕: 马 (horse) supplies the meaning, 加 *jiā*
supplies the sound, shifted to *jià*. Shuowen's gloss describes a horse
fitted into its yoke — the literal act of harnessing an animal to a cart —
which generalised to "to drive, to pilot" any vehicle once carts gave way
to cars and planes.

## Mnemonic
A horse 马 harnessed on 加, ready to pull.

## Notes
驾 covers driving (驾驶 "to drive," 驾照 "driver's license") and the
honorific register: 劳驾 "excuse me, may I trouble you" and 大驾光临 "your
esteemed presence" both use 驾 as a polite stand-in for "you," a leftover
of addressing someone by their carriage rather than directly.
