## Etymology
The word "not to have" was originally written 無, a pictograph of a person
dancing with tasseled sleeves or ornaments (its real, unrelated original
meaning was "to dance" — 舞 later split off to carry that sense with its own
extra strokes). 無 came to mean "none/not have" purely by phonetic loan: it
sounded like the negation word, so it was borrowed to write it, and the
dance meaning was abandoned to the new character 舞.

无 is not a modern simplification of 無 — Shuowen already records it as an
"unusual form" (奇字) of 無 in the Han dynasty, so both forms are ancient. The
People's Republic simply promoted the simpler old variant to standard status.
Because the whole character is a borrowed sound-alike with no surviving
semantic or phonetic parts worth naming, there is nothing safely decomposable
here.

## Mnemonic
Nothing under the roof: 无, empty-handed.

## Notes
Common in set expressions and somewhat more formal than colloquial 没有:
无所谓 "doesn't matter," 无论 "no matter (what/how)," 无聊 "bored."
