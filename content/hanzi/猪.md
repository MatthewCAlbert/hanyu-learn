## Etymology
Pictophonetic. The animal-meaning component was originally 豕 (pig,
itself a pictograph); modern 猪 substitutes the general animal radical 犭
in its place, keeping 者 *zhě* as the sound. Shuowen's original gloss is
oddly specific — a pig marked by clusters of three bristles — a detail
level typical of how carefully agricultural terms were once distinguished.

## Mnemonic
An animal 犭 that eats whatever 者 comes its way: a pig.

## Notes
Modern Chinese keeps 猪 (the animal, with 犭) separate from 豕, which
survives only as a component in other characters (家, 逐) — a case where
the "generic animal" radical replaced an animal-specific pictograph once
that pictograph stopped being used as a freestanding character.
