## Etymology
Pictophonetic, with a component that looks misleading today: the left side
is 冫 ("ice," two strokes), but here it functions as an old abbreviated form
of 水/氵 ("water"), not as a reference to ice — this character has meant
"water surging" since Shuowen's 沖, long before 冲 became its standard
written form. 中 *zhōng* supplies the sound.

"To rush/dash against" (water) extended naturally to "to mix with water"
(冲茶, to infuse tea) and, figuratively, "to clash, to collide" (冲突).

## Mnemonic
Water pouring straight through the middle 中: it "冲"es forward, rushing.

## Notes
Do not confuse 冲's 冫 with the genuinely ice-meaning 冫 in 冷 (cold) or 冰
(ice) — same two strokes, different job depending on the character.
