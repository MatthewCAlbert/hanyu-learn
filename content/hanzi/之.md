## Etymology
之 began as a pictograph of a foot (止) standing on a baseline (一, the
ground), meaning "to go to" — the oracle-bone form shows a foot pointed away
from the line it stands on. Shuowen already reads it as growth "going out"
past a boundary, a slightly later gloss on the same "moving beyond a fixed
point" image.

The verb "to go" faded from common use and 之 was repurposed by grammatical
extension as a literary possessive/linking particle (equivalent to 的) and as
a third-person object pronoun — both senses built on the same idea of "moving
toward" something, now abstracted into "belonging to" or "referring to" it.

## Mnemonic
A foot stepping off a line: 之 moves *toward* something — toward a place in
its old verb sense, toward a noun it modifies in its particle sense.

## Notes
之 is common only in set phrases and classical/literary registers in modern
Mandarin (总之, 之前, 之后, 三分之一) — the everyday possessive job is done by
的 instead.
