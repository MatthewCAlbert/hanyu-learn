## Etymology
Ideographic. The oracle-bone form shows three 人 (person) figures under
一 or 日 — three or more people together standing for "a crowd, a
multitude," since in the counting logic of the script three of anything
represents "many." Shuowen's own seal-script analysis instead reads
目 (eye) over 乑 (people); either way, every attested form keeps the
core idea of multiple people grouped together. The modern simplified 众
returns most directly to the oldest picture: three 人 stacked plainly.

## Mnemonic
Three people 人人人, standing together: a crowd.

## Notes
众 is the crowd's-eye view companion to 人 (a person) and 从 (two people,
one following the other) — a small, elegant series where the count of
repeated 人 tracks the size of the group being described.
