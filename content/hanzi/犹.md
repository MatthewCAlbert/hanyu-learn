## Etymology
Pictophonetic, traditional 猶: 犭 (animal/dog) supplies the meaning — the
oldest recorded sense names a monkey-like creature, or a dog that follows
closely — and 酋 *qiú* supplied the sound in the traditional form.
Simplification replaced 酋 with 尤, so today's visible right-hand side is
a graphic stand-in rather than the original phonetic. "Just like, similar
to" (犹如) grew from the "closely following/resembling" animal-behaviour
sense.

## Mnemonic
An animal 犭 that follows so closely 尤 it might as well be the same.

## Notes
犹 today is almost entirely grammatical: 犹如 "just like, as if," 犹豫 "to
hesitate" (a disyllabic word, not decomposable into "monkey + hesitant"),
仍犹 "still" in literary registers.
