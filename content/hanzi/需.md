## Etymology
Shuowen reads 需 as pictophonetic: 雨 (rain) for meaning, 而 *ér* for
sound, glossing it as "to wait" — specifically, to be caught by rain and
have to stop and wait it out.

The oracle-bone form suggests a different original picture: a person
standing in rain, being drenched — the character was likely the original
form of 濡 "to soak, to moisten," with the human figure at the bottom
later mistaken for 而 in the small-seal script. Confidence is medium
because that reading, if correct, means Shuowen's own phonetic component
is a scribal misreading of a person, not a sound clue at all.

## Mnemonic
Caught in the rain 雨, you have no choice 而 but to wait it out — or to
need shelter.

## Notes
"Forced to wait [for the rain to stop]" generalised into the modern "to
need, to require" — 需要, 需求 — the same shift many languages make from
"waiting for" to "wanting."
