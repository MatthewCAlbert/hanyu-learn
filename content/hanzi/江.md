## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 氵 (water) supplies the meaning, and 工 *gōng* supplies the
sound. Shuowen's definition names a specific river — what is today the
Yangtze — before the word generalized to "river" broadly.

That specific origin explains a real regional split still visible today:
江 generalized mainly for rivers in the south (长江, the Yangtze), while
河 (originally the proper name of the Yellow River) generalized for
northern rivers (黄河) — the same story, in parallel, for both of China's
two great rivers.

## Mnemonic
Water 氵 shaped by the work 工 of a great river cutting its course: "江,"
a (southern) river.

## Notes
江 and 河 are both generic "river" words today, but their names betray
their origin: 江 traces back to the Yangtze, 河 to the Yellow River —
south and north, respectively.
