## Etymology
Pictophonetic, traditional 槍: 木 (wood) supplies the meaning, 仓 (traditional
倉) supplies the sound. The original weapon was a wooden-shafted spear —
hence the wood radical — and the word was later extended to the metal
firearm (枪 "gun") once one was invented, the way English "gun" outlived any
connection to its own origins.

## Mnemonic
A wooden 木 shaft stored 仓 in the armory, spear first, gun later.

## Notes
Shares 仓 with 抢 (qiǎng, to grab) and 疮/创 (chuāng/chuàng, wound/create) — a
phonetic series spanning several unrelated meanings, all on the same sound.
枪 keeps both senses alive today: 长枪 "spear" in historical contexts, 手枪
"pistol" in modern ones.
