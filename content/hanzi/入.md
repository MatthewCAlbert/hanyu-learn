## Etymology
A pictograph, but of what is disputed — Shuowen sees "coming down together
from above"; others read a plant-root tip pushing into the soil, or the mouth
of an opening. The meaning "to go in, to enter" has been stable since the
oracle bones.

## Mnemonic
A wedge point angled downward, driving in: to enter.

## Notes
It is its own radical (no. 11). Keep it apart from 人 "person" (the legs meet
at the top in 入, and the left stroke starts higher). Senses: enter (进入,
入口, 入学), income (收入), join (加入, 入会), conform to (入时, 入乡随俗).
