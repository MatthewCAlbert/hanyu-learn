## Etymology
Ideographic. The oracle-bone form shows two people, one upright and one
inverted — a picture of turning, transformation, or things being flipped
on their head. By clerical script the inverted person had flattened into
the unrelated-looking shape 匕. Duan Yucai's commentary reads the
Shuowen gloss (教行也, "teaching put into practice") as an extension: when
those above set an example (匕) and those below follow it, that spreading
influence is called 化.

## Mnemonic
One person standing 亻, one person upside down 匕: something has changed.

## Notes
化 as a suffix is extremely productive in modern Chinese — 现代化
"modernisation," 消化 "to digest," 转化 "to convert" — always carrying
the core sense of one state turning into another.
