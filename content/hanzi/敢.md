## Etymology
Ideographic in origin: the oracle-bone form shows hands gripping a weapon,
facing down a wild boar — hunting a dangerous animal at close range, hence
"to be bold enough to." Shuowen, working only from the already-worn seal
script, glosses it simply as 進取, "to advance and take the initiative,"
without recovering the hunting scene.

Confidence is medium because the modern components (耳 and 攵) are a further
graphic simplification that no longer shows hands, weapon, or boar
distinctly — 攵 plausibly continues "a hand with a tool," but 耳 has no
established link to any part of the story, so neither is declared as a
checkable semantic or phonetic piece.

## Mnemonic
Ears 耳 pricked up, weapon 攵 in hand, facing the boar: it takes courage.

## Notes
Now purely an auxiliary verb of courage/permission — 敢不敢, 不敢当 — with
no trace of the hunt left in ordinary use.
