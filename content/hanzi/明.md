## Etymology
Ideographic: 日 (sun) beside 月 (moon), the two bright things, giving "bright,
clear." Both components contribute meaning; neither is phonetic.

One caveat worth knowing: the Shuowen analyses the older form as 从月从囧 —
moon plus 囧, a window — so the sense was light through a window rather than
sun-and-moon. The 日 form is later. The modern reading is sound; the ancient
one was different.

## Mnemonic
Sun 日 and moon 月 side by side: the two bright things.

## Notes
Extends naturally to 明天 (tomorrow — the next dawn) and 明白 (to understand —
for something to become clear).
