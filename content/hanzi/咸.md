## Etymology
Ideographic per Shuowen: 口 (mouths) with 戌 (a component Shuowen glosses
directly as 悉, "entirely, all") — many mouths speaking together, all of
one accord. Shuowen's own attested meaning is "all, entirely" — a
classical adverb, not a taste word at all.

**The "salty" sense is a separate, likely unrelated development.** How a
word meaning "all, entirely" came to also mean "salty" is not something
this entry can confirm — the two are conventionally written with the
same character in simplified usage, but this entry does not assert a
bridge between them, hence medium confidence.

## Mnemonic
Many mouths 口 speaking as one, entirely 戌 in agreement: "咸," all — and,
by a separate and less certain path, salty.

## Notes
咸 in the "salty" sense is common in cooking vocabulary (咸的, "salty,"
contrasted with 甜, sweet); the classical "all, entirely" sense survives
mainly in literary and historical texts.
