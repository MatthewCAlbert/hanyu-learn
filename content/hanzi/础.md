## Etymology
Pictophonetic. 石 (stone) supplies the meaning; the traditional phonetic
was 楚 *chǔ*. Shuowen's gloss, 礩也, is "a plinth" — the Guangyun spells
out the practical sense directly: 柱下石也, "the stone placed beneath a
pillar," the load-bearing base every column rests on.

Simplification replaced 楚 with the much simpler, identically-pronounced
出 *chū* — close enough in sound, far easier to write. Confidence is
medium because that substitution, while a clean simplification, is not
declared as the formal phonetic here since 出's own reading (chū) is a
looser match than most pairs in this dataset.

## Mnemonic
Stone 石, set down before the pillar even goes up 出: the foundation
stone.

## Notes
"The stone beneath a pillar" generalised fully into the abstract
"foundation, basis" — 基础 "foundation," almost always used
figuratively today, with no need for an actual pillar in sight.
