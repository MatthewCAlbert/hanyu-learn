## Etymology
The oracle-bone graph shows a person 人 with dots or short strokes down the
sides — a man wearing scale armour. The early meaning is "armour" (介胄), from
which come "to be shielded by," "to lie between two parties," and so "a
go-between" (介绍). Shuowen gives a different gloss, 畫也 "to demarcate,"
reading the side strokes as 八 "to divide."

Composition is clear; which of the two readings — armour, or a dividing line —
is primary is not settled, hence medium confidence.

## Mnemonic
A person 人 flanked by two plates of armour, standing between the sides.

## Notes
Phonetic in 界 *jiè* (boundary), 价 / 價 *jià* (price). The "between" sense
drives 介绍, 介入, 中介.
