## Etymology
亻 "person" + 也 *yě* for sound. Originally written 佗 or 它, the word meant
"another, some other person" — the sense preserved in 其他, 他人, 他乡. It then
became the general third-person pronoun. The graphs 她 "she" and 它 "it" were
split off from 他 only in the early 20th century.

## Mnemonic
The other 也 person 亻: him / her / that one.

## Notes
The 也 phonetic also writes 地 *dì*, 池 *chí*, 驰 / 馳 *chí*. All three of
他 / 她 / 它 are pronounced *tā* — the distinction is only written.
