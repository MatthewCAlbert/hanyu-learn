## Etymology
Pictographic, but the two traditional readings of the picture disagree.
Shuowen, working from the small-seal form, reads it as a bird flying down
from height to land on the ground (一, "the ground"). The oracle-bone form
more plausibly shows an arrow — 矢 upside down — striking the ground:
something shot from a distance arriving at its target.

Confidence is medium because Xu Shen's own analysis is now generally
thought to misread an arrow as a bird; both readings agree on the core
meaning, "to arrive, to reach," just not on what is pictured arriving.

## Mnemonic
An arrow 一, having flown its course, lands exactly here: it has arrived.

## Notes
"To arrive" generalised into "to the utmost, most" (至少 "at least," 甚至
"even, to the extent that") — reaching a place becomes reaching a limit.
