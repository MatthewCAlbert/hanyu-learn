## Etymology
Pictophonetic. 囗 (an enclosure) supplies the meaning; the traditional
phonetic was 韋 *wéi*. Shuowen's gloss, 守也, "to guard, to defend," fits
the oracle-bone and bronze forms, which show feet positioned around a
walled settlement — troops stationed to defend, and so also to encircle,
a city.

Simplification replaced 韋 with the much simpler 韦 (already itself a
simplified independent character). Confidence is medium: the semantic
enclosure 囗 is solid, but the phonetic-simplification chain (韋→韦) adds
a layer this dataset treats cautiously rather than declaring outright.

## Mnemonic
A wall 囗, guarded on every side 韦, ringing the whole city: to surround.

## Notes
"To guard [by surrounding]" split into the neutral "to surround, to
encircle" (周围, 范围) and the more hostile "to besiege" (包围) — guarding
a perimeter and trapping something inside it are the same action seen
from opposite sides.
