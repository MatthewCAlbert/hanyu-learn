## Etymology
A deliberate 20th-century coinage. Until the 1910s–20s, 他 covered "he, she,
it" alike. Writers translating from European languages wanted a distinct
"she," and 她 was proposed — 他 with 亻 replaced by 女 "female" — with 也
carrying over as the phonetic. It caught on within a decade.

## Mnemonic
The pronoun 也 marked as female 女: she.

## Notes
她 / 他 / 它 are all *tā*; the split is only on the page. For a group of mixed
gender, writing falls back to 他们. See also 它 (inanimate, likewise a modern
split).
