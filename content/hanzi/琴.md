## Etymology
Pictophonetic layered onto an older pictograph. Shuowen's entry is under
珡, marked 象形 "a picture" of the instrument itself — a hollow wooden body
strung with silk cords. 今 *jīn* was added later purely for sound, giving
today's 琴 *qín*, and the modern top (written 玨, resembling two jade
tablets) is a stylised trace of those original strings, not a jade
reference.

## Mnemonic
Two strings 玨 stretched over a soundbox, tuned to now 今.

## Notes
琴 names both the specific ancient zither (古琴 "guqin") and, generically,
keyboard and stringed instruments borrowed from elsewhere: 钢琴 "piano"
(literally "steel qin"), 小提琴 "violin" (literally "small hand-held qin").
