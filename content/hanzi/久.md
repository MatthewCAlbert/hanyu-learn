## Etymology
Not securely attested. Shuowen describes a figure with something pressed
against the back of the legs — often taken as the original of 灸
"moxibustion" — borrowed early for the unrelated sense "long in time." The
early graphs support no confident reading.

## Mnemonic
A back bent under a long, slow burden of time: a long while.

## Notes
Senses: for a long time (很久, 长久, 好久不见), of a stated duration
(三天之久, 多久), long-lasting (持久, 天长地久, 耐久). 久仰 "I have long
admired you" (a greeting).
