## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 囗 (an enclosure, the same frame seen in 圈, 国) supplies
the meaning "a closed, bounded shape," and 员 *yuán* (from 員, itself an
old pictograph of a round vessel's mouth) supplies the sound.

"Round, circular" (圆形) extends naturally to "complete, satisfactorily
whole" (圆满, "satisfactory," lit. "round and full") — roundness as a
symbol of completeness with no missing piece.

## Mnemonic
A boundary 囗 closed all the way around a member 员 standing at its
center: perfectly "圆," round.

## Notes
圆 and 园 (garden, also built on 员/元) are easily confused by shape but
unrelated in meaning — 园's enclosure holds plants, 圆's holds a shape.
