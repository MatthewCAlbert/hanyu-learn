## Etymology
The traditional form 臨 pictured a figure bending down (臥, a reclining
body) to look over several objects (品, three 口-shapes standing for
"multiple things") — someone overlooking or supervising from above.
Shuowen marks 品 as the phonetic, but simplification compressed the whole
picture down to a single vertical stroke plus a small right-hand
fragment, leaving nothing of the original bending figure or the
multiple-objects shape recoverable in the modern form — this entry does
not assert a decomposition for 临 and keeps confidence low.

"To look down over, to oversee" generalized to "to approach, to arrive
at" (临时, "temporary," lit. "arriving at the moment"; 面临, "to face,"
lit. "to arrive facing") — a formal, somewhat literary verb of arrival.

## Mnemonic
A figure bending low to look down over everything laid out below: "临,"
to oversee — or simply to arrive.

## Notes
面临 ("to face, to be confronted with") and 临时 ("temporary, for the time
being") are the two most common everyday compounds, both keeping this
character's formal, event-arriving register.
