## Etymology
**The "田" is not "field."** Oracle-bone and bronze forms show repeated
spiral or circle marks representing the rumble/roll of thunder, clustered
around a lightning-bolt shape (申, the same source as 电's own graph). As the
script squared off through seal and clerical script, those thunder-roll
marks flattened into the grid-like shape that now reads as 田. 雨 (rain) was
added afterward as a semantic classifier once thunder was grouped with other
weather characters — Shuowen already analyzes an old form of 雷 as
pictophonetic, 雨 for meaning and a component 畺 for sound, which is a
different reading again from the modern "rain over field" folk parse.

Because the modern "田" is a graphic descendant of the thunder-roll marks
rather than a real field, and not clearly the same shape Shuowen calls 畺,
this entry does not declare a phonetic component.

## Mnemonic
Rain 雨 rumbling down in echoing rolls over the fields 田: "雷," thunder.

## Notes
Compare 电 (diàn, lightning/electricity) — both descend from the same
ancient lightning-bolt pictograph (申), 雷 keeping the rain determiner and
电's simplified form dropping it.
