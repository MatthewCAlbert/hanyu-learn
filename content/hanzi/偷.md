## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 亻 (person) supplies the meaning, 俞 *yú* supplies the sound,
shifted to *tōu*. Shuowen's original gloss 苟且也 is closer to "careless,
slack, taking shortcuts" than to theft specifically; "to steal" is the sense
that won out in later usage, with the "cutting corners" flavour surviving in
words like 偷懒 "to shirk work" and 偷工减料 "to skimp on materials."

## Mnemonic
A person 亻 slipping past unnoticed 俞 — sneaking, whether stealing goods or
just skipping the work.

## Notes
The "shirk, sneak" family (偷懒, 偷看 "to peek," 偷偷 "stealthily/secretly") is
as productive in everyday Mandarin as the literal "to steal" sense (偷东西) —
both trace back to Shuowen's original "cutting corners," not to two separate
words.
