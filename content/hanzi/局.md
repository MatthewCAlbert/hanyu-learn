## Etymology
Ideographic per Shuowen: 口 (here read as a small enclosed space, not
"mouth") sitting under 尺 (a foot-rule, "measure") — something hemmed in and
measured, hence "cramped, confined" (促). That confined-space sense narrowed
in one direction to a bounded playing area (a chess/game "board," hence 局 as
the classifier for a match or round of a game) and broadened in another to
any bounded state of affairs — a "situation."

"Office, bureau" (邮局, 公安局) is a later administrative extension: an office
is itself a bounded, measured jurisdiction.

## Mnemonic
A space 口 boxed in and measured off by a ruler 尺: a tightly bounded
"situation" — or the bounded board a "round" of a game is played on.

## Notes
The classifier sense (一局棋 "a game of chess," 三局两胜 "best of three") and the
"situation" sense (局势, 局面) both derive from the same "bounded area" image;
"office/bureau" (局长, 邮局) is the administrative branch of that same idea.
