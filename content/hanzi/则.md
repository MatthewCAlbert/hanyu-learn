## Etymology
Ideographic per Shuowen: 贝 (cowrie shell — money, valuables in the archaic
economy) cut with 刂 (knife) — dividing goods into equal shares. That
"dividing into equal, fixed portions" sense is why 则 came to mean "standard,
rule, principle" (规则, 原则): a rule is a fixed measure everything is cut
against.

The conjunction use ("then," 一...则..., expressing a contrast or a
consequence) is a later grammatical extension of that same "fixed
correspondence" idea — each cause is matched to its consequence the way each
share was cut to its fixed size.

## Mnemonic
A knife 刂 cutting shells 贝 (money) into equal shares: a fair "rule" for
everyone, and — cut one way, "then" the other follows.

## Notes
As a bound noun, 则 mostly survives in compounds (规则, 原则, 准则); as a
conjunction it is more formal/written than 就 and typically pairs two
contrasting or sequential clauses.
