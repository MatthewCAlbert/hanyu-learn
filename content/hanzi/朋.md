## Etymology
Not two moons. The oracle-bone and bronze graphs show two hanging strings of
cowrie shells — 朋 was a unit for counting cowries, the early currency. From
"a matched pair of strings" came "a set, a group," and so "companions."
Shuowen instead links it to 鳳 (phoenix), imagining a flock of birds trailing
the phoenix; this is a later gloss, not the picture.

The modern 月月 shape is a flattening of the two shell-strings.

## Mnemonic
Two matched strings of shells, of equal worth: companions.

## Notes
朋 pairs with 友 in 朋友 — classically fellow-students (朋) versus
kindred-spirit companions (友), now merged.
