## Etymology
The traditional 親 was pictophonetic: 見 (to see) supplied the meaning;
the character's own right-hand component supplied the sound (Shuowen calls
it circularly 親聲, and bronze-script versions show it varying — 辛 in
Western Zhou, then a different phonetic by the Warring States). Xu Shen
glosses the meaning as 至也, "to arrive, to reach [a depth of feeling]" —
parents are 親 because the bond with them is what one's feelings most fully
reach.

Simplification dropped 見 (see) entirely, leaving only the old phonetic
component to stand for the whole word — the same pattern as 总, where the
meaning-bearing half was cut and the sound-bearing half survived alone. No
semantic or phonetic field is declared here because what remains, 亲, is
that phonetic fossil, not a fresh semantic-plus-phonetic pair.

## Mnemonic
Standing 立 close enough to see clearly, small 小 and near: family.

## Notes
Two readings split old and new senses: *qīn* "parent, relative, close" is
the common one; *qìng* survives mainly in 亲家 (qìngjia), the term for
one's child's in-laws.
