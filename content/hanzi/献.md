## Etymology
Pictophonetic, traditional 獻: 犬 (dog) supplies the meaning — Shuowen
explains that a fattened dog was called 羹獻 and offered at the ancestral
temple, so "to offer, to present" begins as a specific sacrificial term —
and 鬳 *xiàn* supplied the sound in the traditional form. Simplification
replaced 鬳 with 南, so today's visible "南" is a graphic substitute, not the
original phonetic; it should not be read as carrying "south" or any other
independent meaning here.

## Mnemonic
A fattened dog 犬, offered up at the temple 南 (as a stand-in shape) as a
gift.

## Notes
献 generalised far beyond sacrifice: 献血 "to donate blood," 贡献 "to
contribute," 献身 "to devote oneself to a cause" — all descend from that
original ceremonial act of presenting something valued.
