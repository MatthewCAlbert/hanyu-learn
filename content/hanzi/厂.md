## Etymology
Pictographic — but of a different word than the one it now writes.
Shuowen's 厂 (read *hǎn*) pictures a cliff overhang, a hollow in a
rock-face people could shelter beneath: 山石之厓巖，人可凥. As the
simplified form of 廠 "factory," 厂 (now read *chǎng*) is unrelated in
meaning to that ancient cliff-word; it was chosen purely because the
shape of 廠 already contained 厂 as its outer frame, letting the rest be
dropped entirely.

## Mnemonic
A wall and a roof, open on one side: a workshop, a factory floor.

## Notes
This is a case of simplification by *component deletion* rather than
substitution: 厂 keeps only the outer shell of 廠 and discards everything
that used to sit inside it, quite differently from 极/证/胜/录/价, which
each reused a whole separate ancient character.
