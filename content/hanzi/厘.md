## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 厂 (a cliff or shelter, sometimes standing more generally
for a bounded measure or place) supplies a loose semantic frame, and 里
*lǐ* supplies an approximate sound toward *lí*. This entry cannot cite a
Shuowen definition matching the modern "one-thousandth" sense directly,
so it keeps confidence at medium.

厘 names a very small fractional unit — one-thousandth of the base unit
it attaches to (厘米, a centimeter, one-hundredth of a meter, despite the
literal "thousandth" root — usage here has settled on "centi-" rather
than "milli-").

## Mnemonic
A tiny sliver measured off, smaller even than the neighborhood 里 it sits
within: "厘," a small fractional unit.

## Notes
厘米 (centimeter) is by far the most common everyday use — worth learning
as a fixed unit name rather than deriving its exact fraction from the
character's literal parts.
