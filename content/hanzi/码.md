## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 石 (stone) supplies the meaning — 码/碼 was originally a term
for agate or a small counting/weighing stone (compare 玛瑙, agate) — and 马
*mǎ* supplies the sound.

From small stones used for counting or measuring, 码 generalized to
"number, numeral, code" (号码, 密码) and, unrelatedly by simple phonetic
loan, to "yard" as a unit of length, borrowed for its sound alone.

## Mnemonic
A stone 石 set aside to keep count, as steady and countable as horses 马
in a row: "码," a number.

## Notes
密码 (password/code) and 号码 (number) are the two most common everyday
compounds; the "yard" (unit of length) sense is a separate, purely
phonetic borrowing with no connection to the stone-counting sense.
