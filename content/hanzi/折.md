## Etymology
Ideographic per Shuowen: the oldest form shows 艸 (grass, plant stalks)
being cut through by 斤 (an axe) — "to sever, to break." The modern
left-hand 扌 (hand) is a later graphic evolution of that grass-stalk
shape, not the "hand" radical doing new work of its own.

"To break, to fold" (折断, 折纸) is the direct descendant of that
axe-cutting image; "to lose (money), a discount" (打折, 折本) extends the
same idea of a whole being cut down or reduced.

## Mnemonic
An axe 斤 cutting straight through a stalk 扌 (once grass, now a hand
shape): "折," to break or fold.

## Notes
打折 ("to give a discount," lit. "to strike a fold/reduction") and 骨折
("a bone fracture," lit. "bone-break") both build on the same "to break/
reduce" core, one in commerce, one in the body.
