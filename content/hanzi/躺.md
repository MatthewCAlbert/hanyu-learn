## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 身 (body) supplies the meaning "to lie down," and 尚 *shàng*
supplies an approximate sound (*tǎng* and *shàng* share little overlap by
modern readings; this entry cannot confirm a securely attested closer
historical link, so the phonetic role is noted but not asserted with
confidence). 躺 is a comparatively late character, absent from Shuowen and
the earlier classical dictionaries, which is part of why its sound history
is thinner than the ancient pictophonetic compounds.

## Mnemonic
A body 身 that has had quite enough 尚 (still, yet) of standing: it "躺"s
down.

## Notes
躺 specifically means to lie down at full length (躺在床上) — distinct from
坐 (to sit) and 睡 (to sleep), though the three often appear together
describing someone's posture and state.
