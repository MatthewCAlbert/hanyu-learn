## Etymology
The original character for "village" was 邨: 邑 (a populated settlement,
meaning) with 屯 *tún* (to gather, station — also the sound). In running,
cursive-influenced handwriting, 屯 came to be written in a way that looked
like 木 "tree," and that written form was eventually standardised as 村,
with 寸 taking over as the (looser) phonetic. Confidence is medium: the
村 spelling is not, strictly, a semantic-plus-phonetic character built
from scratch for "village" — it is a graphic drift of 邨 that happened to
land on real, if coincidentally meaningful, components.

## Mnemonic
Trees 木, measured out 寸 in neat rows around every house: a village.

## Notes
村 is a useful caution against reading every modern character as a
deliberate semantic + phonetic design — some, like this one, are the
settled-down result of centuries of handwriting drift.
