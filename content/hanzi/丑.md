## Etymology
Pictographic in its original use. Shuowen glosses 丑 (as the second Earthly
Branch 地支) as 紐也, "a knot/binding," and describes the graph as picturing
a hand or claw. That astronomical/calendrical sense (1–3 a.m., the 12th
solar month, the Year of the Ox) is the character's own original meaning
and has nothing to do with ugliness.

**Simplification merged in a second, unrelated character.** 醜 "ugly" —
built from 鬼 "ghost" for meaning and 酉 for sound — was simplified by
reusing the already-simple 丑 as its stand-in. So modern 丑 carries two
historically separate words: the Earthly Branch, and "ugly," which is a
borrowed shape rather than a real etymological sense of 丑 itself.

## Mnemonic
A hand-shape 丑 marking the second hour of the ancient clock — separately
borrowed to write "ugly" because writing 醜 in full is a lot of strokes.

## Notes
丑角 "clown role [in opera]" descends from the "ugly" sense, not the
Earthly Branch. Distinguishing the two histories matters for anyone reading
traditional texts, where 醜 and 丑 (the branch) were never the same
character.
