## Etymology
Pictophonetic. 巾 (cloth) supplies the meaning; the traditional phonetic
was 敝 *bì*. Shuowen's original sense, 帛也, is simply "silk cloth" —
bolts of silk functioned as a form of currency and gift-exchange in early
China, which is exactly how "cloth" became the word for "currency."

Simplification dropped 敝 entirely, keeping only 巾 with a single added
stroke. Confidence is medium, and no phonetic is declared, because
nothing sound-bearing survives from 敝 in the modern shape.

## Mnemonic
Cloth 巾, folded and counted out like banknotes: currency.

## Notes
币 is a bound form today, never standing fully alone — 人民币 "Renminbi,"
货币 "currency," 硬币 "coin" — always naming money as a category rather
than a specific bill or coin.
