## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 酉 (a wine jar — the semantic component in every
fermentation-related character: 醋, 醉, 醒) supplies the meaning, and 夋
*qūn/suān*-ish supplies an approximate sound. Sourness is grounded in
fermentation the same way 醋 (vinegar) itself is — both grow from wine
turning.

"Sour" (taste) extends to the body: 酸 also describes a dull, aching
muscle soreness (腰酸背痛, "sore back and aching waist") — the same tart,
uncomfortable sensation applied to sensation rather than taste.

## Mnemonic
A wine jar 酉 left to ferment too long, turning tart: "酸," sour.

## Notes
心酸 ("heartache, a bittersweet sadness," lit. "sour heart") extends 酸
into emotion — one more sense joining taste and muscle soreness under the
same "tart, uncomfortable" umbrella.
