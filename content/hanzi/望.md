## Etymology
The oracle-bone form shows a person with an exaggerated, upright eye,
standing on a raised mound — someone straining to see into the distance.
Bronze script adds 月 (moon), giving the specific sense "gazing at the
full moon," and also the character's traditional use as the name for the
15th day of the lunar month.

By the time of Shuowen, the shape had eroded enough that Xu Shen instead
analyses it as built on 亡 *wáng* "to be lost, to be away" — someone gone
from home, watched for and awaited (出亡在外，望其還也) — treating 亡 as
the phonetic. Confidence is medium because this is a case where the
Han-dynasty analysis and the earlier script plausibly describe two
different stages of the same character's drift.

## Mnemonic
Standing on a mound 王, eyes fixed on the moon 月, waiting for someone
gone away 亡 to return.

## Notes
望 covers both the literal "to gaze into the distance" (看望, 眺望) and
the abstract "to hope, to expect" (希望, 愿望, 失望) — hoping as a kind of
long-distance watching.
