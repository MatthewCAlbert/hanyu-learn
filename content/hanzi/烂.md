## Etymology
Pictophonetic, traditional 爛: 火 (fire) supplies the meaning, 兰 (traditional
蘭) supplies the sound, shifted to *làn*. Shuowen's gloss 孰也 "cooked
[through]" is the origin: food cooked over fire until soft and falling
apart. "Rotten, worn out, in a mess" all extend that same "gone soft and
falling apart" image from cooking to decay to general disorder.

## Mnemonic
Cooked over fire 火 until as soft and fragrant as orchids 兰 wilting — too
far gone.

## Notes
烂 spans a wide range: 稀烂 "mushy, well-cooked," 腐烂 "to rot," 破烂 "worn-out
junk," and the intensifier use in 烂熟 "thoroughly familiar" — all variations
on "broken down past its original form."
