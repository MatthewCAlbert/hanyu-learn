## Etymology
Pictophonetic, and Shuowen notes explicitly that both halves carry meaning
as well as sound: 刂 (knife) and 朿 *cì* (a pictograph of a thorn) — 朿
supplies the sound *and* reinforces the sense, since a thorn already
pierces the way a knife-stab does. "To stab, a direct piercing wound," per
Shuowen's own definition.

The noun sense "thorn, splinter" (荆棱刺, 鱸刺) traces straight back to 朿's
own original pictograph; the verb "to stab" (刺伤, 刺激) adds the knife.

## Mnemonic
A thorn 朿 sharpened further by a knife 刂: "刺," to stab — or the thorn
itself.

## Notes
Two readings split by register: *cì* is the everyday reading (刺伤, 讽刺
"to satirize/needle someone"); *cī* survives mainly in the onomatopoeic
刺啦 (a ripping/tearing sound).
