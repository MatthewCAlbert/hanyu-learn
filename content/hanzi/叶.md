## Etymology
Modern 叶 ("leaf") is a simplification of traditional 葉, pictophonetic
with 艹 (grass, plants) for meaning and 枼 *yè* for sound. But the shape
简化 settled on — 口 plus 十 — is not a fresh reduction of 葉's own
components; it reuses an old, separate character 叶, which historically
meant "harmonious, in accord" and was read *xié* (a sense and reading
that survive today in some personal and place names). Because the "leaf"
sense and this shape's own older "harmonious" sense are historically
unrelated, this entry does not declare a semantic or phonetic component
and keeps confidence at medium.

## Mnemonic
A leaf, reduced to its simplest possible written form — borrowed
wholesale from an older, unrelated character rather than built fresh from
"grass."

## Notes
叶 as a surname or in old place names sometimes keeps the *Xié* reading
(叶公好龙, a well-known classical idiom) — a fossil of this character's
older, unrelated "harmonious" identity peeking through the modern "leaf"
word.
