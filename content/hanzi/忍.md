## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 心 (heart/mind) supplies the meaning, 刃 *rèn* supplies the
sound, shifted to *rěn*. Shuowen's gloss 能也 is close to "capacity, ability
[to bear something]." 刃 does its job as a sound clue only — it does not mean
"a blade piercing the heart," even though that reading is a vivid and very
common modern mnemonic.

## Mnemonic
A blade 刃 held right over the heart 心 — and still, you hold still.

## Notes
The vivid "knife over heart" image many learners already know is exactly
that: a mnemonic, not the actual etymology — 刃 lends only its sound here.
忍 is the core of 忍耐 "to endure," 忍受 "to bear/tolerate," 忍不住 "unable to
hold back."
