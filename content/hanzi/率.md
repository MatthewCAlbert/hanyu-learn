## Etymology
率's oldest forms are usually read as a pictograph connected to cordage —
a twisted rope or net, possibly used for catching birds — but the
components visible in the modern shape do not resolve cleanly into a
semantic + phonetic pair that this entry can confirm, and the classical
dictionaries offer competing accounts. Given that, this entry does not
assert a specific decomposition and keeps confidence low.

The two modern readings diverge from whatever the original picture was:
*shuài* ("to lead," as in 率领) treats a group the way a rope binds many
strands together and leads them as one; *lǜ* ("a rate, a ratio," as in
效率, 比率) treats it as a fixed proportion, as if measured out along a
cord.

## Mnemonic
A rope binding many strands into one, led forward as a single line: "率,"
to lead — or the fixed ratio a cord is measured against.

## Notes
Two readings, two register-distinct families: *shuài* verbs of leading
(率领, 统率); *lǜ* nouns of ratio/rate (效率, 概率, 汇率) — worth memorizing
by which family a given compound belongs to rather than by any reliable
spelling cue.
