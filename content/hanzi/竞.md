## Etymology
Ideographic, traditional 競: Shuowen's gloss covers both "to argue
forcefully" and "to race, to pursue" — and analyses the graph as 誩 (two
言 "speech" side by side, i.e. two people talking over each other) above
二人 (two people). Simplified 竞 keeps 儿 as a graphic trace of "two
people" but drops the doubled-speech top entirely, replacing it with 立
"to stand" — so the modern shape reads more naturally as "standing up to
someone" than the original "shouting match" picture.

## Mnemonic
Two people 儿 standing up 立 against each other, racing to win.

## Notes
竞 anchors 竞争 "to compete," 竞赛 "competition," 竞选 "to campaign [for
office]" — the "racing against another" sense has fully displaced the
older "arguing" sense in modern use.
