## Etymology
Ideographic. Shuowen glosses 舍 as "a dwelling in a market town" and
analyses the graph through 亼 (a gathering/roof shape) with 屮口 below —
a structure people gather and shelter under. The modern shape's top
reads, at a glance, as 人 and the bottom as 舌, but that is a later,
simplified reinterpretation rather than Shuowen's own account of the
components.

## Mnemonic
A roof 人-shape sheltering a household — a dwelling to live in, or to
leave behind.

## Notes
舍 carries two very different jobs: "dwelling" (宿舍 "dormitory," 舍友
"dorm-mate") and, as a separate borrowed use, "to give up, to abandon"
(舍不得 "can't bear to part with," 取舍 "to choose what to keep and what to
discard") — homophonous uses that modern speakers rarely connect.
