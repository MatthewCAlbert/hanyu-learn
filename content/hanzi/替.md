## Etymology
The top of 替 is two 夫-like figures standing side by side (竝, "to stand
together") — one person literally standing in for another, which fits the
modern meaning "to substitute, to replace" cleanly. Shuowen, however,
glosses the whole character quite differently ("to fall into disuse, one
side sinking lower") and cites a 白-based phonetic that is hard to square
with the modern reading *tì* or with the visual "two people" story.

Given that mismatch between the plausible visual reading and Shuowen's own
gloss, this entry keeps confidence low rather than asserting either account
as settled.

## Mnemonic
Two people standing side by side, one stepping forward as the sun 日 moves
on: one replacing the other — "替."

## Notes
替 is common as a preposition ("on behalf of," 我替你做 "I'll do it in your
place") as well as a verb ("to replace," 代替) — both senses trace to the
same "standing in for another" image, however uncertain its deep history.
