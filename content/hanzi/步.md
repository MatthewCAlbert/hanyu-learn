## Etymology
Ideographic. Two feet (止, each meaning "foot"), one above the other and
facing opposite directions — left foot, then right foot, one stride.
Shuowen's own gloss, 从止相背, "from two 止 facing away from each other,"
already describes exactly this. The app's decomposition data can only
resolve the top 止; the bottom is a further-simplified second foot that no
longer reads as 止 on its own.

## Mnemonic
Left foot 止, then the other, facing the opposite way: one step, then the
next.

## Notes
止 alone means "foot" only in fossilised contexts now (its everyday sense
has shifted to "to stop"); 步 preserves the older "foot → stride" sense in
compounds like 步行, 跑步, 散步.
