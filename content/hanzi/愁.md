## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 心 (heart/mind) supplies the meaning, 秋 *qiū* supplies the
sound and reads unchanged. Shuowen's gloss 憂也 "worry, sorrow" matches
the modern sense directly. 秋's role here is formally phonetic only, but
autumn carries a long-standing poetic association with melancholy in
Chinese literature, which makes this pairing feel unusually apt even though
it is not the character's documented reason for being built this way.

## Mnemonic
A heart 心 heavy as the falling leaves of autumn 秋.

## Notes
愁 is the standard word for worry and sorrow: 发愁 "to worry," 愁眉苦脸 "a
worried, troubled expression," 不愁吃穿 "not worried about food and
clothing" (i.e., comfortably provided for).
