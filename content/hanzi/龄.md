## Etymology
Pictophonetic, traditional 齡: 齿 (teeth) supplies the meaning, 令 *lìng*
supplies the sound, shifted to *líng*. The teeth radical is not
arbitrary: examining wear on teeth was a real, ancient method of judging
an animal's — and by extension a person's — age, which is why "age" is
written with the tooth radical rather than, say, the person radical.

## Mnemonic
Teeth 齿, worn down just enough to tell the years 令 that have passed.

## Notes
龄 is almost always bound: 年龄 "age," 高龄 "advanced age [polite]," 学龄
"school age." Rarely stands alone as a single-character word for "age" —
that job belongs to 年纪 and 岁数 in everyday speech.
