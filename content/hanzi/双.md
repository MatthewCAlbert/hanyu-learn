## Etymology
The traditional character 雙 is ideographic: 雔 (two birds, 隹 doubled) held
by 又 (a hand) — "a pair of birds in hand," giving the general sense "a
pair, two of something."

The simplified 双 is not a mechanical reduction of 雙 piece by piece; it
reuses a much older folk-simplified variant that drops the birds entirely
and doubles 又 itself — two hands, standing iconically for "two, a pair."
Confidence is medium: the *idea* (doubling something to mean "pair") is
continuous across both forms, but 双's own components are not a direct
etymological descendant of 雙's, just a graphically simpler substitute
carrying the same idea.

## Mnemonic
Two hands 又又, side by side: a pair.

## Notes
双 pairs with 只 in classifiers: 一只鞋 "one shoe" but 一双鞋 "a pair of
shoes" — 双 specifically counts things that naturally come in twos.
