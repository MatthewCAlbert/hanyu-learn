## Etymology
Not securely attested. Shuowen analyses the graph as 亼 "to gather" plus a
stroke, glossing it "the present time." The oracle-bone form is a simple shape
— often read as an inverted mouth or a bell-clapper — and scholars do not
agree on what it depicts. Some link it to 吟 / 含, "something held in the
mouth."

What is certain is the meaning: 今 is "now / the present," the fixed opposite
of 昔 "the past." The graph is best learned as an arbitrary sign for that
sense.

## Mnemonic
A lid drawn over the moment you are in: now.

## Notes
Phonetic in 念 *niàn* (to think of), 琴 *qín* (zither), 含 *hán* (to hold in
the mouth), 吟 *yín*. Compare 令, which looks similar but is unrelated.
