## Etymology
Ideographic. The oracle-bone graph shows a person standing on top of a
tree — climbing up and riding on top of something. That "on top of, astride"
image is the root of every modern sense: riding a vehicle (乘车 "to take a
car/bus"), taking advantage of an opportunity (乘机 "to seize the chance"),
and even multiplication in mathematics (乘法 "to multiply" — repeatedly
piling quantity on quantity). The modern shape's visible 禾 and 北 are later
graphic drift, not the original tree-and-person picture, and are best not
over-interpreted piece by piece.

## Mnemonic
A person standing tall on top of a tree, riding the view from up there.

## Notes
Two readings split by sense: *chéng* is the common verb "to ride/take
advantage of"; *shèng* survives in the classical measure word for
chariots and in 千乘之国 "a state of a thousand chariots." Compare 剩
shèng, which reuses this reading with an added 刂.
