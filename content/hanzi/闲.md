## Etymology
Two originally separate characters converged here. Shuowen's 閑 (门 gate
with 木 wood inside) meant 闌也, "a fence, an enclosure" — a wooden barrier.
Separately, 閒 (门 with 月 moon, showing moonlight through a gap in the
door) meant "a gap, an interval" and gave rise to "free time, leisure."
Simplification merged both into one shape, 闲, so the modern character
carries the "idle, leisure" meaning that historically belonged to 閒, not
to the fence-sense 閑 whose shape it actually continues.

## Mnemonic
A gate 门 with nothing but empty wood 木 behind it — nothing going on, time
to relax.

## Notes
闲 today is entirely about free time and idleness: 空闲 "free time," 闲话
"idle chat," 闲人 "an idle person." The "fence/enclosure" sense is extinct
in modern use.
