## Etymology
Ideographic. Shuowen preserves a famous moralising gloss, attributed to
King Zhuang of Chu: 止戈為武, "stopping the spear/halberd is what makes
true martial virtue" — reading the character as 止 (to stop) placed with
戈 (a dagger-axe). Confidence is medium because modern scholarship
generally doubts this etymology: oracle-bone forms show 止 (a foot,
marking advance) beneath 戈, more plausibly "marching forward with a
weapon" than "stopping" one — a case where a philosophically appealing
story may not be the original picture.

## Mnemonic
A weapon 戈, and the wisdom to know when to stop 止: true strength.

## Notes
武 anchors the whole martial vocabulary — 武器 "weapon," 武术 "martial
arts," 武力 "military force" — regardless of which etymology is
correct, the character has meant "military, martial" since the earliest
attested forms.
