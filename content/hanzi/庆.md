## Etymology
The traditional 慶 combined 心 (heart, feeling) and 夂 (a foot, marking
movement — going to congratulate someone in person) with an abbreviated
鹿 (deer): Shuowen explains that auspicious ceremonial gifts were
traditionally deer hides, so a piece of "deer" was folded into the
character for celebration itself.

Simplification dropped all of this, reducing 慶 to the unrelated shapes
广 and 大. Confidence is medium, and no component is declared, because
the modern shape preserves none of the heart, movement, or deer-hide
picture.

## Mnemonic
Under one roof 广, everyone gathers, larger 大 than any ordinary
occasion: a celebration.

## Notes
庆 today appears almost entirely in set celebratory compounds — 庆祝,
国庆 "National Day," 庆典 — the deer-hide gift-giving custom behind it is
long gone from ordinary awareness.
