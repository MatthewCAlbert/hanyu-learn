## Etymology
The traditional form 畢 pictured a hunting net mounted on a long handle
— Shuowen's own definition, 田罔, names it directly: a net for catching
game. "To finish, to complete" is generally understood to come from the
hunt itself being finished once the net closed over its catch.

**Simplification reduced the whole net-and-handle picture to 比 over
十**, a shape that no longer resembles a net at all — this entry keeps
confidence medium given how completely that original picture has been
compressed away.

## Mnemonic
A hunt brought to its close, the net's work finally done: "毕," finished.

## Notes
毕业 (to graduate, covered elsewhere in this app) and 完毕 ("to be
completed," pairing 毕 with 完) both build on this character's core "task
brought to completion" sense.
