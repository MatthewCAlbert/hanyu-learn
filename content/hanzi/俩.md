## Etymology
Ideographic, and unusually transparent for a bound colloquial word: 亻
(person) plus 两 (two) directly compose "the two of [people]" — 俩 is a
fused, spoken contraction of 两个(人), with 两 doing real semantic work here,
not just supplying sound. This is why 俩 can never be followed by its own
measure word: 两个 is already baked in.

## Mnemonic
A person 亻 standing next to "two" 两: the pair of them — "俩."

## Notes
Common trap: 俩 already includes the classifier, so 我们俩 ("the two of us")
is correct but 我们俩个人 is redundant and wrong — never write 个 after 俩.
