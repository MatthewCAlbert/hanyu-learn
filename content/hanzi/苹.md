## Etymology
Pictophonetic. 艹 (plant) supplies the meaning; 平 *píng* supplies the
sound. Shuowen's original sense has nothing to do with fruit: 苹 named
duckweed (蓱/萍), 無根，浮水而生者, "a rootless plant that grows floating
on water." The modern "apple" sense in 苹果 has a separate, much later
origin (via the older loanword 频婆果) and simply borrowed this existing
character for its sound. Confidence is medium because the "apple" sense
is a phonetic reuse layered on top of the original word, not a
semantic development from it.

## Mnemonic
A plant 艹 floating level 平 on the water's surface: duckweed — and,
quite separately, borrowed later for the fruit you actually think of.

## Notes
苹 in isolation still technically means duckweed in classical and
botanical registers; in everyday modern speech it survives almost
exclusively as the first syllable of 苹果 "apple," a sense entirely
disconnected from the character's original meaning.
