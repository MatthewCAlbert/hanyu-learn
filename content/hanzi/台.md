## Etymology
Today's 台 merges two originally unrelated characters, which is why its
etymology cannot be given as one clean story.

One is the small, ancient 台 itself: Shuowen reads it as 从口𠙴聲, "to speak
[with pleasure]" — a phonetic character built on 口 (mouth). Separately,
the bronze-script form has been read as 厶 (an inverted 子, "child") over
口, picturing an unborn child — 台 as the original character for 胎 "fetus."

The other is 臺, an unrelated word for a raised platform or terrace,
etymologically distinct and pronounced identically. Modern simplification
merged 臺 into 台 purely to reduce the character count — not because they
share a history. Confidence is low because no single etymology covers what
today's "台" is asked to mean.

## Mnemonic
A private 厶 word 口 shared from a raised platform.

## Notes
Because two characters were merged, 台's modern senses split cleanly along
the old boundary: "platform, stage, station" (电视台, 舞台) comes from 臺;
"typhoon" 台风 and the *tāi* reading (胎-adjacent) trace to the original 台.
