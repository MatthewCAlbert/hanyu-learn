## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 石 (stone) supplies the meaning, 更 *gèng* supplies the sound,
shifted to *yìng*. Not in Shuowen; the character is attested from later
texts. The image is straightforward — stone-hard — and every figurative sense
(硬性 "rigid," 硬要 "to insist," 生硬 "stiff, forced") extends from literal
physical hardness.

## Mnemonic
As hard as stone 石, and only getting harder 更 the more you push.

## Notes
硬 is the standard antonym of 软 ruǎn "soft" across both literal (硬的/软的
食物) and figurative uses (硬件/软件 "hardware/software" — a modern calque
pair built directly on this opposition).
