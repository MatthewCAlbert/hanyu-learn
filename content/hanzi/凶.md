## Etymology
Shuowen: a picture of a pit in the ground (凵) with crossed lines inside, "the earth dug and something falling into it" — a trap pit, hence ominous, unlucky, and then fierce or deadly. The reading of 乂 as stakes or ensnaring marks is Shuowen's; modern shapes confirm only the pit-and-marks structure.

## Mnemonic
A trap pit 凵 with crossed stakes 乂 inside — nobody wants to fall in.

## Notes
凶手 "murderer", 凶恶, 吉凶 "fortune and misfortune". It is the base of 胸 *xiōng* (where 匈 shows the same element) — sound only.
