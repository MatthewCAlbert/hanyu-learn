## Etymology
Shuowen's primary definition is startling to a modern reader: 治 was
originally the *name of a specific river* in ancient Shandong — pictophonetic,
with 氵 (water) for meaning and 台 *tái/yí* for sound, exactly like any other
river-name character (河, 江, 汉).

The leap from "a river" to "to govern" runs through flood control: the
legendary sage-king Yu the Great earned the mandate to rule by successfully
taming (治) the great floods. "To manage/bring under control (water)" became
"to govern" generally, and — since illness is also something "brought under
control" — also "to cure" (治病, 治疗).

## Mnemonic
Water 氵 brought under control on a raised platform 台: to "治," to govern
(or cure).

## Notes
Do not confuse 治 (govern/cure) with 冶 (yě, to smelt metal) — visually
close, semantically and phonetically unrelated.
