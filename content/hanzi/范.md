## Etymology
Two originally distinct characters stand behind today's 范. Shuowen's own
范 (semantic 艸, phonetic 氾) simply names "a type of grass." A separate,
unrelated traditional character 範 (built on 車 "cart") named 範軷, an
ancient ceremonial rite performed before a journey, read the same as 犯.

Modern simplification reused the small, ancient 范 to write every sense
of 範. Confidence is medium: the 艸/氾 split declared here is genuinely
Shuowen-attested, but for a plant, not for "a pattern, a model" — the
sense speakers actually use 范 for today (as in 模范, 规范).

## Mnemonic
A humble grass 艹, overflowing 氾 into a shape everyone else follows:
a model, a pattern.

## Notes
范 sits alongside 极, 证, 胜, 录, 价 in this dataset's small family of
"borrowed spelling" simplifications — an old, unrelated character
resurrected to carry a more complex traditional word's full meaning.
