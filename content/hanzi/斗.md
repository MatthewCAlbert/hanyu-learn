## Etymology
A pictograph, per Shuowen: 斗 draws a ladle with a handle, used as both a
scoop and a unit of volume (ten 升, roughly a gallon-scale measure — the
same 斗 seen inside 料, "grain measured in a ladle"). This is the reading
behind *dǒu*.

**The "to fight" sense (*dòu*) is an entirely different, merged-in
character.** Traditional 鬥 depicted two men wrestling, with no
etymological connection to the ladle at all; simplification reused 斗's
shape to write it purely to save strokes, collapsing two unrelated words
into one modern character.

## Mnemonic
A ladle with a long handle, scooping out a measure: "斗." Two wrestlers
locked together also became this shape, purely by simplification's
economy — not by any shared history.

## Notes
北斗 ("the Big Dipper," lit. "northern ladle") keeps the *dǒu* ladle sense
alive in its most famous compound; 斗争 ("to struggle, to fight") is the
*dòu* sense, unrelated in origin.
