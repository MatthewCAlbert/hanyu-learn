## Etymology
Pictophonetic in origin, but the sound clue is gone from the modern shape.
The oldest form pictured a person 亻 struck by an arrow 矢, with 昜 *yáng*
supplying the sound — 矢+昜 together written as 𥏫. Shuowen already records
傷 as using a *reduced* form of that phonetic (省聲), and by the time of
simplification the arrow-and-sound compound had eroded into shapes that no
longer read as either "arrow" or "yáng."

The simplified 伤 keeps 亻 (person, meaning) but the remaining strokes are
not a recoverable phonetic component, so none is declared here — the same
situation as 过 and 边, just for a character outside that original set of
eight.

## Mnemonic
A person 亻 struck down, in pain: injured.

## Notes
伤心 "heartbroken" extends the physical wound (伤) metaphorically to
emotional pain — the same move English makes with "wounded feelings."
