## Etymology
Simplified from 東. The reading Shuowen passes on is 日 "sun" showing through
the middle of 木 "a tree" — sunrise behind trees, "east." An alternative,
argued from the bronze forms, takes it as a bundle or sack tied at both ends
(the source of 橐), borrowed for "east" by sound. The sun-and-tree image is the
one usually taught.

## Mnemonic
The sun 日 caught in the branches 木 at dawn: the east.

## Notes
东 also "host" (做东 "to stand treat" — the host sat on the east side), and
"lord / owner" (房东 "landlord"). 东西 "east-west" → "a thing" (see the word
entry). Opposite 西.
