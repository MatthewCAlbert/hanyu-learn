## Etymology
Ideographic. Shuowen analyses it as three parts: 彳 (to walk), 日 (here a
simplification of 皀, a vessel of food), and 夊 (an inverted 止, "to trail
the feet") — walking away, dragging one's feet, from a meal: to withdraw,
to retreat. Duan Yucai's commentary glosses 夊 directly as "walking
slowly, feet dragging."

The modern component 艮 is a clerical-script regularisation that fuses
what were once the separate 日 and 夊 into one now-inseparable shape, with
彳 folding into 辶. Confidence is medium because the "leaving a meal"
picture, while Shuowen-attested, is a fairly specific reconstruction that
later sources do not universally repeat.

## Mnemonic
Walking 辶 backward, reluctant 艮, away from the table: to retreat.

## Notes
退 covers both literal withdrawal (后退, 退出) and formal disengagement —
退休 "to retire," 退票 "to return a ticket" — stepping back from a
position, a place, or a commitment alike.
