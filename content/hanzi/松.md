## Etymology
Pictophonetic in its original, independent sense: 木 (tree) supplies the
meaning "pine," and 公 *gōng* supplies the sound.

**The "loose, relaxed" sense is a separate, merged-in character.**
Traditional 鬆 ("disheveled, loose," built on 髟, hair, with this same 松
as its phonetic half) was simplified by dropping 髟 and keeping only the
phonetic 松 to stand for the whole word. So modern 松 covers "pine tree"
(its own original word) and "loose/relaxed" (borrowed by simplification
from a once-distinct character that merely sounded the same) at once.

## Mnemonic
A pine tree 木, its needles growing loose and open in every direction like
the sign 公 (public, open) itself: "松," pine — and loose, relaxed.

## Notes
放松 ("to relax") and 松树 ("pine tree") look like the same word doing two
jobs, but they descend from two originally separate characters (松 and
鬆) that simplification happened to collapse into one shape.
