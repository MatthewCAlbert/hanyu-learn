## Etymology
Pictophonetic. 走 (to run, to move on foot) supplies the meaning. This
entry, too, is a later Shuowen addendum, originally describing an animal
running with its tail raised. The traditional character 趕 used 旱 *hàn*
as its phonetic; the modern simplified 赶 swaps in the closer, simpler 干
*gān/gǎn* instead — a substitution for ease of writing, not a change in
meaning.

## Mnemonic
Running 走 flat-out, straining every fibre 干: to hurry, to catch up.

## Notes
"An animal running, tail up" generalised fully into human urgency — 赶紧
"hurry up," 赶不上 "unable to catch [a bus, a deadline]," 赶时间 "racing
against time."
