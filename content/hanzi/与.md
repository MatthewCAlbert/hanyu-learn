## Etymology
与 is not an independent creation but the surviving core of the traditional
character 與. Shuowen analyzes 與 itself as 从舁从与 — built from 舁 (four hands
lifting together) plus 与, where 与 alone already depicted two hands passing
something between them: an act of giving or joining. Simplification kept only
that giving-image core (与) and dropped 舁, which is why the modern character
looks too spare to decompose into a clean semantic/phonetic pair — it is a
pictograph of exchange, not a compound.

The senses "and," "together with," and "to give" all trace back to this one
image of two parties meeting in the middle.

## Mnemonic
Two hands reaching toward each other across the middle stroke — one hand
giving, one receiving: "与" for "with," for "and," for "to give."

## Notes
与 also reads yù ("to take part in," as in 参与) and yú (a sentence-final
particle in classical texts, later written 欤). The three readings correspond
to three distinct grammaticalizations of the same "joining" image, not three
unrelated words.
