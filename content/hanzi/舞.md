## Etymology
The oracle-bone character for "dance" is 無 itself: a person holding
tasseled or leafy ornaments in each hand, dancing. Once 無 was borrowed to
write the far more common word "not to have," 舛 (two feet, pointing in
opposite directions — the footwork of a dance) was added underneath to
keep the "dance" sense distinct and readable.

Confidence is medium because Shuowen calls 無 here a plain phonetic
(無聲), when it is really the *original* dancing pictograph, retrofitted
into a semantic-plus-phonetic role only after the borrowing forced 舞 to
be created as a new, disambiguated character.

## Mnemonic
Arms raised, holding ornaments 無, feet stepping opposite ways 舛: dance.

## Notes
舞 keeps its original sense intact and vivid — 跳舞, 舞蹈, 舞台 — none of
the semantic drift seen in characters like 胜 or 证 earlier in this batch.
