## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 艹 (grass, plants) supplies the meaning, and 溥 *pǔ*
supplies the sound. Shuowen's original definition is a genuine surprise:
薄 named a dense thicket of undergrowth — the near opposite of "thin."

The modern "thin" sense is generally taken to be a separate development —
most likely a phonetic loan onto this same graph rather than a natural
extension of "thicket" — so the character's oldest attested meaning and
its everyday modern meaning point in almost opposite directions.

## Mnemonic
Plants 艹 spread 溥 (widely, thinly) across the ground rather than piled
thick: "薄," thin — even though the character's oldest sense was
precisely the opposite, a dense thicket.

## Notes
Three readings split by register: *báo* is the everyday spoken word
(薄一点, "a bit thin"); *bó* is the more literary/compound reading (单薄,
薄弱); *bò* survives only in the plant name 薄荷 (mint).
