## Etymology
Ideographic in the traditional form 婦: 女 (woman) holding 帚 (a broom,
the same pictograph seen in 扫) — Shuowen's own gloss is explicit,
"holding a broom to sprinkle water and sweep," reflecting the domestic
role assigned to married women in that era.

**Simplification reduced the broom 帚 to the smaller fragment 彐**, the
same graphic reduction used in 扫 — the broom is still faintly present,
just compressed to fewer strokes.

## Mnemonic
A woman 女 holding a reduced broom-shape 彐: "妇," a married woman.

## Notes
妇女 (women, as a social category) and 夫妇 (a married couple, lit.
"husband-wife") both build on this character's core "married woman"
sense.
