## Etymology
A pictograph, per Shuowen — but of something unexpected: a huge snake,
"said to swallow elephants," referencing the mythical 巴蛇 of classical
legend. The character's everyday modern senses — "to hope for, to cling
to" (巴望, 巴不得) and its role as a purely sound-carrying suffix (尾巴,
covered in this app's entry for 尾; 嘴巴, mouth) — have no clearly
attested bridge back to that legendary snake, so this entry keeps
confidence low.

## Mnemonic
A snake coiled so large it could swallow an elephant whole: "巴" — though
in modern use, mostly just a sound tacked onto the end of another word.

## Notes
巴 carries almost no independent meaning in most modern words — 尾巴,
嘴巴, and 眼巴巴 (looking on eagerly/helplessly) all use it as a fixed
suffix rather than a free-standing meaningful word.
