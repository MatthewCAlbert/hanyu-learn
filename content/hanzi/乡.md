## Etymology
The oracle-bone form of 鄉 (乡's traditional ancestor) pictures two kneeling
figures facing each other across a serving vessel of food — a shared
meal. That single picture split into three related words as the script
developed: 饗 "to host a banquet," 向 "to face," and 鄉 "the local
community you would share a feast with" — one's home village or district.
Confidence is medium because the app's decomposition data cannot resolve
乡's own components at all, so this account rests on the character's
premodern ancestor rather than the modern simplified shape itself.

## Mnemonic
Two people, seated across a shared meal, in the place they both call
home: the village.

## Notes
乡 keeps only the "home community" branch of that three-way split — 家乡,
乡下, 故乡 — while 向 (to face) and the 饗-derived 响 (sound, echo) carry
the other two senses forward as fully separate characters today.
