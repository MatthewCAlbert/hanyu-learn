## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 页 (from 頁, a kneeling figure with an enlarged head — the
semantic component in every head-related character: 顶, 颜, 顺, 题) supplies
the meaning "back of the neck," and 工 *gōng* supplies the sound — a wider
gap between *gōng* and *xiàng* today than most phonetic pairs, but a good
match in Old Chinese, when both began with a *k*-like sound.

"Item, clause" (事项, 项目) is a later extension: a list's items were
metaphorically its "necks" — the joints where one segment meets the next —
or, by a simpler account, where a numbered item is written one below another
the way vertebrae stack down a neck.

## Mnemonic
The back of the neck 页, where a *gōng* 工 (work) collar sits — and, by
extension, one item on a list, stacked like vertebrae.

## Notes
The "item" sense (第一项, 项目) is by far the most common use in modern
Mandarin; the literal "back of neck" sense is largely confined to medical and
classical contexts.
