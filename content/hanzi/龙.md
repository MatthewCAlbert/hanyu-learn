## Etymology
Pictographic. Shuowen calls it 象形 outright and describes the dragon
itself — "chief of the scaled creatures, able to be dark or bright, thin
or huge, short or long." Oracle-bone forms show a long, sinuous body with
an open, fanged mouth and a crested head, coiling and twisting.

Confidence is medium because the modern simplified 龙 is a cursive-derived
shorthand of the far more elaborate traditional 龍 (whose pieces are
traditionally read as head-and-crest on the left, scaled body on the
right); the simplified strokes no longer trace out those parts
individually, so no component is declared.

## Mnemonic
A long, coiling body with a crested head, caught mid-flight: the dragon.

## Notes
龙 appears almost entirely in fixed compounds and idioms in modern
Chinese (恐龙 "dinosaur," 龙舟 "dragon boat") — it is a mythological, not
zoological, character.
