## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 足 (foot) supplies the meaning, and 巨 *jù* supplies the
sound, an exact match. Shuowen's original definition is unexpectedly
specific: 距 named a rooster's spur — the sharp projection on a
rooster's leg used in cockfighting.

The modern sense "apart, at a distance" is a later development with no
clearly attested bridge from "rooster's spur" — this entry does not
invent one, and keeps confidence at medium for that reason, though the
semantic and phonetic components themselves are securely sourced.

## Mnemonic
A foot 足 with a great 巨 spur jutting out, holding its ground at a
distance: "距," apart.

## Notes
距离 (distance, covered elsewhere in this app) is 距's most common
modern compound — the rooster-spur sense survives only as etymological
trivia, not in any living usage.
