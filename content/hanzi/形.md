## Etymology
Pictophonetic. 彡 (fine lines/markings, used generally for visible
texture or depiction) supplies the meaning; the traditional phonetic was
幵 *jiān*, later regularised in writing to the more common, similarly
shaped 开. Shuowen's gloss, 象也, "an image, an appearance," is unchanged
in the modern word.

## Mnemonic
Fine lines 彡, opened up 开 for all to see: a visible shape.

## Notes
形 pairs constantly with 式 (as in 形式 "form") and with 象 (形象 "image,
public persona") — all three characters circle the same idea of external,
visible shape as distinct from inner substance.
