## Etymology
Pictographic. Shuowen describes the animal directly before noting the
character pictures it: "a long-nosed, tusked great beast of the south,
bearing young once every three years" — 象耳牙四足之形, "it pictures the
shape of its ears, tusks and four legs." The oracle-bone form is
recognisably an elephant in profile, trunk curling forward.

## Mnemonic
Ears, tusks, trunk, four sturdy legs: an elephant, drawn from the side.

## Notes
"A concrete, recognisable animal shape" generalised into the completely
abstract "an image, a representation, a phenomenon" — 现象 "phenomenon,"
形象 "image," 想象 "to imagine," 印象 "impression" — the elephant's own
unmistakable form becoming the model for "form" itself.
