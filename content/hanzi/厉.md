## Etymology
Pictophonetic, traditional 厲: 厂 (a cliff/rock face) supplies the meaning,
万 (traditional 萬) supplies the sound, shifted to *lì*. Shuowen's gloss
旱石也 is "a coarse whetstone" — a rough stone found on a cliff, used for
sharpening blades. "Severe, strict, harsh" is a direct extension: an edge
sharpened to its keenest, most cutting state.

## Mnemonic
A rough stone on a cliff face 厂, sharp enough to grind ten thousand 万
blades keen.

## Notes
厉 today is almost entirely figurative: 严厉 "strict/severe," 厉害 "amazing,
formidable" (also, confusingly, "terrible" depending on context), 变本加厉
"to get worse and worse." The literal "whetstone" sense has fallen away.
