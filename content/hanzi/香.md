## Etymology
Ideographic. Shuowen reads it as 黍 (millet) with 甘 (sweet) — millet, a
prized staple grain, standing for fragrance itself: 黍稷馨香, "the aroma
of millet and grain," is quoted directly from the Chunqiu. By clerical
script, 黍 had simplified to 禾 (grain generally) and 甘 had converged in
shape with the unrelated 日.

## Mnemonic
Grain 禾 cooking under the sun 日: the smell of a good meal.

## Notes
香 covers fragrance in the broadest sense — food (香喷喷), scent (香水),
and even "sound, satisfying sleep" (睡得很香) — anything pleasing enough
to be compared to the smell of good grain.
