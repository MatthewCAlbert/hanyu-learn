## Etymology
Pictophonetic, traditional 漲: 氵 (water) supplies the meaning, 张
(traditional 張, "to stretch, to open wide") supplies the sound — and here
also an echo of meaning, since water rising is water spreading/stretching
outward, close to what 张 itself describes. Not in Shuowen.

## Mnemonic
Water 氵 stretching 张 higher and wider until it overflows its banks.

## Notes
Two readings: *zhǎng* is the common verb for rising levels, literal and
figurative (水涨 "the water rises," 涨工资 "to get a raise"); *zhàng*
survives in 涨红了脸 "to flush red in the face" and a few dialectal uses
about swelling.
