## Etymology
Shuowen reads 昌 as 日 (sun) and 曰 (to speak), glossing it "fine words", which then extends to "good, flourishing". Other scholars read the early forms as a sun above a mouth, with different readings; the origin is not settled. Nowadays "prosperous" is the usual sense.

## Mnemonic
Say 曰 it with the sun 日 shining on it: bright prospects.

## Notes
昌盛 "flourishing", 繁荣昌盛 "prosperous", 昌明 "enlightened (era)". 昌 *chāng* is a sound part in 唱 *chàng*, 倡 *chàng*, 猖, 娼.
