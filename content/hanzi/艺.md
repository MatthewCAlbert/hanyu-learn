## Etymology
The traditional 藝 was built on 蓺, itself a picture of a person kneeling
and planting a seedling in the earth — "to cultivate, to plant" — with 云
added below. From "to cultivate a crop with skill" the sense generalised
into "to cultivate a skill," and from there into the classical "Six
Arts" a cultivated person was expected to master: ritual, music,
archery, charioteering, calligraphy and mathematics.

Simplification cut the entire picture down to 艹 and 乙, keeping only a
trace of the plant radical. Confidence is low, and no component is
declared, because the modern shape preserves neither the kneeling figure
nor the seedling directly.

## Mnemonic
A single sprout 艹, carefully tended 乙: a cultivated skill, an art.

## Notes
艺 now covers "art" broadly — 艺术 "art," 手艺 "craftsmanship," 文艺
"literature and the arts" — with cultivation of plants and cultivation
of talent sharing one deep root, even if the modern shape no longer
shows it.
