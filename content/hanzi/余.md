## Etymology
Simplification merged two originally separate words into this one
shape. 余 was already, on its own, an ancient first-person pronoun, "I,
me," of uncertain pictographic origin (possibly a shelter or tool shape).
Traditional 餘 — 食 (food, semantic) plus this same 余 as phonetic — meant
"surplus, leftover [food, and by extension anything]." Simplification
dropped the 食 from 餘, letting the bare 余 stand for both the ancient
pronoun and the modern "surplus" sense at once — this entry keeps
confidence medium given that dual, unmerged history.

## Mnemonic
What's left over after a meal, set quietly aside: "余," surplus — the
same shape that, in old texts, simply meant "I."

## Notes
业余 ("amateur, spare-time," lit. "surplus to one's profession") and 十
余 ("more than ten," lit. "ten and a surplus") both use the modern
"surplus/remaining" sense — the classical "I" sense survives only in
old texts and a few fixed literary expressions.
