## Etymology
Not securely attested. Shuowen glosses it 肩 "to shoulder (a load)" and reads
the graph as a figure bearing weight; oracle-bone interpretations vary. The
early senses are "to bear" and "to be able to," from which "to overcome, to
subdue" and "to restrain" (克制) developed. *kè* "gram" is a modern
sound-loan.

## Mnemonic
A figure braced under a heavy load: bearing it — mastering it.

## Notes
Senses: overcome / capture (克服, 攻克, 克敌), restrain (克制, 克己), be able
to (克勤克俭), gram (千克 kilogram), and in transcriptions (克隆 "clone",
坦克 "tank"). 克扣 "to dock (wages)."
