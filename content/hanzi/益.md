## Etymology
Ideographic per Shuowen: 水 (water, drawn above as it originally was)
overflowing 皿 (a vessel) — a container filled to overflowing, the very
picture of abundance. Shuowen states the idea plainly: the character's
whole meaning is right there in "water spilling over a dish."

"Overflowing abundance" generalized to "benefit, profit" (利益, 益处) and,
as an adverb, "increasingly, more and more" (日益, "day by day, more and
more") — abundance that keeps growing.

## Mnemonic
Water 水 filled so full in its dish 皿 that it spills over the rim:
"益," benefit — more than enough.

## Notes
利益 ("benefit, interest") and 有益 ("beneficial") both keep this
character's core "overflowing abundance" sense, now firmly abstract
rather than literal.
