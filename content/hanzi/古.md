## Etymology
Ideographic. Shuowen reads 十 (ten) over 口 (mouth) as "one who has heard
[things] passed down through ten mouths" — 識前言者也, someone who knows
what earlier generations said, i.e. old lore, transmitted orally many
times over. Confidence is medium because this "ten mouths" reading is a
plausible folk-etymological gloss on an already-simplified seal-script
shape rather than something confirmed from oracle-bone evidence.

## Mnemonic
A story passed through ten 十 mouths 口, worn smooth with retelling: this
is ancient.

## Notes
古 opposes 今 "now" the way "ancient" opposes "modern" — 古代 "ancient
times," 古老 "age-old," 古怪 "strange" (literally "old and unusual," i.e.
out of step with the present).
