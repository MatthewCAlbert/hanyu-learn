## Etymology
Ideographic. Shuowen reads it as "a sour fruit," 木 (tree) with 甘
("sweet," here standing for the fruit's flesh) — generally taken as the
original character for 梅 (plum). The modern meaning, "so-and-so, a certain
[person/thing]," has nothing to do with fruit: it is a phonetic loan (假借)
that overwrote the concrete sense entirely.

## Mnemonic
A certain tree 木 bearing a certain sweet 甘 fruit — nobody says which one.

## Notes
某 for "a certain, some unspecified" is now so dominant that the "sour
fruit" sense is gone from ordinary Chinese — 某人, 某些, 某种 are the only
uses a learner will meet.
