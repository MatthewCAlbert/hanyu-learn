## Etymology
Not securely attested in its semantic logic. Traditional 廳 is built from 广
(a shelter/hall) with 聽 (to listen, to hear a case) inside — a hall is
where officials sat to hear petitioners, so the "hall" sense may descend
from "the listening-hall." The simplified 厅 keeps only 广 (reduced to 厂)
and 丁 for sound, which is why the modern shape no longer visibly carries
the "listening" story — treat that as background for the traditional form,
not as something readable in 厅 itself.

## Mnemonic
A hall 厂/广 where a single guest 丁 waits to be received.

## Notes
厅 names both rooms in a home (客厅 "living room," 餐厅 "dining room" — also
"restaurant") and government departments (教育厅 "provincial education
department"), a spread from "hall" to "the office housed in it."
