## Etymology
The bronze graph shows a person (人 / 壬) carrying 東, a bundle or sack slung
over the shoulder — a heavy load borne on the body. Shuowen: 厚也 "thick,
substantial." From "heavy" grew "weight," "serious / important" (重要, 重视),
and — read *chóng* — "a layer" and "again, to repeat" (重复, 重新).

## Mnemonic
A figure bent under a slung sack 東: a heavy load.

## Notes
Two readings: *zhòng* "heavy / important" (体重, 重点), *chóng* "again / layer"
(重复, 重来, 双重). The modern shape 千 + 里 is a worn-down person-and-sack.
