## Etymology
Ideographic at its oldest. The oracle-bone form shows 日 (sun) and 月
(moon) together among 茻 (grass/reeds) — the sun risen while a fading moon
still lingers in the sky, i.e. daybreak. By seal script that picture had
already worn down to 倝 (a corruption of "sun among grass") beside 舟
*zhōu*, which Shuowen reads as a late-added phonetic.

The modern right-hand component, 月, is a further convergence: it is what
became of 舟 "boat," not the moon that was part of the original picture —
a coincidence of shape, not a second appearance of "moon" in the story.
Confidence is medium because the phonetic role of 舟/月 for *cháo/zhāo* is
a loose match, and the character's history runs through at least two
distinct reinterpretations.

## Mnemonic
The sun 龺 rising over a boat 月 setting out at first light: morning.

## Notes
Two readings split cleanly by sense: *zhāo* "morning" (今朝, itself
literary) versus *cháo* "court, dynasty, facing towards" (朝代, 朝着) —
the ruler's morning audience is the historical bridge between them.
