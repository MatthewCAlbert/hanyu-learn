## Etymology
The traditional 亂 pictured two hands untangling a snarl of silk thread
(𤔔), with 乙 (a hook-shaped mark for "to sort out") added on top —
literally "to bring tangled thread under control." Shuowen's own gloss,
治也 "to govern, to bring to order," is the *positive* outcome of that
work; "disorder, chaos" is the tangle itself, so the same character has
carried both "the mess" and "fixing the mess" since antiquity.

Simplification discarded the hands-and-silk picture entirely, replacing it
with the unrelated shapes 舌 and 乚. Confidence is low, and no component is
declared, because the modern shape is a wholesale graphic substitution with
no recoverable link to either the tangled thread or the sorting hook.

## Mnemonic
A tongue 舌 twisted into a knot 乚: chaos.

## Notes
乱 is one of the rare Chinese words that historically named both a problem
and its solution — "disorder" and "to set right" — before the modern
language settled on "disorder, chaos" as the only common sense.
