## Etymology
Pictophonetic. 土 (earth/land) supplies the meaning; 竟 *jìng* supplies
the sound — and, as Shuowen notes, classical texts often used 竟 alone
(itself meaning "to finish, an end point") to write this same word,
before 土 was added to specialise it for territorial boundaries.

## Mnemonic
Land 土, right up to where it finally ends 竟: the border.

## Notes
"The edge of a territory" generalised into "any bounded realm, a state
of being" — 环境 "environment," 处境 "one's situation," 境界 "a level of
attainment" — a physical border becoming the model for any kind of
bounded condition.
