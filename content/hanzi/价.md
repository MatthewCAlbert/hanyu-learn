## Etymology
Two originally distinct characters stand behind today's 价. Shuowen's 价
(semantic 亻, phonetic 介) meant "good, excellent" — 善也. A separate,
unrelated character 價 meant "the price of goods," built from 人 with 賈
"merchant" as phonetic.

From the Ming dynasty onward, 价 began to be used as a simpler stand-in
for 價, and modern simplification made this official — every sense of
"price" is now written with the small, ancient 价. Confidence is medium:
亻/介 is a real, Shuowen-attested split, but for "good," not "price."

## Mnemonic
A person 亻 caught in between 介 buyer and seller: negotiating the price.

## Notes
价 is a fourth case in this dataset — alongside 极, 证, 胜, 录 — of
simplification reusing a small, existing, unrelated character rather than
trimming a complex one down.
