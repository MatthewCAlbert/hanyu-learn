## Etymology
Pictophonetic, traditional 膽: ⺼ (flesh/body-part) supplies the meaning,
詹 *zhān* supplied the sound in the traditional form. Shuowen's gloss 連肝之
府 is "the organ attached to the liver" — a direct anatomical naming of the
gall bladder. Simplification replaced 詹 with 旦, so the modern 胆 no longer
carries a working phonetic; 旦 here is a graphic stand-in, not a sound clue
to rely on.

## Mnemonic
An organ ⺼ tucked beside the liver, holding steady at first light 旦.

## Notes
胆 is the organ in 胆囊 "gall bladder," but its everyday use is entirely
figurative: 胆量/勇气-adjacent 胆 for "courage, nerve" (大胆 "bold," 胆小
"timid," 吓破了胆 "scared out of one's wits") — courage located in the gall
bladder rather than the heart, unlike English idiom.
