## Etymology
Pictographic. Shuowen defines 革 precisely as "an animal hide with the
hair removed and treated" — leather, as distinct from raw, hairy 皮 hide —
and marks the graph 象形, "a picture," of a hide stretched out for
processing.

## Mnemonic
A hide stretched flat, hair scraped away, cured into leather.

## Notes
革 covers the literal (皮革 "leather") and, through the idea of stripping
away and remaking, the figurative "to reform, to remove": 改革 "reform,"
革命 "revolution" (literally "to strip away [the old] mandate").
