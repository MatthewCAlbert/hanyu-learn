## Etymology
Pictographic. The oracle-bone form is a frontal view of a sheep's head:
two curling horns above, the face tapering below. Shuowen's own gloss
describes the whole animal — "the shape of its head, horns, feet and
tail" — though the graph itself, both then and now, shows only the head
and horns; the fuller-body reading is Xu Shen's gloss, not the picture on
the page.

## Mnemonic
Two curved horns 丷 over a face: a sheep, looking straight at you.

## Notes
羊 as a semantic component marks a small but productive family — 美, group
with 群, and the taste-word 羊-adjacent 鲜 (though 鲜's own semantic is
鱼, not 羊) — sheep were valuable enough in early agrarian life to leave
a real mark on the writing system.
