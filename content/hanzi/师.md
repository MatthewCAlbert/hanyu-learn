## Etymology
Simplified from 師. The old graph pairs 𠂤 (*duī*, a mound or massed heap) with
帀 (a wrapping). Its earliest sense is military — "a host, an army unit"
(Shuowen: "2,500 men make a 師"). From "one who leads a host" came "master,
one skilled in a craft," and so "teacher."

The simplified left element is a reduction of 𠂤, not the knife 刂 it
resembles.

## Mnemonic
One who marshals a host — and, later, one who marshals a class.

## Notes
Senses: teacher (老师), master craftsman (师傅), a division of troops (师), to
model on (师法). 帀 also appears in 帅 / 帥.
