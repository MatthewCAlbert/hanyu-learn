## Etymology
Pictophonetic, traditional 輩: 车 (chariot) supplies the meaning, 非 *fēi*
supplies the sound, shifted to *bèi*. Shuowen's gloss describes a military
unit — "when an army sends out a hundred chariots, that group is called a
辈" — a batch, a cohort moving together. "Generation, group of people" is a
direct generalisation from that military batch to any cohort sharing an
age or status.

## Mnemonic
A hundred chariots 车, all wrong 非 for solo travel — they move as one
generation, together.

## Notes
辈 anchors 一辈子 "a whole lifetime," 长辈/晚辈 "elders/younger generation,"
同辈 "peers of the same generation" — always about cohort and generational
position, never about a single individual.
