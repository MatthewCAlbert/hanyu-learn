## Etymology
Ideographic. Traditional 絕 shows 糸 (silk thread), 刀 (a knife), and 卪
(a kneeling figure/measuring mark) together — a knife cutting silk thread
at a fixed point, 斷絲也, "cut thread." The oracle-bone form is even more
direct: two strands of thread with a clean break between them.

Simplification replaced 刀+卪 with the unrelated 色, purely as a graphic
substitution. Confidence is medium because 纟 (thread) remains a genuine,
checkable component, but no phonetic or second semantic piece survives in
the modern right-hand side.

## Mnemonic
A thread 纟, its colour 色 fading out completely: cut off, gone.

## Notes
"To cut a thread clean through" generalised into "to cut off entirely, to
sever" (拒绝, 杜绝) and, as an intensifier, "extreme, without exception"
(绝对, 绝望) — nothing left connecting to what came before.
