## Etymology
Simplified from 兒, a pictograph of an infant: an open skull-fontanelle (囟)
over 儿, the "walking legs" element. Shuowen notes the top explicitly as
"a small child's fontanelle, not yet closed." The simplification discards the
head and leaves only the legs.

As the erhua suffix -儿, the character no longer means "child" at all — it
writes a rhotic colouring on the preceding syllable.

## Mnemonic
Two little legs: a child.

## Notes
儿 is Kangxi radical 10, the "legs" that stand under 见, 元, 光, 先, 兄. The
"child" sense proper is now carried by 孩 and by 儿子, 女儿.
