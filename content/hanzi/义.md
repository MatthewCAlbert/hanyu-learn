## Etymology
The traditional 義 combined 我 (a first-person pronoun, originally a
serrated weapon used as a phonetic loan) with 羊 (sheep, marking
auspiciousness) — Shuowen's gloss, 己之威儀也, is "one's own dignified
bearing," with 羊 chosen, per Xu Shen, because it shares the same
positive connotation as 善 "good" and 美 "beautiful."

Simplification replaced the whole thing with the unrelated shapes 丶 and
乂, purely as a stroke-saving graphic substitution. Confidence is low,
and no component is declared, because the modern 义 cannot be read back
into either 我 or 羊.

## Mnemonic
A cross-mark 乂, dotted 丶 with certainty: what is right.

## Notes
义 now anchors words about morality and meaning alike — 意义 "meaning,"
正义 "justice," 义务 "duty/obligation" — all descended from the same
"proper, dignified conduct" root, even though the modern shape shows none
of it.
