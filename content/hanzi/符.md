## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 竹 (bamboo) supplies the meaning, and 付 *fù* supplies the
sound. Shuowen's definition is administratively precise: under Han
dynasty practice, a 符 was a six-inch bamboo tally split into two
matching halves — one kept by each party — that proved a message's
authenticity when the two pieces were fitted back together.

That "matching halves prove authenticity" mechanism is exactly the
etymology behind 符合 (covered elsewhere in this app — "to match, to
accord with").

## Mnemonic
A bamboo 符 tally handed 付 to a trusted party, its jagged matching edge
the proof no forgery can fake: "符," a tally — or a symbol.

## Notes
符合 (to match/accord with) and 符号 ("a symbol, a mark") both descend
from this one administrative image of two halves proving each other
genuine.
