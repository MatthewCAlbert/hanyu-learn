## Etymology
Not securely attested in its concrete origin. Shuowen ties 甲 to yin-yang
cosmology (the first stem, associated with spring growth) and describes a
seed still wearing its outer husk 孚甲 as it sprouts — but the oracle-bone
graph is a simple cross or grid shape, and scholars disagree on whether it
originally pictured a woven mat, a stitched seam in leather armour, or a
turtle-shell pattern. Confidence stays low on the concrete picture; the
grammatical role — first of the ten Heavenly Stems 天干 — is well attested
regardless.

## Mnemonic
A seed 木 still wearing its cracked outer shell as the first shoot of
spring.

## Notes
甲 orders the Heavenly Stems the way 子丑寅卯 order the Earthly Branches, and
by extension "甲" = "A" or "first" in any ranked list (甲乙丙丁 ≈ A, B, C, D).
Its "armour, shell" sense (盔甲, 甲壳) is a separate, later-dominant use.
