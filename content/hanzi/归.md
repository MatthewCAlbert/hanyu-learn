## Etymology
The traditional form 歸 was considerably more complex: 止 (a foot, "to go")
plus 阜 (a mound) plus 婦's 帚 (a broom) — pictures connected, across
different scholarly accounts, to a bride's ritual "return" to her natal
home, or to troops returning from a mound/garrison. Simplification reduced
all of that to 归 (a 彐-like fragment plus 刂), which does not preserve a
recognizable semantic or phonetic component from the traditional form —
this entry does not declare either, and confidence is low given how much
was lost.

The core meaning "to return, to go back to where one belongs" survived
the simplification intact even though its pictorial support did not.

## Mnemonic
A path curving back around: "归," to return.

## Notes
归 is often the second half of a resultative-style compound describing
where something ends up belonging: 属于 uses a different character for a
similar idea, but 归 itself appears in 回归 (to return/regress) and 归还
(to return something borrowed).
