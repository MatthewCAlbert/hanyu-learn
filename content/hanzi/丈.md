## Etymology
Ideographic per Shuowen: 又 (a hand) grasping 十 (ten, here standing for a
measuring rod marked in ten units) — a hand holding out a rod to measure
off "ten feet," the unit 丈 still names today (about 3.3 meters).

The honorific senses — "gentleman" (as in 丈夫, covered elsewhere in this
app) and a term of respect for an elder male (老丈) — are separate,
later extensions: someone of stature, measured out fully, the way the
unit itself measures a full span.

## Mnemonic
A hand 又 holding a measuring rod marked to ten 十: "丈," a measure of
length — and, honorifically, a person of full stature.

## Notes
丈夫 ("husband") and 岳丈 ("father-in-law," a more literary term) both
carry this character's honorific "man of standing" sense, distinct from
its everyday unit-of-length use.
