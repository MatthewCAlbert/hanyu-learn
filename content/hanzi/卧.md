## Etymology
Ideographic. Shuowen glosses 臥 as 伏也, "to lie prone," and explains the
graph directly: 人 (a person, here bent over) beside 臣 — not the "official"
sense 臣 usually carries, but a component originally picturing an eye
turned sideways, "taking its lying-down aspect" — a person reclining with
eyes turned to the side, exactly the posture of lying down. Not "a person
altered from 卜," a shape-based folk gloss with no source behind it.

## Mnemonic
A person 人 lying down, eyes 臣 turned sideways toward sleep.

## Notes
卧 covers lying down generally (卧倒 "to lie flat," 卧铺 "sleeper berth [on a
train]") and, figuratively, "hidden, in reserve": 卧底 "an undercover agent"
(literally "lying hidden at the bottom").
