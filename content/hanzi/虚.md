## Etymology
Pictophonetic, traditional 虛: 丘 (a mound/hill) supplied the meaning, 虍
*hū* supplied the sound. Shuowen's original sense is a specific large
mound — a place name for a particular hill — not "a tiger stalking in the
bushes," a folk gloss with no source behind it. That concrete "hill" sense
is entirely obsolete; "empty, void, unoccupied" is the sense that
completely took over, likely via a hollow space inside or around such a
mound, though the exact bridge is not clearly documented.

## Mnemonic
A hill 丘, hollowed out and empty inside.

## Notes
虚 today is almost entirely abstract: 虚假 "false," 谦虚 "modest" (empty of
pride), 虚拟 "virtual," 空虚 "hollow, empty [of feeling]." The concrete
"mound" sense left no trace in ordinary vocabulary.
