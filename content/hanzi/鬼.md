## Etymology
Pictographic. Shuowen: 人所歸為鬼, "what a person returns to [after death] is a
鬼" — from 人 (person) with a component sketching a large, frightening head.
Oracle-bone forms already show a kneeling person topped with an oversized,
mask-like head, consistent with Shuowen's own reading. This is one of the
more securely attested pictographs in the set: the "scary head on a person"
image is agreed across sources.

## Mnemonic
A person with a huge, spooky head — a ghost.

## Notes
鬼 is Kangxi radical 194 and heads its own small family: 魂 "soul," 魄 "spirit,"
魔 "demon" (borrowed from Sanskrit *māra* via 麻+鬼). Colloquially it also
means "sly, crafty" (鬼点子 "sly idea") and intensifies adjectives (鬼冷
"freezing cold") — senses built on "uncanny," not on the ghost itself.
