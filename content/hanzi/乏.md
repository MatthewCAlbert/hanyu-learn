## Etymology
Ideographic — and unusually explicit about its own logic. Shuowen quotes
the Spring and Autumn Annals commentary directly: 反正為乏, "the reverse of
正 [correct, upright] is 乏." The graph is literally 正 written backwards —
a rare case where a classical source states outright that a character was
built by inverting another one, rather than the more common route of
combining independent pictures.

## Mnemonic
正 "correct" turned on its head — the opposite: lacking, deficient.

## Notes
乏 covers scarcity (缺乏 "to lack," 贫乏 "impoverished, meagre") and
exhaustion (疲乏 "fatigued," 乏力 "lacking strength"). Historically it also
named the raised shield archers hid behind at a target range — the "反正"
story concerns that older, now-obsolete usage.
