## Etymology
Two distinct characters stand behind today's 胜, exactly as with 极 and 证
elsewhere in this batch. 勝 (semantic 力 "strength," phonetic 朕) meant
"to be equal to a task, to bear up under it," and from "having what it
takes" came "to win." 胜, a separate, ancient and rarely-used character
built from ⺼ (flesh) and 生 (phonetic), meant "the rank smell of raw dog
fat" — entirely unrelated.

Modern simplification reused the small, ancient 胜 to write every sense of
勝. Confidence is medium: ⺼/生 is a real, Shuowen-attested split, but for
"raw meat smell," not for "victory."

## Mnemonic
Flesh 月 that is still raw and alive 生 — no victory yet, only potential.

## Notes
Three characters in this one batch — 极, 证, 胜 — all show the identical
simplification move: retire a complex traditional character, and reuse a
small, ancient, similar-sounding one that was already sitting unused.
