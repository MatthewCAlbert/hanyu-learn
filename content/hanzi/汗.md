## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 氵 (water) supplies the meaning, 干 *gān* supplies the sound,
shifted to *hàn*. Shuowen's gloss 人液也 is simply "bodily fluid [from a
person]" — sweat, named as directly as possible. The character has drifted
very little in meaning since.

## Mnemonic
Water 氵 the body wrings out when it's dry 干 and overworked.

## Notes
汗 anchors 出汗 "to sweat," 汗水 "sweat," and the modern internet-slang
interjection 汗 (also read *hàn*) for cringing/speechless embarrassment — a
2000s coinage riffing on the idea of a nervous sweat-drop, unrelated to the
classical sense but built on the same character.
