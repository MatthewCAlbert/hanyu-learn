## Etymology
Shuowen reads the shape as a person with a hunched or deformed back,
glossing the character as "ugly, deformed" — but modern archaeology
generally reads the same cross-shaped oracle-bone and bronze forms
instead as the floor plan of an ancestral temple or tomb chamber, with no
clear connection to a bent human figure. The two accounts do not agree,
so this entry keeps confidence medium rather than asserting either as
settled.

Either way, the modern senses "second, next in rank" (亚军, "runner-up,"
lit. "second army/place") and "Asia" (亚洲) are both later developments —
"second" plausibly from something being lesser or subordinate, and "Asia"
purely as a phonetic transliteration with no meaning connection at all.

## Mnemonic
A shape standing just one rank below the top: "亚," second — and, by pure
sound-borrowing, the first syllable of "Asia."

## Notes
Do not read any meaning into 亚's use in 亚洲 (Asia) — it is a
phonetic transliteration, exactly like 咖啡 (coffee) or 沙发 (sofa), not a
description of the continent.
