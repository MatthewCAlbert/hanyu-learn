## Etymology
A pictograph: Shuowen describes it exactly as "the shape of upper and
lower [teeth] interlocking" — the crossed, jagged strokes represent
molars meshing together, distinct from 齒/齿 (the front incisors, drawn as
a row of separate teeth in a mouth outline). As an atomic pictograph, 牙
has no separate semantic or phonetic component to name.

## Mnemonic
Two rows of teeth locking together like a zipper: "牙," a tooth.

## Notes
牙 and 齿 both mean "tooth" but were originally distinct body parts:
molars (牙) versus front teeth (齿). Modern Mandarin blurs the distinction
in everyday speech (牙齿 pairs both), but 牙 alone still leans toward molars
in technical/dental contexts.
