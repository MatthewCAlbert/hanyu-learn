## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 刀 (knife) supplies the meaning, 前 *qián* supplies the sound,
shifted to *jiǎn*. Not in Shuowen; scissors as a two-bladed hand tool are a
later invention than the classical corpus, so the character postdates it. The
structure — a cutting tool named with the knife radical — is uncontested.

## Mnemonic
Two blades 刀 moving forward 前 together, cutting as they go.

## Notes
剪 covers both the object (剪刀 "scissors") and the verb, including the
figurative 剪彩 "to cut the ribbon [at an opening ceremony]" and 剪辑 "to
edit [film/video]" — cutting and splicing footage the way one cuts cloth.
