## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 阝 (from 邑, a settlement — the semantic component
signaling a place name here) with 良 *liáng* supplying the sound.
Shuowen's original definition names an actual place: a small town in the
ancient state of Lu.

From a place name, 郎 shifted to a title for young officials serving at
court, then broadened to "a young man" generally (新郎, "bridegroom," lit.
"new young-man") — a settlement's name lending itself, over time, to the
people associated with it.

## Mnemonic
A young official once named for the good 良 town 阝 he served: "郎," a
young man.

## Notes
新郎 (bridegroom) and 郎中 (an old term for a physician) both keep this
character's "a young man, especially one of a respected role" sense,
long after its original meaning as a place name faded.
