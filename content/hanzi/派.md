## Etymology
Ideographic-and-phonetic: 氵 (water) beside 𠂢, an obsolete character
meaning a branching, diverging flow — 水之衺流別也. Shuowen notes 𠂢 itself
carries the sound (亦聲), so a river dividing into branches is both the
picture and, loosely, the source of the pronunciation.

The app's decomposition data records the right-hand piece as 氏 rather than
𠂢 — the two look alike, and 𠂢 fell out of independent use entirely once
派 took over, so most modern references no longer distinguish them
visually. Confidence is medium, and no phonetic component is declared,
because the character genuinely in the shape today is the obsolete 𠂢, not
氏, and asserting either literally would misstate the case.

## Mnemonic
A river 氵 splitting into branches: a faction, a school of thought.

## Notes
"A branch of a river" → "a branch/faction of people" (党派, 派别) is a
clean, still-visible metaphor: a school of thought is a stream that has
split off from the main current.
