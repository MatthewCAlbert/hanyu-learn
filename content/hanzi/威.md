## Etymology
Shuowen defines 威 as 姑, the husband's mother (the sense survives in 威姑), writing it from 女 and 戌. The "might, awe" sense is a later extension. Modern scholars disagree about the right-hand component (戌 as phonetic? as an axe 戉/戊?), and the popular "armed force dominating a woman" story is folk reading, not attested. Not securely attested.

## Mnemonic
A woman 女 who inspires awe — the mother-in-law, to whom everyone defers.

## Notes
Common in 威胁, 权威, 示威, 威风. Compare 戚 and 咸, which share the axe-like 戊/戌 shape in the same family of graphs.
