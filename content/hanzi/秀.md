## Etymology
Pictophonetic: 禾 (grain) supplies the meaning. Shuowen's entry itself is
unusual — it reads simply "上諱" ("a name to be avoided out of respect for
the reigning emperor"), a Han-dynasty taboo against writing the actual
definition because it matched part of an emperor's name. Later
commentators generally reconstruct the sense as grain putting out its
flowering ear, a healthy, thriving crop — but because Shuowen itself
withholds the definition, this entry keeps confidence at medium.

"A crop flowering beautifully" extended figuratively to human excellence
and refinement (优秀, covered elsewhere in this app; 秀气, "delicate,
refined-looking").

## Mnemonic
Grain 禾 putting out its flowering ear at just the right moment,
outstanding among the rest of the field: "秀," excellent.

## Notes
优秀 (outstanding, covered elsewhere in this app) is 秀's most common
modern compound; the character also appears widely in given names, where
it carries the same "flourishing, excellent" connotation.
