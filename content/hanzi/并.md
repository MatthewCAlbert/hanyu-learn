## Etymology
Ideographic in origin: oracle-bone and bronze forms show two frontal human
figures (大) standing side by side on a ground line, picturing "standing
together, side by side." By the time of Shuowen the lower stroke had broken
in transmission, so the seal form was read as two 立 ("to stand") stacked
side by side — already a reinterpretation of the original picture.

Confidence is low because the modern simplified shape 并 (丷 over 开) is a
further, later graphic simplification. Neither 丷 nor 开 is the "two people"
of the oracle-bone form, and no source traces the intermediate steps
component by component — so the app cannot respec a semantic or phonetic
piece for the character as written today without inventing one.

## Mnemonic
Two chopsticks 丷 laid together, side by side: "together, and."

## Notes
The 竝/並/并/幷 family is a tangle of historically distinct-but-confusable
characters that modern simplification merged toward 并. Treat "two things
standing together" as the inherited idea, not as something you can point to
in today's strokes.
