## Etymology
Pictographic. Shuowen ties 辛 to autumn, harvest, and the pungent
"metal-hard, bitter" taste that brings tears to the eyes — and the graph
itself is generally read as a picture of a knife-like tool once used for
tattooing or branding, an implement of punishment and pain. That
association with pain is very likely the root of "bitter, laborious,
suffering" (辛苦, 辛酸).

## Mnemonic
A branding tool, sharp enough to bring tears — bitterness, endured.

## Notes
辛 is the eighth Heavenly Stem 天干 and, separately, the everyday word for
hardship: 辛苦 "hard work, toil" (also a common polite phrase thanking
someone for their effort), 辛酸 "bitter and sour, i.e. painful [memories]."
