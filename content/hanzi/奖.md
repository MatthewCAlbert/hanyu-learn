## Etymology
The traditional form 獎 was pictophonetic: 犬 (dog) supplied the meaning —
Shuowen's definition is literally "to urge a dog on with a shout," a
hunting or herding command — and 將 *jiāng* supplied the sound. "To urge on"
generalized to "to encourage, to praise" and, as a noun, "an award" given to
encourage further achievement.

**Simplification changed both halves.** 犬 (dog) was replaced by the
unrelated 大 ("big") — losing the semantic component entirely — and 將 was
reduced to a bare fragment (丬 + 夕, missing 將's own 寸), which no longer
functions as a reliable phonetic either. Because neither surviving piece is
a real semantic or phonetic component of the modern character, none is
declared here.

## Mnemonic
Something big 大 and impressive handed over with a flourish: an "奖,"
a prize.

## Notes
奖 shows the same pattern as 权 and 宝: a modern simplified character whose
components are shape-reductions of the traditional form's real semantic
and phonetic parts, not new meaningful pieces in their own right.
