## Etymology
Pictophonetic (Shuowen also reads it as a double-duty ideographic-phonetic):
酉 (wine jar — the component in 酒, 酿) supplies the meaning, 卒 *zú* supplies
the sound, shifted to *zuì*. Shuowen glosses it 卒也, and further explains
醉 as reaching one's own limit/measure with drink — "having had exactly
enough" — without going so far as chaos. The modern sense "intoxicated" is
continuous with that reading.

## Mnemonic
A wine jar 酉 finished off 卒 to the very last drop — drunk.

## Notes
酉 across 酒 (wine), 酿 (to brew), 配 (to blend/pair) marks the whole
fermentation/wine family. 醉 extends figuratively to any overwhelming
pleasure: 陶醉 "enraptured," 醉心于 "to be infatuated with."
