## Etymology
Pictophonetic, traditional 壽: rooted in 老 (old age), with a reduced
phonetic component supplying the sound. Shuowen's gloss 久也 is "lasting a
long time," matching "longevity" directly. The simplified 寿 preserves only
a graphic trace of the original 老-based structure.

## Mnemonic
A life stretched out long, old 老 age reached and celebrated.

## Notes
寿 anchors 长寿 "longevity," 寿命 "lifespan," and the everyday birthday
greeting 祝你寿比南山 "may your years be as long as the southern
mountains" — a set traditional wish for the elderly.
