## Etymology
Ideographic, but the two forms tell different stories. Traditional 辭
combines 𤔞 (a picture of tangled threads being sorted out, i.e.
"to manage, to put in order") with 辛 (a tool/hardship marker) — speech
sorted and set in order. The simplified 辞 instead pairs 舌 (tongue, spoken
words) with 辛 — a reading that lands on "sharp/bitter words," a plausible
but different picture from the traditional analysis, so treat the "bitter
words" image as describing the modern shape, not as Shuowen's own account.

## Mnemonic
Words from the tongue 舌 that sting like something bitter 辛 — a resignation
speech.

## Notes
辞 covers formal leaving (辞职 "to resign," 辞别 "to bid farewell") and
literary form (歌辞/歌词 "song lyrics," 辞典 as an old variant of 词典
"dictionary").
