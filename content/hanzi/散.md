## Etymology
Shuowen, working from Qin-dynasty script, reads 散 through the "meat"
radical and glosses it as 雜肉, "minced, scattered meat." Modern
scholarship considers this very likely a misreading: the earlier
components were probably 㪔 (small dots scattered off a piece of wood,
already meaning "to scatter, to disperse") merged with an unrelated
wine-vessel character 斝, with 月 "moon" — not 肉 "meat" — completing the
Qin-script shape that Shuowen had in front of him.

Confidence is low because the two accounts genuinely conflict, and the
"meat" reading, while it is what Shuowen himself wrote down, is generally
thought to be built on an already-corrupted source shape.

## Mnemonic
Wood shavings 攵, scattered every which way 月: dispersed, undone.

## Notes
散 splits by tone into *sǎn* "loose, scattered" (adjective: 散文,
零散) and *sàn* "to disperse, to break up" (verb: 解散, 散步) — the same
underlying image of things coming apart, read as a state versus an event.
