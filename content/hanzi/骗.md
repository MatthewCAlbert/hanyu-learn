## Etymology
Pictophonetic, traditional 騙: 马 (horse) supplies the meaning, 扁 *biǎn*
supplies the sound, shifted to *piàn*. 騙 postdates Shuowen; the earliest
recorded sense in later dictionaries is "to vault onto a horse by swinging
one leg over" — a flat, swinging motion, consistent with 扁 "flat." "To
deceive, to swindle" is a later, unrelated extension whose exact bridge from
"mount a horse" is not clearly documented, so treat that jump as attested by
usage rather than by a traceable semantic chain.

## Mnemonic
Swinging a leg flat 扁 over a horse 马 — and, in the modern sense, sweeping
someone off their feet with a lie.

## Notes
骗 is the everyday verb for "to con, to cheat" (骗人, 骗钱, 骗子 "swindler"),
distinct from 撒谎 "to tell a lie," which covers any falsehood, not only
ones told for gain.
