## Etymology
Shuowen actually records two separate characters here. 極 (semantic 木
"wood," phonetic 亟) meant "roof ridge-beam" and generalised to "the
utmost, the extreme point" — the sense everyone wants. 极, a distinct,
much rarer character (semantic 木, phonetic 及) meant a wooden pack-frame
on a donkey's back.

Modern simplification reused the small, ancient 极 to write every sense of
極. Confidence is medium because, purely by lucky coincidence, that
"reused" spelling turns out to be genuinely, anciently pictophonetic —
just for the wrong word. The 木/及 split declared here is real and
Shuowen-attested; it simply is not the etymology of "extreme, pole."

## Mnemonic
Wood 木 reaching 及 as high as it possibly can: the utmost extreme.

## Notes
极 joins 极 (this entry), 胜 and 证 later in this same batch as cases where
simplification solved "too many strokes" by resurrecting an old, unrelated
character that happened to sound right and already existed on the books.
