## Etymology
Pictophonetic. 田 (a field) supplies the meaning; 介 *jiè* supplies the
sound — and, as Shuowen notes, 介 itself already means "to lie between,"
which fits neatly: a boundary is exactly the line that lies between two
fields. Shuowen's gloss, 竟也, is "an end point, a limit."

## Mnemonic
The line lying between 介 one field 田 and the next: a boundary.

## Notes
"The boundary between fields" generalised into any boundary at all
(边界, 交界) and, by a further step, into "a defined realm or sphere" —
世界 "the world," 商界 "the business world" — a bounded field of people
or activity rather than of crops.
